# Show notes style

Show notes (`episodes/NNN-game-name.md`) are what the host reads from while recording. They are the host's own talking points, not research findings or someone else's opinion for the host to adapt.

## Voice

- Write each line so the host can say it as written. No "we found", "worth checking", "unverified", "sources disagree" or notes to the host about what to confirm. If a fact is not solid, leave it out and raise it with whoever asked for the notes.
- Concrete facts, dates, names and numbers. Short source tags in parentheses are fine, used sparingly.
- No em dashes. Be honest about a game's weak points.

## Structure

Open with one line of back-of-box copy (the hook the host opens the episode with), then:

1. **Setup & Premise**: what the game is based on (if applicable); the developers and publishers; the pitch.
2. **What Makes It Stand Out**: the unique mechanics and features. At least four, and more if there are more.
3. **Gameplay Structure**: the core loop; the basic controls.
4. **Unique Gameplay Elements**: specific moments that are notable, weird or much discussed. Different from section 2, which is about what the game offers in general.
5. **History and Development**: what is actually known about how it was made. Not a repeat of the setup.
6. **Music and Presentation**: the graphics (what is clear or unclear, what it looks like) and the music.
7. **Pop Culture**: the themes and motifs around the game and what else was going on in the period it came out. A baseball game gets the big baseball stories of the time; a movie game gets what else that franchise did; a space shooter gets the space and alien movies and news of the day.
8. **Sequels, Spinoffs and Comparison Games**: every sequel and spinoff, each with a sub-bullet on what it added or improved; then at least four modern games it can be compared to. Give each comparison real detail, as three or four sub-bullets: what the game is (developer, year, platform), what it has in common with this game and why that makes it a fair comparison, how the two differ, and a specific moment or mechanic the host can point to.
9. **Essential Games List**: a real case for and a real case against. Do not repeat lines from earlier sections.
