# 346 - Wizardry: Proving Grounds of the Mad Overlord

- May luck and good fortune be your companions
- Wizardry is a pioneering RPG created by Robert Woodhead and Andrew C. Greenberg, released in the early 1980s.Initially available on the Apple II, Wizardry stood out for its wire-frame, 3D graphics and static monster images, innovations that made it more visually engaging than other mostly text-based games of the time.
- The game’s story revolves around the evil wizard Werdna, who steals a powerful amulet. The overlord Trebor devises a dungeon to test adventurers' strength, challenging them to defeat Werdna and retrieve the amulet.
- No quality of life improvements for the NES version. The beginning of the game doesn’t even really tell you about forming a party
- What is the Gameplay Loop?
- Wizardry is a turn-based, first-person dungeon crawler, where players control a party of adventurers exploring a multi-level dungeon.
- The gameplay loop revolves around assembling and managing a party, navigating mazes, fighting monsters, and collecting treasure.
- Progression involves delving deeper into the dungeon, defeating enemies in random encounters, leveling up, and solving puzzles to advance.
- The ultimate goal is to defeat the evil wizard Werdna at the bottom of the dungeon, but players must balance exploration with returning to the town to heal, restock supplies, and save progress.
- Players can create and customize characters with different races, classes, and alignments, allowing for varied playstyles.
- Alignments:
- Classes have restrictions on which alignments they can hold.
- Party alignment influences gameplay, especially interactions with friendly NPCs.
- Good and evil characters can’t be in the same party; neutral characters can travel with anyone.
- Races and Starting Stats:
- Five races: Humans, Elves, Dwarfs, Gnomes, Hobbits, each with unique stats.
- Gnomes and Hobbits excel in specific classes like Thieves, while Humans are balanced but slightly weaker.
- Strength: Your strength enhances the amount of damage that your weapon does to an enemy.
- I.Q.: The higher your I.Q., the better you will be at casting mage spells, as well as avoiding the effects of mage spells that are cast on you.
- Piety: The more Pious you are, the more likely the gods will grant the prayers of clerics, and the more likely the gods will spare you from the effects of prayers against you.
- Vitality: Vitality affects how much damage you can sustain before dying. The better your vitality, the more likely it will be that you can be brought back from the dead.
- Agility: Higher agility makes you faster and harder to hit, giving you a better chance to dodge attacks or avoid traps.
- Luck: Your luck can influence any of the five other attributes in various ways over the course of your adventure. While it's not something that can be relied upon, those with higher luck generally do better throughout the game.
- Classes:
- Variety of classes including Fighters, Mages, Clerics, and hybrids like Samurai and Lords.
- Advanced classes like Ninja require very high stats and can’t be accessed at the start.
- Multi-classing allows for class changes but comes with penalties, such as reduced stats and increased age.
- Clerical and Mage Spells:
- Spells are divided into levels, with Cleric spells focusing on healing, protection, and reviving.
- Mage spells are more offensive, including fire, ice, and teleportation abilities.
- Certain powerful spells can result in different random effects, like summoning divine help.
- Cleric Spells
- Dios (Heal) - Level 1
  - Effect: Restores 1-8 HP to a single target.
  - Why it's useful: Essential for keeping your party members alive during and after battles without relying on limited healing items.
- Milwa (Light) - Level 1
  - Effect: A bright light reveals hidden doors and lets you see further.
  - Why it's useful: Helps in dungeon navigation, especially since there's no in-game map. It uncovers secret passages and extends your visibility.
- Calfo (X-Ray) - Level 2
  - Effect: Lets you safely determine a trap with 95% accuracy.
  - Why it's useful: Prevents disastrous outcomes from opening trapped chests, safeguarding your party from harm.
- Manifo (Statue) - Level 2
  - Effect: Paralyzes a group of monsters.
  - Why it's useful: Immobilizes enemies, making them easier to hit and reducing incoming damage. Paralyzed enemies take double damage from physical attacks.
- Bamatu (Prayer) - Level 3
  - Effect: Reduces party's AC (Armor Class) by 4 points.
  - Why it's useful: Enhances your party's defense, making them harder to hit.
- Mage Spells
- Halito (Little Fire) - Level 1
  - Effect: Inflicts 1-8 fire damage to a single target.
  - Why it's useful: Provides an offensive spell to eliminate weaker enemies quickly.
- Katino (Bad Air) - Level 1
  - Effect: Puts a group of animal or humanoid monsters to sleep.
  - Why it's useful: Disables multiple enemies at once, reducing the number of attacks your party takes. Sleeping enemies are easier to hit and take double damage from physical attacks.
- Dumapic (Clarity) - Level 1
  - Effect: Tells you where the stairs are and what direction you're facing.
  - Why it's useful: Assists with dungeon navigation, crucial in a game without an in-game map.
- Mogref (Body Iron) - Level 1
  - Effect: Reduces the caster's AC by 2.
  - Why it's useful: Provides the Mage with a bit more survivability in combat.
- Mahalito (Big Fire) - Level 3
  - Effect: Inflicts 4-24 fire damage to a group of enemies.
  - Why it's useful: A powerful area-of-effect spell that can turn the tide in battles against multiple foes.
- What Are Some Other Examples of Games Like Wizardry That Released in 1990?
- Might and Magic: Secret of the Inner Sanctum (NES, 1990): Another first-person RPG with a similar focus on dungeon crawling and party management, but with a larger open-world structure.
- Swords and Serpents (NES, 1990): A similar dungeon-crawling experience with first-person exploration and turn-based combat, though it’s more simplified compared to Wizardry.
- Final Fantasy (NES, 1990 in the US): Although it uses a top-down view instead of first-person, it’s a turn-based RPG that also focuses on party management, character progression, and dungeon exploration.
- Ultima: Exodus (NES, 1989): A predecessor to modern RPGs, offering a party-based experience with world exploration, turn-based combat, and dungeon crawling elements.
- What Makes It Unique/Different From Other NES RPG Games?
- The game has a high level of difficulty and complexity, requiring players to manage every aspect of their party, including food, spells, and inventory.
- It introduced a permanent death mechanic, adding tension and higher stakes to dungeon exploration since losing a party member could be devastating.
- Unlike most NES RPGs, Wizardry has minimal story elements beyond the dungeon-crawling experience, focusing heavily on gameplay mechanics and player-driven exploration.
- The party creation and customization system was far more detailed than most NES games, offering a wide range of character options.
- What Kind of Quality of Life Improvements Could Wizardry Have Added?
- Save points within the dungeon: In the NES version, players must return to the town to save progress. Adding save points in the dungeon itself would reduce the frustration of losing progress after lengthy exploration.
- Improved interface and menus: Navigating the game’s menus is clunky and slow, making party management and inventory handling cumbersome. Streamlining these systems would improve the flow.
- Better mapping system: The game doesn’t provide an in-game map, requiring players to manually track their position on graph paper. An automatic map could have made navigation more user-friendly.
- Balancing the difficulty curve: The early game can feel punishing, especially for new players. Smoothing out the difficulty ramp could make it more accessible without losing the challenge.
- Clearer guidance and tutorials: The game’s mechanics and objectives aren’t always clear, and a more in-depth tutorial or hints could help ease players into the complex systems.
- Auto-battle or faster combat: The turn-based combat system can be slow, especially when facing weaker enemies. Adding an auto-battle feature or speeding up combat would reduce downtime.
- General Tips
- Prioritize Healing and Protection: For Clerics, Dios and Bamatu are invaluable for keeping your party healthy and reducing damage taken.
- Crowd Control is Key: Mage spells like Katino and Cleric spells like Manifo help manage groups of enemies by disabling them.
- Enhance Navigation: Use Milwa and Dumapic early to aid in mapping the dungeon and avoiding getting lost.
- Trap Detection: Calfo is essential for safely opening treasure chests, a primary source of loot and gold.
- Offensive Spells: Don't underestimate the damage output of spells like Halito and Mahalito, which can quickly eliminate threats.
- Party Composition Tips:
- Traditional setup: 3 front-line fighters and 3 back-line casters.
- Suggested transitions, like moving a Thief to Ninja and using Fighters-turned-Samurai.
- Gameplay and Mechanics:
- Players need to map the game manually (no in-game map).
- You have the option of going through a maze that is filled in or just line-vector graphics (like the Apple or PC)
- the dungeon layouts are different for the NES version compared to all other versions
- Deceptive Dungeon Design and the Novice Experience:
- Wizardry’s dungeon is designed to mislead players with traps and difficult monsters. Veteran players can skillfully avoid many of these unnecessary challenges, while novices are more likely to stumble into these dangers, facing numerous hardships and setbacks.
- Item Identification: Always identify items before equipping them. Cursed items exist, and equipping one without knowing can cause issues. Using a Bishop’s identification ability can help here
- Trap Disarming: Rogue characters are essential for disarming traps on treasure chests. Pair this with a priest casting "CALFO" to check the trap type. If the results differ between the two, consider leaving the chest unopened
- Cheats:
- Pooling Gold: At the start, gold is limited, but you can get a boost by creating "dummy" characters. Pool their gold into one main character, then delete the extras and repeat the process. This allows you to afford better gear early on
