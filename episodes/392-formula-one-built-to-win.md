# 392 - Formula One: Built to Win

- Introduction
- What it is: A 1990 NES-exclusive racer by Seta that blends arcade racing with career progression, car upgrades, and a full Formula One license in its final tier.
- Why it matters: It’s one of the first racing games on console with a Gran Turismo-style structure, offering car purchasing, upgrading, and license testing nearly a decade before GT1.
- General Overview
- Release Info
  - Released in 1990 (North America ONLY), developed and published by Seta Corporation.
- Loop in one sentence: Start with a clunky Mini Cooper, grind regional races across the U.S. for cash, upgrade or buy faster cars, pass license tests to unlock new race tiers, then tackle a 16‑race international Formula One season.

- Economy, Shops & Side Activities
- Parts Shops: New York, Miami, Yellowstone, Denver, LA, Hawaii – buy upgrades & refill nitro.
- Speed Shops: Detroit, San Francisco – sell spare parts to recoup cash.
- Car Dealership: Dallas – purchase Vector/F40; Mini is default, F1 is earned.
- Casino (Las Vegas): Slot machines with variable denomination (1/10/1000); cherries pay, 777 jackpot nets 1 000× coins. Ideal (or dangerous) way to bankroll upgrades. (gamefaqs.gamespot.com)
- Gameplay Mechanics (Deep Dive)
- Controls: A = Gas, B = Brake, Up = Nitro, Select = Retire mid‑race. (gamefaqs.gamespot.com)
- Nitro Management: Limited tank, refilled at shop; use on clear straights after lining up passes.
- Traffic Types: Green civilian cars impede; blue racers are position rivals—both block aggressively.
- Passing Logic: Center the lane, bait the block, dart inside; boost once mid‑pass to seal it. (gamefaqs.gamespot.com)
- Cornering Model: Sharp turns demand brake‑coast‑exit rhythm; overshoot = grass @ ≤ 60 mph & spin risk.
- Licenses: Short time‑trial tests gate each rank; pass speed & handling trials to unlock next tier races.
- Free Mode: Time‑attack any unlocked car/track (single‑lap, full nitro, no AI). Saves best laps to battery. (en.wikipedia.org)
- Tracks Snapshot
- U.S. Circuit Cities: NY • Detroit • Miami • Yellowstone • Dallas • Las Vegas • Denver • SF • LA • Hawaii.
- International GP Roster (16 rounds): Brazil → San Marino → Monaco → Mexico → USA (Phoenix) → Canada → France → Britain → Germany → Hungary → Belgium → Italy → Portugal → Spain → Japan → Australia. (gamefaqs.gamespot.com)
- Graphics & Sound
- Clean pseudo‑3D scaling similar to Rad Racer; sense of speed jumps dramatically with engine upgrades.
- Distinct engine pitches per car; menu/garage themes provide mid‑race breathers.
- Difficulty & Mastery Angles
- Money Grind: Rank‑up requirement forces full sweep of each city—smart part purchases can shortcut grind.
- Tire Strategy: A‑Grip tires + active suspension are almost mandatory for Rank A circuits but replace each race.
- Nitro Discipline: Hoard for overtake bursts; refill cost is trivial early but significant during F1 season.
- Million‑Dollar Rule: FAQ recommends $1 M bankroll before entering F1 so you can fully upgrade and still afford nitro/tires. (gamefaqs.gamespot.com)
- Unique & Interesting Features
- Battery Backup: Saves garage inventory, bankroll, lap records—uncommon for 1990 racers.
- Pseudo‑Licensed Grid: Endgame rivals have spoofed names (e.g., “S. Nakazma”) resembling 1990‑91 F1 drivers.
- Casino Economy Exploit: Savvy (or save‑state) gamblers can bankroll a Turbine engine long before Rank B.
