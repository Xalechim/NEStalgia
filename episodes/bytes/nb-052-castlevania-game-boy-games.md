# NB 052 - Castlevania Game Boy Games


## I. Setup & Premise

- Between 1989 and 1997, Konami released three mainline Castlevania games on the original Game Boy:
  - Castlevania: The Adventure
  - Castlevania II: Belmont's Revenge
  - Castlevania Legends
- The trilogy is wildly uneven:
  - Game 1: widely considered one of the franchise’s worst entries
  - Game 2: a hidden gem and major improvement
  - Game 3: later removed from official canon by series producer Koji Igarashi
- Original Game Boy hardware limitations heavily shaped development:
  - blurry screen during motion
  - weak processor
  - sprite flickering
  - few examples of successful action games existed yet
- Stars:
  - Christopher Belmont in the first two games
    - Prior to his appearance in the series, Christopher was mentioned in the Japanese instruction booklet for the original Castlevania.
  - Sonia Belmont in the third
    - planned to return in her second starring role in the canceled Dreamcast game Castlevania: Resurrection.

## II. Game One: Castlevania: The Adventure (1989)

- Premise
- Stars Christopher Belmont, ancestor of Simon Belmont.
- Set 100 years before the original NES Castlevania.
- Released in December 1989 as one of the earliest non-Nintendo Game Boy releases.
- Features:
  - only four stages
  - no sub-weapons
  - extremely slow movement
- Major Problems
- Controls
- Christopher moves painfully slowly.
- Jumps cannot be adjusted mid-air. It’s Castlevania 1 rules, only the jump feels even stiffer. Designed so you have to be one foot off the ledge to even make the next platform
- The game is just whipping
- If you try to whip after turning it takes extra time
- Whip Degradation System
- Taking damage downgrades your whip strength.
- Progression:
  - fireball chain whip
  - chain whip
  - leather whip
- Because enemies constantly deal contact damage, players spend most of the game underpowered.
- The mechanic mostly exists to artificially increase difficulty and playtime.
- Stage 3
- Features:
  - spike traps
  - collapsing platforms
  - a pursuing wall chase sequence
- Hardware struggles to keep up with the action.
- I actually thought this stage was cool before the spikes started rising from below. The timing was too difficult after that and I lose all my lives
- Frequently cited as one of the worst Castlevania stages ever made.
- What Holds Up
- Soundtrack
- The music is the game’s strongest element.
- Tracks successfully capture Castlevania atmosphere despite Game Boy limitations.
- Critics consistently praised the soundtrack even while criticizing the gameplay.
- Interesting Development Footnote
- Programmer Masato Maegawa worked on the game.
- He later co-founded Treasure, creators of:
  - Gunstar Heroes
  - Ikaruga
  - Radiant Silvergun
- Maegawa himself later admitted The Adventure was not good.
- His frustrations with Konami’s sequel-driven culture eventually pushed him to leave. Which is really true because Treasure doesn’t really do sequels

## III. Game Two: Castlevania II: Belmont's Revenge (1991)

- Revenge against who? The player?
- Premise
- Set 15 years after the first game.
- Christopher Belmont’s son Soleiyu is corrupted by Dracula.
- Dracula resurrects four themed castles before the final confrontation.
- Its kinda funny that the first level starts with this knee high water area that you feel may kill you if you jump in it
- Major Improvements
- Controls
- Nearly every criticism from the first game was addressed:
  - faster movement
    - You can slide down ropes
  - responsive whip attacks
  - smoother gameplay
  - less slowdown
  - ability to attack while climbing ropes
- Most importantly:
  - taking damage no longer downgrades the whip under normal conditions
- Stage Select Structure
- Four themed castles can be tackled in any order:
  - Crystal
  - Cloud
  - Plant
  - Rock
- Similar to Mega Man structure
- Does each castle have distinct mechanics and/or visual identity?
- Sub-Weapons Return
- Holy Water and Axe
- Soundtrack
- Frequently considered one of the best soundtracks on the original Game Boy.
- Songs like:
  - “Original Sin”
  - “Praying Hands”
are especially praised.
- Final confrontation with Soleiyu feels mechanically underdeveloped.

## IV. Game Three: Castlevania Legends (1997)

- Premise
- Released very late in the Game Boy’s lifespan.
- Stars Sonia Belmont, presented as:
  - the first Belmont
  - ancestor of the entire clan
  - What’s a GIRL doing on the GAME BOY
    - Game also starts with sperm creatures flying at you to a Game Boy rendition of Bloody Tears
- Set in 1450 during Dracula’s earliest rise.
- Development Context
- Directed by Kouki Yamashita, who also directed:
  - Castlevania: Dracula X which is essentially a really bad version of my favorite Castlevania, Rondo of Blood
- The Soul powers. Acquired after the first stage. Change the game a lot.
- Stages are now too long. If you’re taking me to new rooms and its the same enemies in the same kind of room, piss off. Change up the platforming or the enemies or move on.
- Burning Ability
- Sonia can unleash a powerful screen-clearing attack using magic.
- Useful, but it trivializes combat and bosses.
- Resource-management system lacks meaningful depth.
- Difficulty
- Considered too easy.
- Bosses lack complexity.
- Trap rooms offer little reward or challenge.
- Ending
- Sonia meets Alucard and, in the best ending, is implied to carry his child.
- This would make the Belmont bloodline descended from Dracula’s son.
- That lore was later erased.
- The Canon Erasure
- Official Explanation
- Koji Igarashi removed Legends from continuity because:
  - Castlevania: Lament of Innocence established Leon Belmont as the first vampire hunter
  - Sonia’s timeline no longer fit canon
- Less Official Context
- Igarashi publicly called Legends an embarrassment.
- He excluded it from many compilations and rereleases.
- It did not receive an official rerelease until Nintendo Switch Online in 2023.
- Sonia Belmont’s Return
- Sonia later appeared in:
  - Vampire Survivors DLC
- She also appears connected to:
  - Castlevania: Belmont's Curse
- However, she has been reimagined:
  - now portrayed as Trevor and Sypha’s daughter rather than the clan’s originator
- Sonia was not fully restored to canon, only recontextualized.

## V. Legacy & Connections

- The Trilogy as a Case Study
- The trilogy demonstrates:
- how difficult early handheld development was
- how much improvement feedback can create
- how quickly a franchise can decline when treated as secondary IP
- Timing Hurt Legends
- By 1997:
- the Game Boy was aging
- Game Boy Color was near release
- Pokémon Red and Blue had already transformed expectations for the platform
- Legends felt outdated immediately upon release.
- The Masato Maegawa Connection
- The weakest game in the trilogy indirectly led to the formation of Treasure.
- Maegawa’s frustrations with Konami:
  - lack of creative freedom
  - sequel-focused culture
directly mirror many issues visible across the trilogy.
- Castlevania: The Adventure ReBirth (2009)
- Castlevania: The Adventure ReBirth was released exclusively for WiiWare in 2009 as part of Konami’s short-lived “ReBirth” line.
  - Other ReBirth games included:
    - Contra ReBirth
    - Gradius ReBirth
- Developed by M2, a studio highly respected for retro restorations and emulation work.
- Functions as a loose remake/reimagining of:
  - Castlevania: The Adventure
- Rather than faithfully recreating the Game Boy original, ReBirth essentially asks:
  - “What if The Adventure had actually been good?”
- Feels like a completely different game
- Flame Whip is now a pickup that runs on a timer
- Subweapons return
- Very Arcadey feel
- Back to Adventure
- There is a speed romhack
- a patch for the color version of the game contained in the Konami GB Collection Vol. 1.
- Increased character speed
- No whip downgrade when being hit by an enemy
- Tease next episode.
