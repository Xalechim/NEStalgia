# NB 044 - The Magic Treasure of Cleopatra

- Introduction
- Hosts: Mike (keeps the train on time), Joe (excited contrarian), Sean (cynical historian).
- What it is: 1987 Square adventure‑RPG hybrid for the Famicom Disk System (JP title: Cleopatra no Mahou). First‑person exploration with menu‑driven commands, turn‑based battles, and puzzle rooms set in mythic‑Egypt locales.
- Why it matters: A pre‑FF Square experiment—Hiromichi Tanaka directing, Nobuo Uematsu on music—that mixes RPG progression with adventure‑game logic and cinematic FDS animations.
- General Overview
- Release & Lineage
  - Japan‑only, July 24, 1987; developed/published by Square for FDS.
  - Square’s last RPG before Final Fantasy; sometimes subtitled in English as “The Magic/Cursed Treasure of Cleopatra.”
- Elevator Pitch
  - You’re Daisuke Kusano, teen son of a missing archaeologist. Search Egypt for the Tears of Isis to rescue your father; demons and deceptive NPCs block your path.
- Story & Setting (Spoiler‑lite beats for on‑air narration)
- Hook: Dad disappears hunting the Tears of Isis; demons appear at the dig. A mysterious shopkeeper offers help.
- Midgame: Trek through the Great Sphinx, Temple of Kom Ombo, and the Tower of Sand; unravel rites using ankhs, feathers, and ritual items.
- Endgame: Unmask the demon behind the kindly façade; summon Cleopatra’s spirit with the final Tear to close the loop.
- Gameplay Mechanics (Deep Dive)
- Hybrid Structure
  - First‑person field: Navigate towns/ruins one step at a time; random encounters can trigger even while checking menus.
  - Turn‑based battles: Options to fight / item / flee; leveling increases stamina; weapons/armor matter against late‑game mini‑bosses.
- Command‑Menu Adventure
  - Contextual verbs expand per scene (e.g., Proceed / Look / Inventory / Move / Status / Save/Load; plus situational Take / Use / Insert / Place / Pray / Read).
  - Puzzle rooms require object verbs and precise targets (layered walls, statues, scales, hidden passages).
- Economy & Shops
  - Buy Knife early (100G), then maps/rope from second‑hand shop; some shopkeepers refuse to trade until defeated.
  - Inns and heal rooms offset the game’s sharp encounter spikes.
- Progression Pivots (set‑piece items)
  - Rope for pits; Ankhs of Ma’at & Sobek to unlock Sphinx chambers; Anubis’ Sword as major power spike; Rainbow Decoration / Golden Arrow / Silver Feather for temple rites; end‑game artifacts like Cane of Amun / Sun Mirror / Wind Flute enable boss solutions.
- Notable Areas & Set Pieces (for vivid on‑air description)
- NOT an exciting start. Can’t fight, then you can only fight. It becomes a gold/hp management game while you watch XP go up
  - Easy grind once you’ve gotten accustomed to the system. Stay in front of inn and keep doing look then start to cancel to trigger enemy encounters
- Cruel enemy encounter system. Can happen while saving!
- Sphinx Interior: Hidden passages revealed by turning a millstone; tons of guess work, vertical climb with punishing Naga guardians.
- Goddess Temple: Ritual altar puzzle—place offerings in correct hands, pray to open new ways.
- Catacombs/Labyrinth: Torch‑lit navigation; poisonous pools and one‑way doors; healing room vs. trap room mind‑games.
- Boss Rooms: Optional Sphinx fights drop unique artifacts; late‑game enemies can one‑shot under‑leveled players.
- Karado’s Messenger who you’ve been searching for has been reborn as Cleopatra and intends to reign as queen of Egypt forever. Use the sun mirror to reveal her true form which is Karado. A skeleton. After you defeat Karado he taunts you that even if you’ve killed him you can’t bring back your dad. You then get the item (Isis Necklace) that will bring back your dad in the next room.
- Graphics & Sound
- Visuals: Cute but expressive character art with FDS‑era full‑screen animation cels (short but eye‑catching); Egyptian iconography (ankhs, scarabs, hieroglyphs) used as puzzle signposts.
- Audio: Early Nobuo Uematsu score—short, looping motifs that sell mystery and danger; distinct battle stings and shop jingles.
- Difficulty & Mastery (On‑air tips)
- Prepare for spikes: Grind a level before tackling Grass/Thunder/Fire Nagas; buy armor before climbing.
- Verb discipline: The right verb matters—READ before INSERT, PLACE before PRAY; keep a quick list of kana command terms handy.
- Route knowledge: Healing vs. poison rooms sit close together; memorize which doorway leads where.
- One‑off items: Some artifacts are single‑use; don’t waste them testing—save before experiments (FDS write time permitting).
- Beyond the Game (Culture & Context)
- Square before FF: Sits alongside other pre‑FF experiments where Square tested stories/interfaces on FDS.
- FDS Snapshot: Disk‑based saves, longer cut‑ins, and the late‑’80s Japan push for adventure‑RPG hybrids—a design space between Deep Dungeon crawls and menu adventures.
- Egyptomania Angle: 1980s JP media fascination with pyramids/Sphinx imagery; easy hook to talk archaeology myths vs. mechanics (e.g., ankhs as keys).
- Versions & Availability
- Platform: Famicom Disk System only; Japan‑exclusive.
- Fan Access: Community English patches and scanned manuals exist; menus remain terse even in translation.
- Legacy & Reception
- Then: Modest Famitsu score; praised animations/charms; criticized length.
- Now: Cult curiosity as “Square’s last RPG before FF” and an early Uematsu credit; appreciated for puzzle flavor more than depth.
