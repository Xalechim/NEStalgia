# NB 055 - Moon Crystal


## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch: Prince of Persia animation dropped into a normal Famicom action game
  - You play Ricky Slater, a green-haired kid whose father, a scientist, is abducted along with everyone else in Cirrus Village.
  - The kidnapper is Count Crimson, who wants to use the Moon Crystal to raise the dead and rule the world.
  - Seven stages, each ending in a boss.
- Developed and self-published by Hect, August 28, 1992
  - Hect, sometimes written Hector, was a low-profile Japanese outfit better known for puzzle games than for action.
- The timing is the whole story
  - August 1992 is very late for a Famicom release. The Super Famicom was almost two years old by then.
  - It got a limited run, sold badly, and has never been re-released anywhere, including Virtual Console.

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- The animation is the reason anyone still talks about this game
  - Most Famicom games gave a character one jump frame and one duck frame. Ricky gets several of each.
  - He skids when he changes direction.
  - He can grab the lip of any platform and pull himself up, which is rare on the system and doubles as a save from a badly judged jump.
- The visuals go beyond the character
  - Multi-plane scrolling in places.
- It gives you a double jump on an 8-bit console
  - Wing Boots grant it, and you lose it when you die or finish the stage.
  - The level design is built around having it, then quietly built around losing it.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Seven stages
- Controls, kept short
  - B attacks, A jumps, and pressing A again on the way up gives you the double jump if you have the boots.
  - Down crouches. Up climbs when you are hanging off a ledge.
  - Select does nothing at all.
- What the run actually looks like
  - No score. No timer, except in the final stage.
  - Three lives to start, health shown as hearts, up to a maximum of five.
  - Unlimited continues, which matters a lot given how the bosses go.

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- The ledge grab changes how you read a screen
  - You can walk off an edge on purpose and catch the thing below you.
  - Several sections require dropping into a hang rather than landing on top.
- Is the extra animation a problem?
- He is armed with almost nothing
  - A very small knife. One power-up extends the range slightly. That is the entire weapon progression for the whole game.
  - Fine in the stages, where the enemies are sparse and reasonably smart and avoiding them is often the answer.
  - Ruinous in the boss fights, where you have to stand inside their reach to land a hit and there is no room to dodge.
- Bosses are mostly a race with your own health bar
  - The standard solution is to arrive with all five hearts, walk up, and stab until one of you falls over.
  - There is supposedly extra damage for hitting an enemy in the head, but landing it reliably is another matter.
- The final stage is not a fight at all
  - The lab collapses and you get 80 seconds and three hearts to get out.
  - No enemies, no chests, just spikes and pits.
  - Die or run out of time and you restart the escape from the top.

## V. Story, Script, and Presentation

- The script is doing more than a 1992 action game needed to
  - It opens on a folk-tale warning about not going out under a full moon.
  - Ricky's friend Merle sets it up as a village legend. Then the abductors arrive at his house that night.
  - The story runs through Ninja Gaiden-style cutscenes between stages.
- There is a real twist buried in it
  - Rosina, the girl who appears in the cutscenes but is never playable, is a living clone of Count Crimson's daughter, kept alive by the Moon Crystal.
  - It recontextualizes the villain in a way NES-era games almost never bothered with.

## VII. No Sequel, No US Release, and Comparison Games

- America came within an inch of getting it
  - DTMC was lined up to publish it in North America.
  - It was shown at CES and previewed in Electronic Gaming Monthly and Game Informer in mid-1992.
  - There was a full-page ad in GamePro, June 1992. Kids read an ad for a cartridge that would never exist.
- How it landed
  - Famitsu gave it 24 out of 40 and singled out the animation.
