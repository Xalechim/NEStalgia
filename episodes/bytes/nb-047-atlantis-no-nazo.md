# NB 047 - Atlantis no Nazo

- Introduction
- What it is: A 1986 Sunsoft side-scrolling platformer about an amateur adventurer (Wynn) bombing his way through a maze of 101 “Zones” to rescue his master on Atlantis. Infinite bombs, multi-path warps, one-hit deaths. (Wikipedia)
- Why it matters: An early “beat Mario by going bigger” experiment—bi-directional scrolling, a huge non-linear zone graph, and notorious hidden warps like “Black Hole” traps that instantly end your run. (Wikipedia, StrategyWiki overview, HardcoreGaming101)
- Discuss now: Does sheer scope (101 zones) make up for rough physics and one-hit punishments?
- Release, Credits, & Context
- Release: April 17, 1986 (JP, Famicom); never released in the West. Later appeared in Sunsoft compilations on Windows/PS (JP-only). (Wikipedia, NES list date line)
- Developer/Publisher: Sunsoft; music by Naoki Kodaka (later of Batman/Sunsoft fame). (Wikipedia)
- Localization that never was: Activision worked on a Western rebrand as Super Pitfall II before canceling it. (Wikipedia)

- Position in Sunsoft’s arc: A pre-“Sunsoft renaissance” title—interesting ideas, rough feel, and the DNA of later trickier Sunsoft platformers. (HardcoreGaming101)
- Discuss now: If this had launched as “Super Pitfall II,” would its reputation be different?
- Core Gameplay Loop (bombs + warps + timers)
- Loop: Enter a Zone → find an exit door (sometimes hidden) → warp to another Zone (not numerical order) → repeat until the final Zone(s). Each stage has a countdown timer (999→0 → OUT = death). (Wikipedia)
- Bomb-centric play: You have infinite bombs; one active bomb at a time; ~2-second fuse; explosions kill enemies and you. Bomb arcs can hit enemies mid-air; bombs also reveal hidden doors. (StrategyWiki – Gameplay)
- One-hit deaths & choppy jumps: Touching an enemy, falling, bomb splash, or time-out = death. Movement is infamously stiff vs. Mario. (Wikipedia, HG101)
- Discuss now: Is the “one bomb out at a time” limit clever tension or needless frustration?
- World Layout & Warps (how 101 Zones actually connect)
- Zone graph, not a line: Doors can send you forward or way off-route; numbers don’t equal sequence. A few Zones are only reachable via special input/commands. (Wikipedia)
- MAP https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Atlantis_no_Nazo/Zone_Map
- Warp types you learn by dying:
  - Standard doors (visible).
  - Hidden doors (bomb to reveal; some require a second bomb to unlock; tight 1-frame entry trick exists).
  - Pit warps (fall in specific pits to warp).
  - Death warps (die at the right spot to arrive elsewhere).
  - Black Hole doors (e.g., Zone 42) = instant Game Over.

- Path knowledge = progress: Early Zones can jump you dozens ahead (or to your doom) if you know which pit or door to use. (StrategyWiki – Walkthrough)

- Controls, Physics & Readability
- Bi-directional scrolling: You can backtrack left and right (unlike early Mario), which encourages scouting for hidden exits. (Wikipedia)
- Jump & inertia: Short hop, stiff air control, frequent “bonk” on ledges; learning bomb toss arcs becomes as important as jumping. (HardcoreGaming101)
- HUD pressure: Timer ticks constantly; item pick-ups are simple points/treasure—no safety nets.
- Discuss now: Would a tiny forgiveness buffer (e.g., 2-hit life) have changed this game’s reputation?
- Level & Gimmick Variety (what makes zones unique)
- Zone themes: Fields, caves, ruins, temples, cloud platforms, ice floors, and “black” fall-only stages. Later zones reduce visibility or traction. (Wikipedia)
- Set-piece behaviors:
  - Ice → slide control loss.
  - Dark caves → low visibility, bombs double as flares.
  - Moai/pyramids in backgrounds—Atlantis as a kitchen-sink myth mash-up. (Wikipedia)
- Knowledge gates: Some exits are only practical if you know the bomb-to-door spots or pit warp locations in advance. (StrategyWiki – Zone Map)
- Discuss now: Is the “learn the map” demand closer to Adventure Island secrets or proto-Metroidvania routing?
- Enemies & Hazards
- Standard pests: Bats, ground crawlers, projectile tossers; most die to a single bomb, but splash can chain you into pits.
- Biggest killer: Self-inflicted damage—bomb splash, bad jumps, and Black Hole doors. (HardcoreGaming101, StrategyWiki – Gameplay)
- Discuss now: Are the enemies meaningful, or just pressure while you puzzle out exits?
- Comparisons & Legacy
- Intent vs. outcome: Marketed internally as a “surpass Mario” idea via size; remembered as infamous for traps and physics. (Wikipedia, HG101)
- Echoes: Later Sunsoft titles refine control and fairness (e.g., Batman era), but the route-knowledge spirit shows up in many NES oddities. (HardcoreGaming101 – Sunsoft)
- Modern nods: Fans still reference the “Black Hole → Game Over” gag when discussing evil warp doors. (Community shorthand; see TCRF/strategy maps for specifics. StrategyWiki – Zone Map)
- Discuss now: Is Atlantis no Nazo best enjoyed with a map (a puzzlebox) or blind (a prankster platformer)?
- Version Notes (Famicom specifics)
- Famicom-only original; JP compilations later on PC/PS; unreleased in NA/EU. (Wikipedia)
- Hidden commands: A few Zones exist outside the main path and require special input to reach. (Wikipedia)
- Bi-directional scroll + 1-hit deaths distinguish it from many 1986 peers. (Wikipedia)
- Fun Facts
- 101 Zones was a deliberate marketing flex after Super Mario Bros.’ 32 stages. (Wikipedia)
- Composer Naoki Kodaka would later score Sunsoft’s celebrated NES soundtracks (Batman, Journey to Silius). (Wikipedia)
