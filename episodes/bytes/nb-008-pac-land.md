- Introductions
  - Before we can talk about this game, we need to talk about Pac-Man, because I have a feeling as jarring as this arcade sidescrolling platformer might already be, it gets a little weirder with context
- History
  - Pac-Man Fever kicks this all off
  - They make basically the same game with a female character Ms. Pac-Man and it’s the best selling NA arcade game of all time
    - And in my opinion that was the warning sign. That the first idea, was to mostly leave the game as is
  - Pac-Man Plus
    - Copy the game AGAIN
      - eating a bonus item will cause the ghosts to turn both vulnerable and invisible, and doubles their point value while they are vulnerable. Eating a Power Pellet also sometimes has unpredictable results, such as turning the maze invisible or turning only three of the four ghosts blue (the latter of which makes it impossible to clear a round by using a pattern).
  - Baby Pac-Man
    - Same maze game again only now you exit the maze video and become a pinball on a pinball machine also connected to the arcade cabinet
    - Sounds cool, but really just mixing playing the same pac-man with playing pinball.
  - Super Pac-Man
    - instead of eating dots, the player is required to eat keys in order to open doors, which open up sections of "fruits." Eat all the fruit to clear
    - Pac-Man has a super form where he becomes comically large
  - Pac and Pal
    - Now the same pac-man with a match game addition
    - The player guides Pac-Man through an enclosed maze in which several items are locked behind doors. Several face-down cards are scattered throughout the maze; when Pac-Man runs over one of these, it flips over to reveal one of the items, which becomes unlocked so Pac-Man can eat it.
  - Jr. Pac-Man
    - Now the maze scrolls horizontally. So bigger maze. That’s the gimmick
  - Pac-Man TV Show
    - All about the adventure of Pac-Man, Ms. Pac-Man, and other Pac people in Pac-Land.
  - Professor Pac-Man
    - Designed to capitalize on the perceived quiz game niche, Professor Pac-Man presented simple visual puzzles, and required the players (or "pupils", as they are called by the game) to solve each within a short time limit
    - But this bombed and almost all the cabinets would be returned to be repurposed into the Pac-Land we are talking about today.
- Overview
  - Controlling Pac-Man, the player must make it to the end of each stage to return a lost fairy back to its home in Fairyland. Pac-Man will need to avoid obstacles, such as falling logs and water-spewing fire hydrants, alongside his enemies. Like the arcade game eating large flashing Power Pellet will cause the ghosts to turn blue, allowing Pac-Man to eat them for points.

  - Development
    - Kishimoto cited Konami's sports video game Track & Field (1983) as the "number one influence" on Pac-Land. The game's controls were heavily influenced by Track & Field, a game that allowed the player to become faster by constantly tapping the button in succession
    - Kishimoto thought the idea was interesting and that it would make it stand out among other games.
    - To allow for two-layer scrolling backgrounds, more sprites, and more colors, the team created the Namco Pac-Land arcade board
