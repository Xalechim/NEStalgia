- A little historical grounding here
  - One of the earliest computer adventure games ever made
  - Allegedly the first computer adventure that used menus instead of a text parser (predating both Irem and LucasArts
  - Hideo Kojima sights it as a major inspiration for both Snatcher and how he got into gaming.
  - The game was developed by Yuuji Horii, the father of the Dragon Quest series
- Plot
  - You play as a detective trying to solve the murder of KouZou. You’re only lead is that he was found in his study with a knife in his back and the door locked from the inside. No signs of breaking in and yet no indication he committed suicide.
- Let’s break down what you can do to help solve this case
  - Ask a Question
    - "He's the one who was killed. Probably doesn't need to have an alibi."
  - Take an Item
  - Investigate a thing
  - Move to a place
  - Dial a phone
  - Look at evidence
  - Look for someone
  - Call Out (Really only for the Police HQ to call witnesses in)
  - Show an Item
  - Hit
- What I didn’t like:
  - You should be able to see what you need to investigate with the magnifying glass.
    - Maybe the glass could reveal it?
    - Maybe it would be in plain sight
    - Maybe you could open a drawer and then see a new screen of what is in that drawer
  - Sudden Information
    - Investigating with the magnifying glass
    - Sometimes just moving to a location and going back to the Police HQ is enough to trigger progress. That is unclear and doesn’t necessarily feel earned
  - The Maze at the end of the game
    - Completely unnecessary though obviously inspired by Western computer games
- How non-linear does it get?
  - there are no fail-states
  - There’s a lot of dialogue that doesn’t lead anywhere
  - A lot of the sub plots are necessary to get to the end, they just wind up being red herrings
- How deductive can you be? Is the story grounded or do you have to take giant mental leaps

- NEStalgia Byte - STAR LUSTER
- Guide Dang It!: Lots of it, but a few instances especially stand out:
  - The ring. The player has to go to the outside of the Yamakawa Mansion and examine the bottom the door, a completely random and inconspicuous area, and then present it to Toshi. Not only does nothing even hint that such an item would even exist, but by this point the player is likely to have had Toshi arrested, and would thus have no reason to think there's anything else for him to even say.
  - Proving to the barkeep who Kouzou is and that he has a connection with Kawahara. It requires heading back to Kouzou's mansion and examining two spots which are completely unmarked. Not just objects, either, but specific spots, and the latter of the items, the lighter, doesn't even have any clear relation to the conversation anyway.
  - By far the worst however is finding Kouzou's diary. After being told of something hidden in the maze, the player has to go down there, follow the steps provided to them...and find themselves in a random hallway. The solution? You have run into one of the walls multiple times, revealing it conceals a hidden room, the only hint to this being that wall plays a different sound if you run into it. Oh, and forgot the directions given to you? You're out of luck, since it's only told to you once.
