# NB 036 - Cosmo Police Galivan

- Introduction to Cosmo Police Galivan
- Overview of the game:
  - Developed and published by Nihon Bussan (Nichibutsu).
  - Loosely based on their 1985 arcade platformer.
  - Released exclusively in Japan.
- Story and setting:
  - Plot: Cyborg cop Galivan is the last of the Cosmo Police, battling the Mado syndicate on the fortress planet of Badurr.
  - Inspired by Japanese action TV shows (Space Sheriff Gavan and Sharivan).
- Metroidvania Roots and Genre Evolution
- Influence and similarities to other games:
  - Structurally reminiscent of Metroid (horizontal corridors, vertical shafts, special doors).
  - Incorporates elements from Zelda II (elevators, NPC interactions, house-like interiors) and Castlevania II (short-range sword combat and rhythm).
- Key features aligned with Metroidvania conventions:
  - Exploration-based gameplay with distinct stages.
  - Progression tied to finding upgrades and uncovering secrets.
- Gameplay and Combat Mechanics
- Combat:
  - Galivan fights with short-range swords (“blades”), similar to Castlevania: Symphony of the Night.
  - Stun mechanics and repeated hits disrupt enemy attacks, creating a unique combat rhythm.
- Special weapons and CP (Cosmo Points):
  - Ranged weapons like the Cosmo Boomerang and Spark Ball.
  - CP acts as a resource for weapons and abilities, reminiscent of Castlevania’s heart mechanic.
- Blade leveling system:
  - Blades rank up from C to B to A through experience, significantly increasing power.
  - Progression rewards skilled play, but requires grinding for optimal effectiveness.
- Level Design and Progression
- Stage-based structure:
  - Unlike Metroid’s interconnected world, Galivan uses distinct, linear stages with thematic variety (high-tech bases, underwater caverns, etc.).
  - Benefits: Simpler layouts reduce backtracking and encourage exploration within compact areas.
- Exploration and secrets:
  - Dense stages filled with hidden passages and mandatory upgrades.
  - Use of warp points to alternate dimensions adds complexity and variety.
  - Importance of hints from NPC cyborg agents, uncovered through kneeling interactions.
- Difficulty and Challenges
- Death and progression:
  - Punishments for dying include loss of CP and experience progress toward the next level.
  - Blade experience persists after death, offering incremental progression even after setbacks.
- Boss fights:
  - Tough and pattern-based; often require precise learning or resource management.
  - Some encounters can be bypassed with clever use of healing items.
- Cryptic hints and reliance on fan translations:
  - Essential for uncovering secrets and navigating certain stages.
  - Praise for the translation’s role in accessibility.
- Comparison to Other Games
- Parallels with classic and future Metroidvanias:
  - Castlevania similarities: combat rhythm, use of CP, and leveling mechanics.
  - Stages as “mini Metroids,” reducing aimless exploration.
- Linear structure’s modern relevance:
  - Rare in the genre but works well (e.g., Iji).
  - Eliminates excessive backtracking and provides clear stopping points.
- Sequels and Spinoffs
- This began as an arcade game which was more like a beat em up meets shoot em up
- A sequel appears on SNES and goes full beat em up
- Closing Thoughts
- Cosmo Police Galivan as a hidden gem for Metroidvania enthusiasts.
- Discussion on its potential influence on later classics or as an independently innovative title.
- Encouragement to try the game, with acknowledgment of its challenging boss fights and enjoyable progression.
