# 389 - Dr. Mario

- The prescription is fun!
- General Overview
- Developed by Nintendo R&D1, published by Nintendo.
- Released: October 1990
- A puzzle game starring Mario in a lab coat—not a platformer, but a Tetris-adjacent virus-matching game.
- Game was released during the puzzle game craze, post-Tetris.
- Notably composed by Hirokazu “Hip” Tanaka—incredible music.
- Opening Bit Suggestion:
 “The only time Mario saved lives with capsules instead of mushrooms.”
- Story & Objective
- Mario has apparently received his medical license. No details provided.
- Objective: Eradicate viruses from a medicine bottle using colored capsules.
- Viruses come in red, blue, and yellow. Match four of the same color in a row (including capsule pieces) to eliminate them.
- Notably, the viruses are animated and expressive, with little bouncing idle motions.
- Gameplay Mechanics
- Falling block-style gameplay (Tetris comparison inevitable, but different strategy).
- Rotate and position two-color capsules to line up with viruses.
- Strategic depth: Must think in color and orientation; capsule fragments remain after clearing, creating chaos or opportunities.
- Speed and difficulty increase with each level; virus count rises.
- Two game modes: Single-player and Vs. Mode (competitive split-screen).
- Graphics & Sound
- Clean and readable playfield. Bold colors on a simple black background.
- Mario watches you from the top-right corner like a weirdly smug lab supervisor.
- Iconic music: “Fever” and “Chill” themes are beloved 8-bit earworms.
  - “Fever” is one of the most iconic NES tracks of all time.
- Legacy & Reception
- 1. Dr. Mario (NES/Game Boy, 1990)
 The original! Introduced the color-matching, virus-killing capsule gameplay and the iconic “Fever” and “Chill” themes.
- 2. Dr. Mario (Super Nintendo, 1991 – “Tetris & Dr. Mario”)
 Bundled with Tetris; added a Vs. CPU mode and updated visuals, but core gameplay remained intact.
- 3. Dr. Mario 64 (Nintendo 64, 2001)
 Expanded multiplayer with 4-player battles, story mode featuring Wario and Mad Scienstein, and a handful of new modes.
- 4. Dr. Mario Express (DSiWare, 2008)
 A stripped-down, single-player version for handheld digital download—no multiplayer, but perfect for quick play.
- 5. Dr. Mario Online Rx (WiiWare, 2008)
 Brought online multiplayer to the series for the first time, plus included the “Virus Buster” mode (played with pointer control).
- 6. Dr. Mario: Miracle Cure (3DS, 2015)
 Introduced “Miracle Cure” power-ups that add randomness and speed—attempted to modernize gameplay for handhelds.
- 7. Dr. Mario World (iOS/Android, 2019–2021)
 Free-to-play mobile entry with stamina system, gacha elements, and dozens of doctors (Dr. Bowser, Dr. Peach, etc.); shut down in 2021.
- Other Appearances & Spin-Offs
- – Super Smash Bros. Melee (2001)
 Dr. Mario debuts as a playable character—similar to Mario, but with a stronger punch and the “Megavitamin” projectile.
- – Super Smash Bros. Ultimate (2018)
 Returns with a tweaked moveset and distinct character slot; his own music and virus stage included.
- – NES Remix (Wii U/3DS)
 Features Dr. Mario mini-challenges, often with remixed difficulty or visual effects.
- – WarioWare Series (Multiple entries)
 Various microgames spoof Dr. Mario, often involving pill-dropping or virus-zapping in a few seconds.
- – Puzzle League Games (Panel de Pon)
 Dr. Mario sometimes appears as a theme or cameo (especially in Puzzle League DS or Nintendo Puzzle Collection).

 Licensed (?) medical professional dispensing color-coded capsules with alarming speed.
