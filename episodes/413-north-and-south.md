# 413 - North and South

- Your combat prowess and brilliant strategy could rewrite history
- 1. General Overview
- What it is
 North & South is a mash-up of turn-based strategy on a map and real-time action mini-games (battles, fort raids, train robberies), all wrapped in a Civil War campaign. Wikipedia+1
- The hook
 It looks like “Risk-lite” on the map, then suddenly turns into little minigames when armies collide or when you try to take a rail town. Wikipedia+1
- Offbeat questions
- Is the main appeal the strategy layer, or is it really the fact that strategy is just a way to trigger mini-games? Wikipedia
- 2. Source Material and Tone
- Based on a Belgian comic
 The game is an adaptation/spin-off of Les Tuniques Bleues (The Bluecoats), which is set against the American Civil War, but the game leans hard into comedy and caricature. Wikipedia+1
- Tone clash on purpose
 It treats a serious subject with cartoon visuals
- Offbeat questions
- Does the light tone make it easier to approach, or does it flatten the “Civil War game” part into window dressing? Wikipedia
- 3. Setting, Objective, and What the “War” Actually Means Here
- You pick a side
 Union (North) or Confederacy (South), then play out a simplified version of the conflict on a state map. Wikipedia+1
- Start year choice
 You can start the campaign in different years (1861 to 1864), changing the opening situation. Wikipedia
- Winning
 The practical objective is to take territory, build up armies through income, and grind the opponent down until they cannot hold states. Wikipedia+1
- Offbeat questions
- When you “win,” do you feel like you won via smart positioning, or because you were better at the mini-games?
- 4. Strategy Layer: The Map, Turns, and Capturing States
- Turn-based movement
 On your turn you move armies from state to adjacent state, generally once per unit per turn. DigitPress+1
- How a state flips
Multiple capture cases: empty states, enemy-controlled states with no defending army, states occupied by an enemy army (battle), and rail towns that force a fort mini-game to fully claim. DigitPress+1
- Joining forces
 You can merge armies by moving onto a friendly army, up to a cap (manual calls out max totals like 3 cannons, 9 cavalry, 18 infantry). DigitPress
- Surround captures
 There’s a rule where you can flip certain states by occupying all adjacent states (manual describes “surrounding your opponent”). DigitPress
- You can check the composition of an enemy army on the map by moving the pointer over it and pressing A, which shows the counts of cannon, cavalry, and infantry. This becomes the difference between “sure, I’ll attack” and “I’m about to feed my cannons to a cavalry stack.” DigitPress
- 5. Economy and Railroads: Why Towns Matter
- Money loop
 At the end of turns, states generate income, which then helps generate soldiers and keeps you expanding. Rail towns are especially important. Wikipedia+1
  - Notice how you don’t have to then manage feeding an army or keeping civilians happy or raising taxes
- The railway “tax” concept
 The manual highlights controlling towns along the railway route to collect revenues, and also explains the idea of intercepting the opponent’s taxes by attacking along their train route. DigitPress
- Offbeat questions
- Do you play this like a land-grab game, or like a “railway economy” game where the train route is the real win condition? DigitPress
- 6. Action Mini-Game: Battle (Infantry, Cavalry, Cannon)
- When it triggers
 Opposing armies in the same state drops you into a real-time battle screen instead of resolving it abstractly. Wikipedia+1
- Unit roles
  - Infantry: short-range volleys, can move freely.
  - Cavalry: fast melee charges, limited movement behavior.
  - Cannon: long-range shots, movement is constrained (vertical only is a common description), and cannon ammo is limited. Wikipedia+1
- Controls and unit switching
 Manual describes using B to choose what type you’re commanding, and A to initiate movement and attacks. DigitPress
- Bridge and choke points
 Some battlefields hinge on a bridge that can be damaged or destroyed by cannon fire, radically changing the fight. Wikipedia
- Offbeat questions
- Is the cannon the “real” win button, and everything else is support? (A lot of players end up playing it that way.) DigitPress+1
- If the action segments were removed entirely, would the remaining map game still be fun?
- 7. Action Mini-Game: Capturing a Fort (Side-scroller)
- When it triggers
 States with a town (often tied to rail stations) may require you to complete a fort assault mini-game to truly claim them. Wikipedia+1
- What you do
 You control a single soldier running right under a time limit, using knives or punches, navigating obstacles like dogs and explosives, climbing ladders, and reaching the flag. DigitPress+1
- Risk factor
 Failing the fort run can stall your conquest even if you are winning the map game. DigitPress+1
- Offbeat questions
- Do you treat fort captures as a fun skill check, or as an annoyance that interrupts the strategy layer? DigitPress
- 8. Action Mini-Game: Robbing a Train (Side-scroller)
- Why it matters
 Train robberies are basically economic warfare. Pull it off and you steal the opponent’s tax revenue from their rail line. DigitPress+1
- How it plays
 Jump onto the train, move across cars, deal with enemy soldiers, and reach the engine before time runs out. DigitPress+1
- In the train sequence, if you collect 5 bags of gold, you get a new army you can place on blinking states. That can swing a campaign harder than winning one normal battle.
- 9. Options and “Chaos Switches” (Disasters)
- The game has toggles that add wildcards and map friction.
- Native Americans and Mexicans
 Optional threats that can periodically attack or bomb certain areas and mess up armies. DigitPress+1
- Storm cloud
 A moving cloud can disable movement for armies under it. DigitPress+1
- European reinforcements
 Optional setting that can deliver reinforcements to the side controlling North Carolina
- 10. Multiplayer and “How This Plays With a Friend”
- Head-to-head
 Two players can play the full campaign, and the action segments become direct skill contests instead of AI chores. MobyGames+1
- Offbeat questions
- With the minigames Is this secretly a party game pretending to be a war game? MobyGames+1
- 11. Things You’d Only Know If You Played It a Lot
- Cannon management is everything
 Cannons have limited shots and can withdraw automatically, so experienced players treat cannons like a resource you preserve, not just a weapon you spam. Wikipedia+1
- Merging is a commitment
 You can stack up to the cap, but you cannot freely “unmerge,” so you can accidentally create a slow, awkward doomstack that is great in battle but clumsy on the map. DigitPress+1

- Confederate victory timelines (the big alternate-history lane)
- The Guns of the South (Harry Turtledove, 1992)
 Time-travelers arm the Confederacy with modern rifles, and the Civil War veers wildly off the rails. Wikipedia
