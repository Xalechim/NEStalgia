# 442 - Princess Tomato in the Salad Kingdom

- You're a Cucumber. But you're also a Knight of the Realm.

## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch: a vegetable knight goes to rescue a kidnapped tomato You play as Sir Cucumber, the late King Broccoli's bravest knight, in a kingdom populated almost entirely by talking fruits and vegetables. The royal advisor, Minister Pumpkin, stages a coup: he kidnaps Princess Tomato, steals the royal Turnip Emblem, and drags both to his fortress in the Zucchini Mountains. King Broccoli then dies of grief. With his dying breath the king promises Cucumber the princess's hand and the throne if he pulls off the rescue. Cucumber is helped by Percy, a baby persimmon sidekick, and a resistance led by Tomato's younger brother, Prince Lettuce.
- It started in 1984 as a Japanese home-computer adventure
  - Hudson ported it to the Famicom in 1988, and that Famicom version is what got translated and shipped to the NES in North America in February 1991.
  - Developed and published by Hudson Soft.
  - It is a menu-driven adventure game, closer to Shadowgate or a point-and-click than to anything with a sword you swing in real time. You pick commands like LOOK, CHECK, TAKE, MOVE and HIT off the screen and watch the story play out in still pictures.

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- The whole world is produce, and it commits to the bit
  - Threatens that the kingdom could be "tossed into a frenzy, or even into a bowl with a dash of oil and vinegar."
  - A talky, menu-driven adventure about a vegetable was a genuine oddity on the shelf.
  - The story was expanded from four chapters in the original to eight in the remake (HG101),
  - New to this version:
    - getting captured and escaping prison
    - a second town (Sopville) that you explore while disguised as the enemy
    - three maze sequences
    - and the Finger Wars combat.
  - The jail warden is named Sergeant Pepper. When the game hit the Wii Virtual Console he was quietly renamed Lieutenant Pepper, almost certainly to avoid a fight with the Beatles' estate over Sgt. Pepper.
  - In Chapter 5, if you use HIT on the picture in Lisa's room, the game reveals a hidden picture it identifies as Tom Cruise.
  - Lisa, Princess Tomato's sister, is inexplicably a human being in a kingdom of sentient vegetables, and nobody acknowledges this.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Most of the game is static pictures with commands lining the screen.
  - You drive the story by choosing the right verb on the right object.
  - The command and item loop You wander screen to screen collecting objects (water, flowers, keys, gold) and using the right verb in the right spot to advance.

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- Percy the persimmon is the heart of it
  - In the computer original he just hands you info.
  - In the NES version he is promoted to full squire: his own command, useful in puzzles, calls you "Boss," and supplies most of the comic relief.
  - You almost cannot lose. There is no way to die and no real way to get permanently stuck. The only fail state is losing the Finger Wars against Pumpkin, and you just try again.
    - That makes it a great gateway adventure but a low-tension one. Compared to Shadowgate, where death is everywhere, this is a stroll.
    - No battery save, passwords instead You continue via passwords rather than a save chip, which dates it hard.

## V. History, Localization & Censorship

- A cigarette shop became a coffee bar, a liquor store became a juice shop, and a Shinto shimenawa rope on a tree was removed because it would not read to US players.

## VI. Sequels and Spinoffs

- There is no real sequel Princess Tomato is essentially a standalone, ported across many platforms rather than continued in new entries. NES-only players got their one and only taste here.
- The one true "spinoff" is a cameo Princess Tomato resurfaced decades later as a playable DLC fighter, "Princess Tomato Bomber," in Super Bomberman R in 2017
- Comparison games Shadowgate and Deja Vu: the obvious NES adventure shelf-mates, but darker and deadlier. Princess Tomato is the friendly opposite.
- Portopia Renzoku Satsujin Jiken: the Japanese adventure touchstone, and the source of the silent-hero-plus-talkative-partner format Percy slots into.
- Maniac Mansion (NES): another verb-menu adventure squeezed onto the console, good for contrast on how clunky or clean the interface feels.

## VII. Essential Games List Vote

- Case for Essential
  - It is charming, funny, and an ideal on-ramp for anyone scared off by adventure-game difficulty: no death, no dead ends, generous hinting from Percy and Mr. Mole.
- Case against Essential
  - The gameplay is thin: still screens, menu verbs, and rock paper scissors fights that mostly come down to pattern memorization.
  - The mazes and Finger Wars feel bolted on rather than designed in.
  - The redrawn NES art is a clear downgrade from the colorful computer originals, so the NES version is arguably not even the best way to experience the game.
- Prologue. Minister Pumpkin, once a trusted advisor to King Broccoli, turns power-hungry and kidnaps the king's daughter, Princess Tomato. The king dies of grief, and his loyal knight, Sir Cucumber, sets out to rescue the princess and restore the kingdom.
- Chapter 1: The Adventure Commences. Sir Cucumber travels through the celery forest and revives a thirsty baby persimmon named Percy, who joins him as a companion and adviser. He helps Prince Lettuce (the princess's younger brother) and some dried-up melons, earning a gate pass. The pass fails at the town gate, so he finds another route and slips past the sleeping guard into the town.
- Chapter 2: Saladoria. In the town of Saladoria, Sir Cucumber runs a chain of favors and errands, delivering coffee, a letter, and food, while gathering items and clues. He learns that a secret resistance is forming against Minister Pumpkin. At the bookshop, Percy warns that the clerk is working for the enemy. Sir Cucumber confronts him and grabs a key, but on his way out he's captured by the farmies, Pumpkin's henchmen.
- Chapter 3: Captured! Locked up, Sir Cucumber gets a file from a fellow inmate and is dragged in for interrogation by Lt. Pepper. He escapes the torture room, helps a garlic wanderer in the jail, and uses a fake soap grenade to scare Lt. Pepper before tying him up. Disguising himself with a lantern, he walks out of the police station.
- Chapter 4: The Forest Maze. He aids Watermelon and a mole, picking up word of a monster called Saladron, and gets water wings to cross a stream Percy can't swim. This is where combat begins, played as Rock-Paper-Scissors. After navigating the Parsley Forest maze, he defeats Saladron at its lair and digs up a medal. Mr. Leek meets him to close out the chapter.
- Chapter 5: The Resistance Base. Using the medal, Sir Cucumber proves he's a resistance member. He meets Lisa, the princess's human sister, and General Cantaloupe, the resistance leader, who hands him the resistance crest. After recovering Lisa's lost pendant, he gains access to the arms room and takes a nut bomb, then learns of a monster named Bananda near Peanut Village.
- Chapter 6: Peanut Plateau. In Peanut Village he gathers info and supplies, then enters Bananda's cave and destroys the monster with the nut bomb, freeing a captive named Nutty trapped inside. With a battery from the village chief, he pilots a war machine called the Dice-O-Matic and battles Minister Pumpkin's machines, then Pumpkin himself, who escapes. At a giant tree he finds chameleon grass and instructions for it, then disguises himself as a farmie to end the chapter.
- Chapter 7: Sopville. In the town of Sopville, Sir Cucumber works with resistance contacts using the crest and pieces together word of a tunnel leading to the castle. An old lady finally gives him the route through the dark passage, and he digs out the far exit of the cave.
- Chapter 8: The Minister's Castle. Inside the castle, Sir Cucumber searches room by room for the princess, who always seems to have just left. He slips past a sleeping guard, frees imprisoned vegetables, and collects a caterpillar. He discovers Princess Tomato disguised as an old woman and uses the chameleon grass to reveal her. After crossing a perilous basement maze guarded by a monster family, he defeats Minister Pumpkin in a final battle and heads for the garden exit with the princess.
- Chapter 9: The Celebration. At the heroes' celebration, Pumpkin's son, Jr. Pumpkin, challenges Sir Cucumber. Beating him in a fight accomplishes nothing, but using the caterpillar on him settles things, and the game ends with peace restored to the Salad Kingdom.
