# 452 - Totally Rad

- BACK-OF-BOX OPENING HOOK: Totally Gruesome Monsters

## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch
  - A California surfer kid learns real magic and fights his way to the center of the earth.
  - Mega Man style side-scroller with a spell menu bolted on.
- WHAT IS GOING ON IN THAT INTRO. Hyper detailed characters. Nonsensical sentences. And it ends on Jake saying Zebediah the magician is TOTALLY DECENT, followed by the TOTALLY RAD title screen. He should have said the title!
- The story
  - You are Jake, apprentice to a stage magician named Zebediah Pong, whose magic turns out to be real.
  - Jake's girlfriend Allison gets grabbed by creatures from an underground world.
  - The kidnapping is bait. They actually want Allison's father, a scientist.
  - The final villain is Edogy, an underground king coming for the surface world.
  - Ending twist: Zebediah is one of the underground people, exiled. He goes home.
- The identity problem
  - In Japan this is Magic John, Famicom, September 1990. Developed by Aicom, published by Jaleco.
  - The American version kept the game and replaced the people in it.
  - John and Yuu, big-eyed anime kids who are friends, became Jake and Allison, teenagers who are dating.
  - Zebediah was left completely alone. Same sprite, same art, both versions. Nobody knows why.

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- The color is doing the absolute most
  - HG101 calls it close to a virtual rainbow simulator.
  - Purple walls, city lights, glowing underground crystals.
  - One of the few NES games with parallax scrolling. Real technical flex for 1991.
- The bosses are the reason to play it
  - They take up half the screen.
  - Rubyllia: a crocodile whose torso is an ear of corn. Pink punk hair, earrings, platform shoes.
  - It attacks by popping its own kernels at you. Weak point is the gem in its navel.
  - Biclopter: one eye, one leg, and a spare eye on its backside.
  - Nautikill: a rainbow fish that spits smaller fish.
  - Fang: a giant cyber-skeleton.
  - Edogy: final boss, and everybody agrees the design rules. Second phase is on roller skates.
- You start the game fully loaded
  - Every spell and transformation is available on the first screen.
  - The trade: there are no pickups or collectibles anywhere.
  - Inverted Mega Man. You are not earning tools, you are rationing them.
- Short and it knows it
  - Five acts, two or three stages each, five to eight minutes a stage.
  - With twelve total lives, the brevity is a mercy.
- High Score Board
  - Midway through, Zeb patches Allison in telepathically for a rescue update.
  - She reports she has been practicing magic, and she will help as soon as she gets unchained.
  - Out of the locked trunk. Which is submerged in a tank of water. Then she asks where the key went.
  - The kicker comes at the end: she chained herself in there. On purpose. Mid-kidnapping.
  - Jake's response is that they should probably stick to skateboarding.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Controls
  - D-pad to move, Down to kneel.
  - A jumps.
  - B fires the energy blast. Tap for one shot, hold for a double, hold till Jake glows for a triple. (HG101)
  - Up plus B casts the selected spell. Same input as a Castlevania subweapon.
  - Start opens the magic menu. A selects. (GameFAQs)
- The charge shot catch
  - You can only charge on the ground. Jumping dumps the charge automatically.
  - Half the bosses are fought at head height, where a charged jump shot is exactly what you want.
  - The base shot barely does anything. This is the worst decision in the game.
- Level structure
  - Five acts, miniboss partway, full boss at the end.
  - No time limit anywhere, and that is deliberate.
  - The game punishes anyone playing it at Mega Man speed. Slow and methodical is correct.
- The magic economy is the actual game
  - Twelve spells. Each cast burns 2 or 4 notches.
  - The meter does not refill until a checkpoint or the end of the stage. No farming your way out.
  - Health is six pips, and plenty of enemies hit for two. Effectively a three-hit life bar.
  - So every screen-clear you fire is a heal you gave up. That is the whole loop.
- The twelve spells, honestly counted
  - Four utility: full heal, half heal, time stop, invincibility.
  - Four elemental screen-clears: fire, water, wind, earth. Differences appear cosmetic. (HG101)
  - Three transformations: tiger, bird, fish.
  - One revert to Jake, free, required before casting anything else.
  - Twelve sounds enormous. Meaningfully distinct tools is closer to six.

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- The transformations are the good idea here
  - Tiger: highest damage, invincible while jumping, short range, ground only.
  - Bird: flies indefinitely by holding jump, throws boomerangs.
  - Fish: swims properly, fast attack, high jump, throws stars.
  - You cannot cast anything else while transformed. Every switch is a commitment.
- They also expose the balance problem
  - Pick the right form and bosses fold in seconds.
  - The Act 4 boss dies in about ten seconds to the bird. (GameFAQs)
  - So difficulty is less about execution and more about guessing the right animal.
- The minibosses are harder than the bosses
  - The GameFAQs author says so outright and gave them their own section.
  - Best one: a drill robot whose eye pops out and bounces around.
  - You ignore the eye and hit the body, but you have to keep the eye on screen.
  - Let it wander off or reattach and the fight gets much worse.
- The offensive magic is basically decorative
  - A screen-clear costs two unrechargeable points and most enemies respawn seconds later.
  - Twelve spells, and the correct play is almost always heal, or turn into a bird.
- The mentor tells you your own spells are broken
  - Second cutscene, Zeb warns Jake that some of his magic just will not work.
  - Either a mistranslation or the writers being honest about the balance. Either way, remarkable.

## V. History, Development, and Reception

- Who made it
  - Aicom developed, Jaleco published. Music by Kazuo Sawa and Toshiko Tasaki.
  - Aicom also made Vice: Project Doom and The Mafat Conspiracy.
- The localization
  - Jaleco USA rewrote the whole script into surfer and Valley slang.
  - Aged the leads into teenagers, redrew the sprites, retitled the game.
  - The manual got the same treatment, every page in the voice.
  - The writer slipped a photo of his own girlfriend into the booklet, with commentary on how good she looks.
  - The manual also spoils Zeb's ending twist, then tells you that you are not supposed to know that yet.
- Jaleco did this twice in one month
  - Whomp 'Em shipped the same month, March 1991, same publisher.
  - It is a reskin of Saiyuki World 2, which starred Sun Wukong, the Monkey King.
  - They swapped in a Native American hero and changed almost nothing else.
  - He still runs through visibly East Asian environments. Bamboo forests and all.
- The press liked it at the time
  - Nintendo Power, Volume 23, April 1991. Strategy feature, graphics and sound score of 3.7.
  - Mean Machines gave it 63 out of 100. Called it reasonable but oversimplified.
- Then the reputation flipped
  - It got reviewed well when new and mocked relentlessly fifteen years later.
  - The gameplay never changed. Only the jokes landed differently.
- Where to play it now
  - City Connection put both versions on Switch in April 2025.
  - Includes an English translation of the original Magic John script, plus rewind and quick save.

## VI. What Actually Changed in the American Version

- It is harder on purpose
  - A long list of enemies deal 1 damage in Magic John and 2 in Totally Rad. (TCRF)
  - Foot soldiers, hover-pods, the 1-1 miniboss, the clowns, the drill riders, the Act 3 boss projectiles.
  - On a six-pip health bar, that halves your effective health across most of the game.
- And the continues got gutted
  - Magic John has unlimited continues. You basically cannot lose. (Wikipedia)
  - Totally Rad gives you three. Fourth time out of lives, straight to Game Over.
  - Three continues, three lives each. Twelve attempts at the whole game, no way to earn more.
  - Nobody talks about this because everybody is busy talking about the word bodacious.
- Two hidden codes
  - Level select and sound test: Up, Up, Down, Down, Left, Left, Right, Right, Start on the title screen.
  - Staff credits: Right, Left, Left, Right, Up, Down, Down, Up on controller two, then Start on controller one.
- The credits are broken in the American version only
  - They display fine in Magic John.
  - The American logo is bigger, and nobody moved the scanline interrupt to match. The bottom pulls wrong tiles.
  - The version that changed everything about the presentation broke the screen with the developers' names on it.
- Unused art
  - Both versions have sprites of the hero in a raise-the-roof pose that the game never uses.
  - A raise-the-roof pose cut from Totally Rad is almost too on the nose.
- Hardware quirk
  - The Japanese cart skips a PPU warm-up delay the Famicom does not need.
  - Run Magic John on an American NES and it only boots after the console has been off 30 seconds.

## VII. Pop Culture

- The slang came from a very specific moment
  - Bill and Ted's Excellent Adventure, February 1989. Saturday morning cartoon, September 1990.
  - Totally Rad shipped March 1991. Bogus Journey did not arrive until that July.
  - Jaleco rode the first movie and the cartoon and beat the sequel to shelves.
  - Everyone who reviews this game says the same thing: Jake looks like Ted Logan.
- "Rad" was already a decade old
  - Short for radical, out of skate and surf culture in the late seventies.
  - Rad Racer was on the NES in 1987. The BMX movie Rad came out in 1986.
  - So this was not cutting edge slang. It was the safe, slightly-behind version of cool.
  - That gap is most of why the game aged the way it did.
- The economics of a late NES release
  - March 1991 is the tail end. The Super Nintendo launched that August.
  - Jaleco had a shelf space problem, not a quality problem.
  - A loud yellow box shouting TOTALLY RAD was a rational answer to that problem.
- The California of it all
  - This is specifically Southern California, not generic America. Nintendo Power called Jake a kid from Southern Cal.
  - In the ending, Allison explains that she and Zeb got there by taking the Hollywood Freeway.

## VIII. Sequels, Spinoffs, and Comparison Games

- There is no sequel in either language
  - Magic John got no follow-up. Totally Rad got no follow-up.
- Comparison games
  - Mega Man 4: Totally Rad had a chargeable shot first, in early 1991. Worse version, but earlier. (HG101)

## IX. Essential Games List Vote

- Case for Essential
  - It is the clearest artifact of the repaint-it-for-America era, and the repaint went all the way down into the difficulty numbers.
  - Supporting: the bosses are memorable in a library full of forgettable ones.
- Case against Essential
  - Strip the localization and it is a stiff Mega Man clone where the right play is always heal or turn into a bird.
  - Supporting: reviewers said the same thing in 1991, before anyone was laughing at the box.
