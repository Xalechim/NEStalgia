- What Jackie Chan Was Doing Around 1990
- Film career context
 By the time this game hit in late 1990, Jackie Chan was already a gigantic star in Hong Kong and much of Asia, known for combining slapstick comedy with elaborate, dangerous stunts. Films like Project A, Police Story, and Armour of God had cemented that "I will actually hold onto this this bus for real" persona.
- Immediate pre game period (late 80s)
  - Police Story 2 (1988) and Dragons Forever (1988) continued his modern cop and ensemble action streak. Wikipedia
  - Miracles (1989) put him in a period gangster setting, again as star and director. Wikipedia
- Right around release (1990–1991)
  - Island of Fire (1990) had Jackie in a grim prison action film. Wikipedia
  - Armour of God II: Operation Condor (1991) followed shortly after, sending him on a globe trotting treasure hunt with huge stunt sequences. Wikipedia+1
  - Internationally, he was still in that odd space where he was a legend in HK cinema, but mainstream American breakout hits like Rumble in the Bronx were still a few years out. Wikipedia+1
- Why this matters for the NES game
 The Jackie on screen in 1990 is incredibly physical, improvising with props, and constantly one bad step away from breaking his neck. The NES Jackie cannot throw ladders or slide down buses, but the game leans into:
  - Athletic movement and clean, readable hits.
  - A light, comedic tone instead of grim violence.
  - A hero who keeps getting back up after absurd punishment, just like his film persona.
- 1. General Overview – NES Version
- Elevator pitch
 Side scrolling action platformer where you control Jackie Chan, knocking around cartoon martial arts enemies across five stages to rescue his kidnapped sister from an evil sorcerer. Developed by Now Production and published by Hudson Soft, released on NES in December 1990 in North America, with later Famicom and PAL releases. Wikipedia+1
- Basic feel
Chunky, responsive action with big sprites, generous hitboxes, light combo juggle vibes, and very few cheap deaths compared to other NES action games. The difficulty sits more in pattern learning and managing your limited continues than in perfect jumps.

- 2. Story & Objective
- Set up
 Jackie and his twin sister Josephine are training, when the Prince of Sorcerers swoops in, zaps Jackie, and kidnaps Josephine. Jackie recovers in a field outside town and begins a five stage march to punch through monsters and rescue her, ultimately taking down the sorcerer himself.
- Overall objective
Clear five linear stages, each capped by a boss, while collecting orbs, power ups, and bonus continues. Stages move left to right or sometimes vertically, ending in a big set piece encounter before Jackie advances to the next region of China.
- Tone
 The plot is straight heroic kung fu fantasy, but the presentation is cute: Chibi Jackie, frogs carrying rice bowls, and goofy enemies like kappas and tigers. It is very "Saturday afternoon Hong Kong cartoon filtered through NES chibi" rather than gritty Police Story (a movie he was in). Wikipedia+1

- 3. Core Gameplay Loop & Level Structure
- Basic loop
  - Walk, jump, and kick through enemy filled stages.
  - Smack frogs for power ups and rice bowls.
  - Collect green orbs to recharge life and your special beam.
  - Reach the boss, learn their pattern, repeat for five levels. Wikipedia+2Codex Gamicus+2
- Levels
 Strategy guides and playthroughs generally frame it as:
  - Stage 1: Grasslands and basic platforms, introductory boss.
  - Stage 2: Mountain area with fire and lava style hazards. StrategyWiki+1
  - Stage 3: Ice and snow themed area with slippery footing and yeti type enemies. Tropedia+1
  - Stage 4: More fantastical temple and statue heavy set pieces.
  - Stage 5: Final castle / lair leading to the sorcerer and his giant spider form. Tropedia+1
- Bonus stages
  - Hit a bell in a stage to warp to a 30 second bonus game.
  - These usually have Jackie bouncing on clouds or pummeling statues to rack up points.
  - After time is up, points are converted into extra lives and special attacks. Wikipedia+1
- Only if you played
 The bonus stages feel almost like a proto "WarioWare warmup": weird, self-contained challenges that break up the otherwise straightforward brawling.

- 4. Controls, Combat & Special Moves
- Controls
 Standard NES layout:
  - D pad: move and crouch.
  - A: jump.
  - B: attack.
  - Certain combinations trigger spin kicks and uppercuts depending on your power ups.
- Combat flow
 Jackie has a short range punch and a flying kick, with a few special moves obtained via power ups. Unlike many NES action games, his attacks feel fast and give you a bit of stun on contact, so you can bully crowds if you keep moving. Wikipedia+2RetroGame Man+2
- Beam attack and power ups
  - Hitting frogs can drop special scrolls that grant limited use special moves (stronger kicks, area attacks, etc). You can only hold one at a time.
  - Jackie can also charge a beam like attack that fires a ranged blast up to five times before needing more orbs. SO HE IS A SAYIN WARRIOR TOO?
- Orbs and continues as part of the combat rhythm
Most defeated enemies drop green orbs that refill both life and beam shots once you hit 30, but kills with beam attacks do not drop orbs. So there is a constant small decision: use your ranged beam now and lose a potential refill, or grind it out with fists to build your stocks.

- Game over acorn
 The Game Over screen shows Jackie struggling to hold up a giant acorn labeled "Game Over". If you burn all continues, you see a longer version where he fails and gets squashed under it, then get booted back to the title screen. game-over-dex.fandom.com
- 6. Difficulty, Pacing & Fail States
- Checkpoints and flow
 Stages are broken into segments with respawn points, so you are rarely kicked too far back. Combined with the continue system and bonus game padding, it feels tuned for kids to eventually see the ending rather than to eat all their allowance.

- 7. Graphics & Sound
- NES vs TurboGrafx look
 The TurboGrafx-16 version has more detailed, slightly more realistic art and larger sprites, while the NES version feels a bit more chunky and toy like.
