# 438 - Isolated Warrior

- THEY’RE COMING TO YOUR TOWN

## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch: one soldier refuses to evacuate an alien-infested planet
  - The game takes place on the planet Pan
  - An unknown alien force attacks and starts consuming living things, machines, and buildings.
  - The people and soldiers of Pan are told to evacuate, but army captain Max Maverick refuses to leave.
  - He goes in alone, using a motorcycle and hovercraft as his only means of transportation.
- Developed by KID, published in North America by NTVIC
- The simplest way to frame it for listeners
  - This is a diagonal/isometric scrolling shooter with light platforming.
  - It looks vaguely like Zaxxon, plays more like an autoscrolling run-and-gun, and occasionally asks you to jump over pits, walls, bullets, and environmental hazards.
  - The result is a game that feels technically impressive, slightly awkward, and genuinely distinctive.

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- The isometric perspective is the whole identity
  - Most NES shooters scroll vertically, horizontally, or from a straight overhead view.
  - Isolated Warrior scrolls diagonally, giving the action a pseudo-3D look.
  - That makes it visually memorable right away, but it also changes how aiming, dodging, and jumping feel.
- It mixes shooter design with platforming
  - Max can jump, hold the jump button for a somersault, and fire bombs while flipping.
  - The game is not just asking you to shoot enemies. It wants you to read terrain, avoid damage zones, clear gaps, and survive forced-scrolling hazards. (World of Nintendo)
  - KID also developed Burai Fighter, another NES shooter that tries to do more with movement and screen direction.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Controls
  - D-pad moves Max in eight directions.
  - B fires a laser beam.
  - A jumps.
  - Holding A performs a jump and somersault.
  - Select changes shooting mode.
  - On the motorcycle, pressing down performs a wheelie, which can help avoid some collisions but still leaves Max vulnerable to bullets. (World of Nintendo)
- Weapon modes
  - Max has two laser styles:
    - Spreading beam: better for wide enemy groups.
    - Focusing beam: better for tougher enemies and enemies behind you.
  - Both can be upgraded across five levels.
  - The spreading beam expands into wider shot patterns, while the focusing beam eventually fires forward and backward. (World of Nintendo)
- Bomb system
  - Bombs also have power levels.
  - Level 1 fires in one direction.
  - Level 2 fires in three directions.
  - Level 3 fires in five directions.
  - Level 4 fires in eight directions.
  - Level 5 is a one-use boosted state that drops back to Level 4 afterward. (World of Nintendo)
- Stage progression
  - The game has six regular stages.
  - After finishing each stage, the game gives you a password.
  - If you finish all six stages before the game is over, the manual says you can enter a special Stage 7 for highly skilled players. (World of Nintendo)

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- Jumping in a shooter
  - This is the mechanical twist that makes Isolated Warrior more than just “Zaxxon on foot.”
  - You jump over bullets, terrain hazards, pits, walls, and sometimes enemies.
  - In theory, this gives the game more depth. In practice, it also creates a lot of the game’s frustration because the perspective makes depth hard to judge.
- The motorcycle stage
  - Stage 4
  - It moves fast, throws in gaps, and gives Max a wheelie command that can accidentally interfere with jumping.
  - holding down can trigger a wheelie when you really need to jump over a gap.
- The hovercraft stage
  - The hovercraft section increases the speed and gives the game a different feel without totally changing the controls.
  - It is a smart way to make the same basic mechanics feel more dangerous.
  - The downside is that faster scrolling makes power-up collection and hazard reading harder. (Hardcore Gaming 101)
- Power-up economy
  - The game has upgrades for spreading shots, focusing shots, bombs, bomb count, speed, health recovery, bonus points, and barriers.
  - The barrier protects Max for five hits, then starts blinking before disappearing.

## VI. Sequels and Spinoffs

- No direct sequel
  - Isolated Warrior appears to be a one-off NES release rather than the start of a franchise.
  - That is a little surprising because the concept feels sequel-ready: cleaner diagonal movement, better depth readability, bigger bosses, and more vehicle stages could have made a follow-up really interesting.
- Closest comparison points
  - Contra / Super C: not the same perspective, but relevant when talking about action-shooter pacing and power-up pressure.
  - Snake Rattle ’n’ Roll: useful only as an NES comparison for “isometric perspective that looks cool but can make movement weird.”

## VII. Essential Games List Vote

- Case for Essential
  - It is one of the most visually distinct shooters on the NES.
  - It mixes shooting, jumping, vehicles, power-ups, passwords, and a hidden final challenge.
- Case against Essential
  - The isometric angle can make basic actions feel less precise than they should.
  - Hit detection and boss aiming can feel fiddly.
