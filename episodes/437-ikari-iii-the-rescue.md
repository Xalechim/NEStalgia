# 437 - Ikari III: The Rescue

- IT IS THE END OF THE 20th CENTURY
- Drop the “Warriors” its cleaner

## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch: the Ikari Warriors become hand-to-hand rescue commandos
  - The president’s daughter, Elise, has been kidnapped by a secret criminal organization called Crime Ghost.
  - Crime Ghost’s leader, Faust, threatens to execute her unless the president resigns.
  - The president calls in Paul and Vince, the Ikari Warriors, to infiltrate the enemy hideout, rescue Elise, and destroy Crime Ghost. (Internet Archive)
- Developed and published by SNK
  - It is the third NES entry after Ikari Warriors and Victory Road.
- The simplest way to frame it for listeners
  - The first Ikari Warriors is basically a top-down military shooter.
  - Victory Road turns that into a bizarre sci-fi sequel.
  - Ikari III yanks the series back to Earth, but instead of doubling down on guns and tanks, it becomes a weird top-down martial arts rescue mission.
  - It is less “Rambo with a machine gun” and more “Rambo forgot most of his weapons and decided to roundhouse kick a terrorist organization.”

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- It is a huge mechanical pivot for the series
  - The main action is no longer built around constant gunfire.
  - You spend most of the game punching, kicking, jump kicking, and using occasional weapons or items.
  - That makes it feel closer to a top-down beat ’em up than a traditional run-and-gun shooter.
- It supports two-player simultaneous action
  - Like the earlier Ikari games, the co-op angle is a major part of the appeal.
- The premise is hilariously action-movie generic
  - There is a global recession.
  - A secret vice organization is infiltrating every country.
  - The president’s daughter is kidnapped.
  - The villain gives a resignation ultimatum.
  - Two muscle-bound commandos are the only answer.
  - It is extremely late-80s action nonsense, and honestly that is part of the charm.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Top-down scrolling combat
  - Unlike the first Ikari Warriors, you are not relying on tanks or vehicles. This is mostly a body-to-body brawl with occasional weapons.
- Basic controls
  - The D-pad moves the hero.
  - A Button: roundhouse kick.
  - B Button: uses picked-up items.
  - A + B: jump kick.
  - A + B + D-pad: roundhouse kick while jump kicking.
  - Start pauses, and Select can show score and remaining lives. (Internet Archive)
- Combat loop
  - Walk into enemy groups.
  - Use punches, kicks, and jump kicks to clear space.
  - Grab items when they appear.
  - Use limited weapons carefully.
  - Push forward before the game overwhelms you with bodies.
- Weapons and items
  - Machine gun: only 10 bullets, so it is a limited-use power tool rather than your default attack.
  - Grenade: strong destructive power, but also limited.
  - Diamond: bonus points.
  - Oil Drum: wipes out all enemies on screen.
  - Rock?: can be thrown at enemies.
- Limited weapon pickups
  - The machine gun having only 10 bullets
  - The game is not letting you retreat into the comfort zone of the older Ikari formula.

## V. Arcade Game

- Arcade Version / Differences From the NES Version
- The arcade version came first, and it is the harsher version of the idea
  - The arcade Ikari III: The Rescue was released by SNK in 1989 and later reissued through Arcade Archives.
- The story is noticeably different
  - In the arcade version, Ralf and Clark are rescuing a presidential candidate’s son from a terrorist base.
  - In the NES version, the plot is rewritten around Paul and Vince rescuing the president’s daughter Elise from Crime Ghost, led by Faust.
- The arcade controls are the biggest mechanical difference
  - The arcade cabinet used an 8-way rotary joystick with a 12-position rotary switch and three buttons: punch, kick, and jump.
- The arcade version has a nasty pursuit / timer system
  - One of the arcade version’s defining frustrations is the “main terrorist forces are catching up” pressure.
  - A distance-style timer, shown as a distance to a skull. If it reaches zero, you are captured and sent back to the beginning of the level.
  - The NES version removes that pressure, which makes it a lot less punishing and a lot more reasonable to actually play through.

## VI. Sequels and Spinoffs

- No Ikari IV
  - Ikari III is effectively the last main entry in the original Ikari Warriors line.
  - There was no NES follow-up and no direct fourth game continuing this specific format.
- Ralf and Clark continue beyond Ikari
  - Even though the NES manual calls the heroes Paul and Vince, the broader SNK identity of the Ikari Warriors becomes strongly tied to Ralf Jones and Clark Still.
  - Those characters later become much more famous through SNK’s larger universe, especially The King of Fighters and Metal Slug appearances.
  - So the Ikari brand does not disappear, but it stops being its own mainline action series.
- Related comparison games
  - Guerrilla War: probably the cleanest NES comparison if you want an SNK-made top-down action game that feels more traditional and satisfying.
  - P.O.W.: Prisoners of War: useful comparison for SNK’s interest in martial-arts/military brawling.

## VII. Essential Games List Vote

- Case for Essential
  - It is one of the most interesting late-series pivots on the NES.
- Case against Essential
  - but the punching and kicking need to feel great for this design to work, and they do not get there.
  - It lacks the iconic status of the first game and the bizarre novelty of Victory Road.
- Recommended vote
  - Not Essential.
  - The reinvention is more interesting as a topic than as a game you would tell every NES fan they need to play.
