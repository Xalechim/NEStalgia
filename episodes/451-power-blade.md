# 451 - Power Blade

- Opening hook
- 22nd Century Warrior

## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch: 22nd century muscle guy with a boomerang
  - The year is 2191. New Earth runs on a Master Computer. Aliens get into it and the whole society goes sideways.
  - You are Arnold Schwarzenegger Nova. You go into six sectors, find a contact, take his ID card, unlock the security room, kill the guard, take the tape.
  - Six tapes gets you into the Control Center for the finale.
- Developed by Natsume, published by Taito
  - Natsume is the same studio behind Shadow of the Ninja and, later, Harvest Moon.
- The identity problem, and it is a big one
  - In Japan this came out on April 20, 1990 as Power Blazer, and it was a much different game.
  - America did not get a translation. America got a rebuild. New levels, new objectives, new hero, tightened controls.
- The simplest way to frame it for listeners
  - Structurally it is Mega Man: pick your stage off a grid, beat seven bosses, fight the final one.
  - Texturally it is a 1980s action movie. The hero is a buzz-cut slab in sunglasses who throws a boomerang.

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- Eight-directional attack
  - The boomerang goes in any of eight directions, including diagonals and straight up.
- The levels are not corridors
  - You climb, drop, backtrack, and route through ladders. Sectors go up and down as much as left to right.
  - Most stages hide branching paths and side rooms, and most of those rooms hold power-ups.
- The contact hunt is the real objective
  - Reaching the boss door is not enough. If you have not found the agent and taken his ID card, the door will not open.
  - This is the single design idea that separates Power Blade from every Mega Man clone of the era, and it is also the one that will make a first-time player wander in circles.
- The Power Blade suit
  - The helmet pickup wraps Nova in armor, upgrades his attack to energy waves that pass through walls, and gives him three extra hits before it breaks.
  - It is a power-up that is also a health bar
- High Score Board
  - The six contacts you are hunting through alien-infested sectors of a rogue supercomputer are named John Gordon, Robert Williams, David Strader, Lynda Baker, Martin Douglas, and Daniel Leeds.
  - No codenames. No ranks. Six guys from a homeowners association, apparently the last line of defense for the human race in the year 2191.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Seven stages, six of them in any order
  - Six sectors on a select screen, then the Control Center. The stage select even marks which sectors you have already disarmed. (HG101)
  - Each sector: find the contact, take the ID card, find the security door, beat the guard, take the tape.
- Basic controls
  - B: throw the boomerang. Hold the D-pad to aim it in eight directions.
  - A: jump.
  - Select: use the currently equipped item.
  - Start: pause, check sector progress, and swap between grenade and health item. (GameFAQs)
- The item set is small and readable
  - Boomerangs change color as they get stronger, and a separate pickup lets you have several in the air at once.
  - A star extends throw range. Grenades hit everything on screen. The health item refills the meter.
  - The Power Blade helmet is the one you actually go out of your way for.
- There is a time limit, and it rarely matters
  - On normal difficulty you will not run into it. On Expert it can bite.

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- The difficulty problem, said honestly
  - HG101's verdict is that the redesign from Power Blazer fixed everything and in doing so made the game too easy. Nova is fast, the boomerang cuts through enemies, and the bosses are pushovers.
  - Expert mode was added to compensate and barely moves the needle.
- You can skip four of the seven bosses with a bug
  - On stages 1, 2, 4, and 5, hitting Select to detonate a grenade as the boss door closes behind you kills the boss before the fight screen even loads.
  - It does not work on 3, 6, or 7, which suggests those bosses load differently rather than that anyone caught it.
- Missing the ID costs you real time
  - Get to the door without the ID and you have to walk the whole sector back. In the bigger sectors that is a genuine punishment for exploring wrong.
- The plot argues with itself
  - The manual framing says restore the Master Computer. The in-game intro tells you to destroy it. The ending has humanity vowing never to hand that much power to a machine again.
  - Nobody appears to have reconciled these, and the ending is the more interesting reading.

## V. The Redesign: Power Blazer to Power Blade

- What Power Blazer actually was
  - A stumpy Mega Man-style robot with a boomerang, on a stage select screen, in seven straightforward left-to-right levels.
  - The boomerang ran off a power meter that drained with every throw and took a second or two to refill, so you could not spam it. Good idea, except the enemies crowd you and take multiple hits.
  - Slow walk, weak jump, pixel-perfect platforming deaths.
  - Famitsu gave it 23 out of 40.
- What changed for America
  - Every level redesigned from scratch, now with branching paths and the contact objective.
  - Faster movement, longer jumps, faster meter refill, boomerangs that cut through enemies by default, multi-boomerang pickups, eight-directional throwing, the power suit.
  - New intro cinema, new music for intro and ending, an enemy health bar on bosses, less cartoony cutscenes. Most enemies, most bosses, and nearly all the music carried over unchanged.
- Who did it, with the caveat attached
  - Per Twentieth Century Gamer, the push came from Randy Studdard, a former Nintendo of America staffer then working at Taito's US branch.
  - Studdard is independently documented as the author of the Captain Nintendo short story that ran in Nintendo Power in 1988 and 1989 and became the seed for Captain N: The Game Master.
- It nearly did not happen
  - HG101 reports that the Japanese side was annoyed enough at Americans rebuilding their game that it came close to being cancelled.
  - It shipped anyway, got a Nintendo Power cover, and sold.
- The working title was Power Mission
  - It was shown under that name at the Summer 1990 CES in Chicago with different character sprites, and Nintendo Power previewed it as Mega Man II style action.

## VI. Music

- Kinuyo Yamashita wrote it, and she is not a small name
  - Her debut was the original Castlevania in 1986, where Konami credited her and Satoe Terashima jointly under the fake name James Banana, a pun on James Bernard, who scored Hammer's 1958 Dracula.
  - She left Konami in 1989 and went freelance. Power Blazer and Power Blade are early freelance work. She later scored Mega Man X3.
- The soundtrack survived the rebuild intact
  - Almost all of it came straight over from Power Blazer. HG101 rates it as rivaling Konami's better NES scores, which given who wrote it is not a coincidence.
  - If you want one track to play on the episode, Sector 5 and Sector 6 are the fan picks.

## VII. Pop Culture

- The Schwarzenegger problem
  - The title screen portrait of Nova is a near-copy of Terminator-era promotional images of Arnold Schwarzenegger.
  - Box artist Mike Winterbauer says he was contacted by a certain movie star's lawyers over the cover painting.
  - His defense was that the reference photo was of himself, standing under fluorescent lights holding a T-square, which became the boomerang in the painting. He sent them the photo and never heard back.
  - Worth sitting with: the most Schwarzenegger-looking man on any NES box is a graphic designer holding a drafting tool.
- 1991 and the Americanized cover hero
  - This is the same era that turned Dragon Ball into Dragon Power and Gegege no Kitaro into Ninja Kid. Anime-styled art was considered unsellable in American toy aisles.
  - Power Blade is the extreme version of that instinct. Most publishers changed the art. Taito USA changed the game.

## VIII. Sequels, Spinoffs, and Comparison Games

- Power Blade 2, 1992
  - Known in Japan as Captain Saver. Nova returns in 2200 against a cyborg army from the Delta Foundation.
  - Adds swappable suits that let him walk on ceilings, swim, and fly, plus a slide move. The structure drifts from Mega Man toward Metroid.
  - Kinuyo Yamashita scored that one too.
- Where the game lives now
  - It also ran on the PlayChoice-10 arcade cabinet, which is an odd place for it to turn up.
  - The 2009 IGN Top 100 NES Games list put it at number 82, which is roughly where its reputation still sits: respected, not canonized.

## IX. Essential Games List Vote

- Case for Essential
  - It is the best evidence on the console that localization could be authorship, not damage, and it happens to also be a very good game.
  - Supporting: the eight-directional boomerang
- Case against Essential
  - If the point of an essentials list is the games that defined what the NES felt like, Mega Man 2 already occupies this exact chair.
