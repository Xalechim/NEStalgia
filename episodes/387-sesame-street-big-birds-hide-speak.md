# 387 - Sesame Street Big Bird's Hide & Speak

- Released in 1990 by Hi-Tech Expressions, developed by Riedel Software Productions.
- Officially licensed by Children’s Television Workshop.
- feature digitized speech — and it's Big Bird's! Even says Starring Caroll Spinney
- Aimed squarely at preschoolers, it’s part edutainment, part memory game.
- 👤 Story & Objective
- You play as a friend of Big Bird, helping him find Elmo and friends (like Ernie, The Count, and Grover) hiding behind windows.
- There’s no real "story," just a friendly set of increasingly tricky mini-games designed to teach basic word recognition, memory, and listening skills.
- You guys probably won’t admit you’re wrong, and that’s fine, but this game proves my point about the dpad menu in the other Sesame Street games and.
- 🎮 Gameplay Mechanics
- Controlled entirely with the D-Pad and A button — no reading required.
- The game has six modes:
  - Find the character Big Bird names.
  - Find the character by memorizing where they were
  - Find the letter Big Bird names
  - Find the letter by memorizing where they were
  - Find the word Big Bird names
  - Find the word by memorizing where they were
- Players select windows to reveal characters or words.
- After completing each mode one of the characters goes outside and does something. Elmo blows gum that lifts him in the air, Bert plays jump rope, and Ernie paces back and forth
- 🎨 Graphics & Sound
- Bold, colorful characters faithful to the Sesame Street aesthetic.
- Sprites are large, friendly, and recognizable — perfect for young kids.
- Does the looping music avoid distraction or overstimulation.

- Big Bird’s Hide & Speak vs. Donkey Kong Jr. Math
- 🎮 Gameplay Design
- Big Bird: Very simple — press a window, hear a word, see a face. Six gradually more complex variations of that idea.
- DK Jr. Math: Based on Donkey Kong Jr.’s platforming engine, but math slows the game down. Only two game modes, both clunky.
- ✅ Winner: Big Bird — It may be basic, but it’s built for preschoolers. DK Jr. Math is frustrating for everyone.
- 🕰️ Replay Value
- Big Bird: Low, unless you’re a literal toddler. But for that group, it’s perfect.
- DK Jr. Math: Also low, because it’s clunky and not particularly fun or rewarding.
- 🟥 Loser: Everyone — but Big Bird at least knows it’s a babysitter, not an arcade hit.
