# 464 - Battletoads

- "BATTLETOADS get real MAD, and then they get EVEN!"

&nbsp;

## I. Setup & Premise

- What it is based on: nothing. It is an original Rare creation, and it is the studio's answer to the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles craze.
  - The three heroes are Rash, Zitz and Pimple, anthropomorphic toads named after skin conditions. Rash is Player 1 and Zitz is Player 2. (Wikipedia, NES manual)
  - So this is a game about three mutant amphibians with attitude who fight in a beat-em-up, in the same year Turtles were on every lunchbox. 
- Who made it
  - Rare developed it. The designers are Tim Stamper, Chris Stamper and Gregg Mayles, Kevin Bayliss was the artist, Mark Betteridge was the programmer and David Wise wrote the music. (Wikipedia)
  - Tradewest published it in North America in June 1991. It reached Japan on the Famicom on December 20, 1991 from Masaya, and Europe on February 18, 1993. (Wikipedia)
  - Tradewest is the same company that brought Double Dragon to the NES in North America, which matters later. (Wikipedia)
- The story, straight from the manual
  - After the Dark Queen loses a war against the Galactic Corporation at the battle of Canis Major, she hides out in the dark spaces between the stars.
  - On the spaceship Vulture, Professor T. Bird and the toads are escorting Princess Angelica home to her father, the Terran Emperor. Pimple, the biggest of the toads, takes Angelica out for a cruise in the Toadster.
  - The Dark Queen ambushes them. Pimple sends out a distress signal before the Toadster is swallowed and carried to Ragnarok's World, her planet.
  - The box asks if you will cry, hide or call the Starcops. No. Battletoads do not call for help.
- The pitch
  - A side-scrolling brawler for one or two players that never stays the same game for long. Twelve levels, and in between the fighting you get a speeder bike, a surfboard, a jet, a rappel down a pit, snake platforms, an underwater maze and unicycles.

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- Every level is a different game
  - Fight in a canyon, then descend a crater on a cable, then ride the Turbo Tunnel, then climb in an ice cave, then surf a river. Rare added racing and climbing on purpose to separate it from other beat-em-ups.
  - The cost is that you keep learning new rules, and the game does not ramp up gradually. The difficulty comes from abrupt changes in mechanics.
- Smash Hits
  - Most attacks have a flashier version that triggers after a series of hits, as a finisher or sometimes by default. A toad's fist swells up, a boot grows to giant size, and the head becomes a pair of horns.
  - The manual names eight: Turbo Thwack, Swingin' Size Thirteens, Jawbuster, Kiss-My-Fist, Battletoad Butt, BT Bashing Ball, Nuclear Knuckles and Big Bad Boot.
- Improvised weapons
  - You can grab the leg of a Walker, a raven's beak, a pipe or a flagpole in the Dark Queen's tower and use it. You can also pick enemies up and throw them. (Wikipedia)
- Two players at once
  - Rash and Zitz play together. It is a real co-op beat-em-up, and Nintendo Power gave it the 1991 award for Best Multi-Player. It also has friendly fire, and you can kill your partner by accident, which we will get to.
- Humor and personality
  - The manual is written in full toad slang, with enemy entries like Hornhead, "voted ugliest in his class," and Steel-Beak, a duck no toad wants in the bath. Professor T. Bird is a sarcastic vulture who mocks you when you continue.
- A look that stands out on the NES
  - Isometric-style side-scrolling stages, multi-layer scrolling and cartoon cutscenes. Reviewers in 1991 praised the graphics, the cutscenes and the humor. (Wikipedia)
- Mega warps
  - Four levels hide warp points that skip you ahead two levels. One is in Level 4, where you jump repeatedly on a falling platform near the bottom, and a warp appears at the top that takes you to Level 6.
- High Score Board
  - The two-player bug. In Level 11, Clinger-Winger, Player 2 cannot move properly on the original American cartridge, so two players cannot finish the stage, and it sat in the game for about 24 years. The second player can still join in at the next stage (which also happens to be the last). I wouldn't recommend it, though - The last level is two-player hell and you are going to need every credit you have managed to save.
  - The Japanese and European versions do not have it. Rare fixed it in the 2015 Xbox One collection Rare Replay, and studio head Craig Duncan said the team talked about whether fixing it would cost the game some authenticity before deciding to fix it anyway.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- The core loop
  - Fight through twelve levels to reach the Tower of Shadows and rescue Pimple and Princess Angelica. You win by beating every baddie in a level and avoiding its obstacles.
  - You have six hit points and eat flies to restore them. You start with three lives, and continuing restores your lives. There are no passwords and no saves, so you play it in one sitting. 
  - There are three continues, and when they are gone you are back at the title screen. In two-player, if one toad runs out of lives both of you restart the stage.
  - The scoreboard shows lives, energy and points, and the manual dares you to earn a 1UP.
- The twelve levels, in order
  - 1. Ragnarok Canyon: on foot against Psyko-Pigs, Walkers and rats, ending in the Tall Walker.
  - 2. Wookie Hole: a downward descent on a Turbo Cable, with ravens that try to cut it.
  - 3. Turbo Tunnel: the Speed Bike.
  - 4. Arctic Cavern: ice, snowballs, falling ice spikes and the evil Snowman.
  - 5. Surf City: the Space Board down a river of logs, mines and whirlpools, with Big Blag as the boss.
  - 6. Karnath's Lair: climbing and hopping on giant snakes.
  - 7. Volkmire's Inferno: the Jet Turbo through fire, meteorites and missiles.
  - 8. Intruder Excluder: a vertical climb with Robo-Manus at the top.
  - 9. Terra Tubes: the one swimming level, with eels, sharks and spikes.
  - 10. Rat Race: a fall down a tower where you have to defuse three bombs before the rat Giblet gets to them, then General Slaughter.
  - 11. Clinger-Winger: unicycles, and a chase from the boulder-sized Buzzball.
  - 12. The Revolution: the Dark Queen's tower, then the Dark Queen herself.
- Controls
  - D-pad: walk. Tap left or right twice to run. Down crouches in the vertical 2D levels.
  - A: jump.
  - B: attack, called "Action" in the manual. What B does changes with the level, so it does not do the same thing in every stage. Hit enemies several times and you get the Smash Hit, such as Kiss-My-Fist.
  - Run, then press B: headbutt, and its finisher is the Battletoad Butt.
  - Select: no function, according to the manual.
  - Start: starts the game and pauses it. For two players, both players press Start.
- Enemies
  - Psyko-Pigs with battle axes, the Scuzz, which attack in groups from behind, Rat Rockets, Saturn Toadtraps, the Giblet rat, and ravens. The manual says the Battletoad Butt sends the Scuzz on their way.

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- The Turbo Tunnel
  - Level 3 is the most famous stage in the game. On a Speed Bike you dodge stone walls and use ramps to cross long gaps, and the walls come fast enough that you need to memorize them. A 1991 consumer guide called it one of the toughest challenges of any NES game.
  - Low walls fill the lane and must be jumped, floating low walls must be passed under, and trampolines get you across gaps. In the last section the warning flashes go away and the walls alternate left and right at short intervals. (StrategyWiki)
  - It is not even the hardest level. Volkmire's Inferno the real spike, with fast sections that you cannot get through the first time without memorizing them.
  - There is also a warp hidden in the last bike section. If you slam headfirst into the tenth wall, on the bottom of the island, you skip ahead.
- Wookie Hole
  - You hang from a cable and go down an impact crater, and the ravens can cut it. Hanging from the cable gives you new moves, including a kick.
- Surf City, the Space Board
  - You are on a board in a river with logs, mines and whirlpools coming at you. Fans tend to treat it as the easier stretch after Turbo Tunnel and the Arctic Cavern.
- The wrecking ball
  - The BT Bashing Ball, where the toad turns into a wrecking ball next to a wall, is one of the more unusual moves. The manual says it is the way to short-circuit the Retro-Blasters and uproot the Saturn Toadtraps.
- Terra Tubes
  - The one swimming stage. Full of one-hit kills, including cogs, sharks, spikes and rubber ducks.
- Rat Race
  - You race down a tower against Giblet to reach three bombs before he does. It is the closest the game gets to a timed puzzle.
- Clinger-Winger
  - Unicycles in a maze chased by Buzzball, a huge ball of static. The manual says the only way to shake it is to out-leap it to the finish.
- Professor T. Bird
  - Every time you use a continue the sarcastic vulture mocks you.
- The two-player trouble
  - Friendly fire is on, so the second player can hurt you, and one toad's death restarts the stage for both. Den of Geek calls two-player nearly unplayable, and Yahoo! Games singled out accidental partner kills.

## V. History and Development

- Where the toads came from
  - Wikipedia says the Stampers made the series in response to the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles craze, and that Gregg Mayles described it as a typical case of Rare taking a popular trend and adding its own twist.
  - Kevin Bayliss tells a slightly different story. He says Rare turned to original games because licensed work was too limiting, and when asked for ideas he suggested toads. The shared pond with the turtles was not the main reason. (Popgeeks, Rare Revealed)
  - The working title was Amphibianz.
- The tone
  - Bayliss first designed it as a Disney-style game. As it got more violent he toned it down and limited weapons. The cartoon look was also a way to follow Nintendo's rules on violence.
  - He said the characters were designed to sell merchandise on a large scale, as Tim Burton's Batman had.
- The difficulty was on purpose
  - The team made the game very hard to get more life out of it, and Mark Betteridge reportedly got frustrated with levels he had built himself. They believed it could be finished with practice and skill. 
- Release
  - It was shown at CES in January 1991 and came out that June. The Famicom version has changes that make it easier. And we're not talking about small things like more lives. Redesigning a ton of levels, yes the Turbo Tunnel. The Genesis version is based off this.
- The ports
  - Arc System Works did the 1993 Genesis and Game Gear versions, The Game Gear version cut three levels and has no two-player mode.
  - Mindscape did the Amiga and CD32 versions, which were finished in 1992 but sat unreleased until 1994.
  - Virgin planned a Master System version, Mindscape planned MS-DOS and Atari ST versions and Telegames planned an Atari Lynx version. None came out.
  - Rare made a Game Boy port, Battletoads in Ragnarok's World, in 1993, with several levels missing and no two-player mode. (NOTE: There is a Game Boy game just called Battletoads and that is a completely different game)

## VI. Music and Presentation

- Graphics
  - The characters are bright, cartoony and big, and Rare used the isometric style in the side-scrolling stages to give depth. Vertical levels are flat 2D. Reviewers also praised the cartoon cutscenes. (Wikipedia)
  - Contemporary critics complained about small sprites, flicker, bland backgrounds, attacks that look weak and poor collision detection in platforming. (Wikipedia)
  - Retronauts says it is one of the best looking NES games for its color, sound and polish.
- Music
  - David Wise wrote the NES soundtrack. He later did Donkey Kong Country, which Rare released for the Super NES in 1994 and which sold over nine million copies. (Wikipedia, Kill Screen)
  - His style uses the game's own sound effects as part of the rhythm. The track Pause is built on the sound of a punch, and the first-level track, Ragnarok Canyon, opens with a heavy beat and then a fast melody over layered combat-sound percussion. (Kill Screen)
  - The cutscene track Dark Calling has a slow, steady beat and a menacing synth.
  - In 2016 iam8bit released the score on a double vinyl set in green and yellow "toad skin" colors, with Game Over as the last track, a nod to how often players heard it. (Kill Screen)

## VII. Pop Culture

- The Turtles
  - The Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles film opened March 30, 1990 and took over 25 million dollars on opening weekend, then the biggest independent opening ever. It made over 135 million dollars in North America. Pizza Hut ran a 20 million dollar campaign. (Wikipedia)
  - The Secret of the Ooze followed in March 1991, with a 20 million dollar opening and about 78 million dollars in total. (The Numbers)
  - Konami's Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles II: The Arcade Game hit the NES in December 1990 and sold over a million copies by 1991. Battletoads arrived six months later as the new mutant brawler. (Wikipedia)
- Double Dragon
  - Technos' 1987 arcade hit came to the NES in 1988, published by Tradewest in North America. Tradewest later merged its two brands into one game. (Wikipedia)
- Other 1991 happenings
  - The mutant-hero craze was not only Rare's. Toxic Crusaders, a cartoon based on The Toxic Avenger, arrived in 1991, and Biker Mice from Mars followed in September 1993.
- The internet and Battletoads
  - Pawn Stars prank calls: 4chan users flooded the Gold and Silver pawn shop's phone line asking whether it sold Battletoads, and Rick Harrison shouting at callers went viral. Rare Replay has an achievement called "Do You Have Battletoads?" as a joke on that.
  - A later GameStop prank asked about pre-orders for a nonexistent Xbox reboot.
- The cartoon
  - DIC made a half-hour pilot for Thanksgiving weekend 1992, a prequel where three junior high students from Oxnard, California become toads to protect Professor T. Bird and Princess Angelica from the Dark Queen. It was never picked up as a series. Buena Vista put it on VHS in January 1994.
- Cameos
  - Battletoads appear as a bonus boss in the Xbox One and PC versions of Shovel Knight. Rash is a guest fighter in Killer Instinct season 3, and shows up as a toy in Grounded.

## VIII. Sequels, Spinoffs and Comparison Games

- The series, in order
  - Battletoads (Game Boy, November 1991, Rare)
    - A different game with the same title and box art. Only Zitz plays, in eight levels such as a chasm rappel, a jet ski, a speeder bike, a boulder chase and a 99 second jet pack flight, ending against Robo-Manus. It was never ported. (Wikipedia)
  - Battletoads (Tiger Electronics LCD game, 1991)
    - A handheld LCD version from Tiger Electronics.
  - Battletoads in Ragnarok's World (Game Boy, 1993)
    - A stripped-down port of the NES original.
  - Battletoads in Battlemaniacs (Super NES, June 1993, Rare, Tradewest)
    - Each toad has its own combos and special moves, health bars are longer, and the six stages remix levels from the NES game.
    - It adds bonus stages for extra lives. The story moves into a virtual reality system, Psicone's T.R.I.P.S. Silas Volkmire is the villain, and the ending depends on whether you land three missiles. Its Turbo Tunnel Rematch revisits the bike level. (Wikipedia)
    - A Master System port was made in 1994, pulled in Europe at the last minute and released in Brazil in 1996.
  - Battletoads/Double Dragon (NES, June 1993, with Genesis, Super NES and Game Boy versions, Rare, Tradewest)
    - Billy and Jimmy Lee join the toads against the Dark Queen and the Shadow Warriors. Five characters are playable, the first time all three toads can be chosen.
    - It adds hanging from platform edges and kicking from there, a shooter level, and a choice of two-player modes where players can or cannot hit each other. The difficulty is more forgiving than the original. IGN ranked it 76th on its NES list.
  - Battletoads Arcade, also called Super Battletoads (1994, Rare, Electronic Arts, arcade)
    - A separate arcade game and not a port of the NES one. It added three-player co-op and voice-overs, and it was Rare's first game to use PowerAnimator 3D. Gore could be turned off using DIP switches.
    - It sold poorly, and Rare canceled finished Game Boy and planned Super NES ports. Retro Gamer critics later called it the best in the series, and it first reached consoles in Rare Replay in 2015.
  - Battletoads (2020, Dlala Studios with Rare, Xbox Game Studios, August 20, 2020, Windows and Xbox One)
    - Hand-drawn art and up to three players. It includes a bike course based on the Turbo Tunnel, now seen from behind the bike, and it has selectable difficulty levels.
    - The toads wake after 26 years in a simulator bunker and team up with the Dark Queen against the Topians. It was revealed at E3 2018. Metacritic was 66 on PC and 72 on Xbox One, and Game Informer gave it 5 out of 10.

## IX. Essential Games List

- Case for Essential
  - It is one of the most inventive NES games, with no two levels alike and a trick up its sleeve in each. It is a rare NES game that is both a technical show-off and a joke.
  - It sold about 1.25 million copies in the US by September 1992, got to number two on the Nintendo Power chart and was a big enough name that Rare brought it back in 2015 and 2020.
  - It is a real two-player game, and Rare Replay and Nintendo Classics make it easy to play now.
- Case against Essential
  - It is cruel, and that is the main thing people say about it. GameTrailers ranked it the hardest game ever made, and with only three continues a mistake in the late levels sends you back to the title screen.
  - The platforming collision is rough, the sprites are small and flickering, and in two-player you can kill each other.
