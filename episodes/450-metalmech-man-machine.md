# 450 - MetalMech: Man & Machine

- Opening hook
- It can save mankind, but beware, this machine has a mind of its own

## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch: a stolen alien war machine and one guy in a jumpsuit
  - Aliens led by an Alien Queen have taken over Earth. The US Army gets hold of one of their walking machines.
  - You are Tony. You drive the mech through six levels and go kill the Queen.
  - That is the whole story. There is no mid-game plot, no characters, no cutscenes past the intro.
- Developed by Sculptured Software, published by Jaleco
  - Sculptured Software was a Salt Lake City studio, incorporated in 1985, later swallowed by Acclaim and eventually shut down in 2002.
  - Same studio that later did the Super Star Wars games and the SNES ports of Doom and Mortal Kombat. This is very early work from them.
- Japan got it first as Metal Flame Psybuster, December 14, 1990.
  - It was announced in the US as Mechanoids: Man & Machine before becoming MetalMech.

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- The eject button is the whole game
  - You start inside a three-legged mech that looks unmistakably like ED-209 from RoboCop.
  - At any point you can pop Tony out of the hatch and run around on foot.
  - The mech is tougher but it is also huge, cannot climb ladders, and cannot pick anything up.
- The one change from Blaster Master that ruins everything
  - In Blaster Master, Sophia sits safely while Jason is out walking around.
  - Here the mech keeps taking damage while you are outside it. So the tool you depend on is quietly rotting while you do the errand that requires leaving it.
  - Tony's health refills at the start of each level. The mech's does not. It just gets worse all game.
- The maps are enormous
  - One MetalMech level would be two or three levels in a better-paced game.
  - That sounds like a compliment until you remember you are looking for one key hidden inside one shootable object.
- Exactly one boss
  - Five levels have no boss at all. You find the key, you walk to the exit, that is it.
  - The Alien Queen at the end of level six is the only boss fight in the entire game.
- High Score Board
  - The game has essentially one line of dialogue, and it is Tony shouting "LET'S DO IT MECH!!!" at his own vehicle in the intro.
  - Better still: TCRF notes the Japanese intro cutscene has no text at all. That line appears to be an American addition. Somebody at Jaleco USA looked at a silent cartoon of a man climbing into a robot and decided what it needed was for him to yell that.

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Six levels, and they get weird fast
  - The City, The Towers, The Sewers, Atlantis, The Alien City, The Alien Queen's Mothership. (GameFAQs)
  - Yes, Atlantis. Level four of a robot game about aliens is the lost city of Atlantis, and it is never explained.
- The loop: find the key, find the exit
  - Every level except the last hides one key inside a piece of scenery you have to shoot: a newspaper box, a security camera, a piece of paper taped to a sewer wall, a pillar, a computer part.
  - Only Tony can actually pick the key up. The mech can crack the container open but cannot grab anything.
  - Level three even lampshades it with an in-game hint about the writing on the wall, which is as close as this game gets to a puzzle.
- Basic controls
  - D-pad: move, duck, climb, and aim the cannon, which swings from straight up around to a downward angle.
  - A: jump. B: fire, hold for auto-fire.
  - Select: hop back into the mech, or cycle special weapons.
  - Select plus A: quick eject. Select plus B: smart bomb.
  - Start: the cockpit menu. (GameFAQs)
- The cockpit menu is a strange little room
  - Three options: eject, view the credits, and turn the music off.
  - Eject is buried in a pause menu even though there is a perfectly good button shortcut for it.

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- The power-ups are a trap
  - Picking up a special weapon makes the mech absorb less damage and recover less health from pickups. The upgrade actively downgrades you.
  - Worth sitting on for a minute. This is a game where the correct strategy is to walk past most of the treasure.
- You can strand yourself from your own mech
  - Tony can only climb back in if he is standing on the same floor the mech is on.
  - Park badly and the mech is now a very large paperweight you have to finish the level without.
- Enemies respawn constantly, and there is no score
  - Kill something, walk five steps, it is back. Fighting is almost always the wrong choice.
  - There is no scoring system in the game at all, so there is not even a bragging-rights reason to shoot anything.
- Individual levels have one-off gimmicks nobody warns you about
  - The Towers: a machine on a bridge emits a signal that shuts the mech down completely until you eject and go destroy it.
  - The Sewers: an energy barrier that only drops when a rat walks through it.
  - Atlantis: you need a hidden waterproofing item before entering the water or you die instantly, plus teleporters that dump you in a random one of two areas.
- The last level is a different game
  - Level six drops the mech entirely. Tony gets a jet pack,  movement like section z, and a wave gun.

## V. History, Development, and the Two Versions

- A very small credited team
  - Designed by Joe Hitchens, programmed by Ken Grant, art by Lorin Nelson and Hitchens, music by Paul Webb.
  - Six credited people total on MobyGames. This was a small job, and it plays like one.

## VI. Music and Presentation

- Paul Webb wrote it on a sampler
  - Webb composed on an Ensoniq EPS keyboard and the results were converted into Ken Moore's NES sound driver.
  - He later worked on Super Star Wars, so this is early-career work from someone who got good.
- There is barely any music in the game
  - What is there is a few short rock tracks. The Video Game Music Preservation Foundation rates them as sounding genuinely good for 1990 and then notes there simply are not many of them.
  - Given that the pause menu includes a dedicated option to turn the music off, somebody involved had opinions about how often you would be hearing it.
- The visual identity is borrowed wholesale
  - The mech is an ED-209 (from Robocop) with the serial numbers filed off.
  - The one thing the game does have is variety in setting. City, tower interiors, sewers, undersea ruins, alien biology. It is not a boring-looking game.

## VII. Pop Culture

- The reverse-import story is the genuinely interesting part
  - This is the reverse. A Salt Lake City work-for-hire shop built a Famicom game that Japanese kids played before American kids did.

## VIII. Sequels, Spinoffs, and Comparison Games

- There is no sequel
- The rights went on a long journey anyway
  - Sculptured Software went to Acclaim, and the game's ownership eventually landed with Throwback Entertainment and then Piko Interactive.
  - It resurfaced in 2023 on the Evercade in Piko Interactive Collection 3 and on the Atari Gamestation Pro.

## IX. Essential Games List Vote

- Enormous maps, a single hidden key per level, respawning enemies, no score, one boss in six levels. There is very little to do and a lot of ground to cover doing it.
  - Supporting: if the point is to understand the man-and-machine idea, Blaster Master is right there and it is one of the best games on the system.
