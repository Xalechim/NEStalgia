# 414 - Shadow of the Ninja

- Use the arts of Ninjitsu to overthrow the emperor
- 1. General Overview
- What it is
 Shadow of the Ninja is a side-scrolling action game, part hack-and-slash, part platforming, built around quick stages. It is also notable for simultaneous 2-player co-op. Wikipedia+2Hardcore Gaming 101+2
- Release identity
 In Europe and Australia it was released as Blue Shadow. Wikipedia+2StrategyWiki+2
- 2. Story and Objective
- Setting
 The game is set in 2029, in a dystopian America ruled by Emperor Garuda, who has built a stronghold in New York City
- Heroes
 Two Iga clan ninjas, Hayate and Kaede, are sent to infiltrate Garuda’s fortress and assassinate him. Wikipedia+2Co-Optimus+2
- Offbeat questions
- Does “ninjas in future America” read cool cyber-ninja pulp like Ninja Crusaders or is this less futuristic?
- 3. Core Gameplay Loop
- Loop
  - Enter a short stage
  - Clear hazards and enemy patterns
  - Break containers for upgrades and consumables
  - Fight a boss or sub-boss
  - Repeat, with limited continues and lots of pressure to play clean Wikipedia+1
- Campaign structure
 The game spans five levels broken into fourteen stages (most levels are three stages, the last is shorter). Wikipedia+2KBHGames+2
- Co-op changes the loop
 Two players share the screen, share pressure, and share resources like continues, which changes decision-making constantly
- 4. Controls and Movement
- Baseline moveset
 Run, jump, crouch, climb ladders, and attack. A key signature move is the ability to hang from an overhang and move underneath it, which becomes a core platforming language in several stages. Wikipedia
- Feel and rhythm
 This is a game about committing to jumps and managing spacing. You are frequently asked to move forward while enemies pressure from multiple heights.
- 5. Weapons, Power Ups, and Resource Play
- Primary weapons
  - Katana is the default close-range weapon.
  - Kusarigama (chain and sickle) gives you reach and angled swings, but can be awkward if enemies are too close. StrategyWiki+1
- Weapon levels and durability vibe
 Picking up the same weapon upgrades it up to three levels, but taking enough damage can knock your weapon level back down. Wikipedia
  - Weapon leveling is a plan, not a bonus
 Experienced players route stages around upgrading the katana or chain to higher levels, because weapon level swings your ability to control crowds. Wikipedia
- Limited sub-weapons
 The game also uses limited-count throws like shuriken and grenades, found primarily by breaking containers. Wikipedia
  - Should a Ninja have a grenade?
- Ninjutsu panic button
 There is a powerful screen-clearing attack that summons a storm, but it costs a large chunk of your vitality and requires you to be above a threshold of health to use it. Wikipedia
- 6. Enemies, Bosses, and Stage Setpieces
- Enemy language
 You are fighting an authoritarian future army plus mechanical and cyber-ish threats. Patterns tend to be about forcing you to move and punishing hesitation.
- Boss cadence
 Many stages end in a boss or sub-boss, keeping the game feeling like a chain of “setpiece exams.” Wikipedia+1

- 7. Co-op and Character Choice
- Two playable characters
 Hayate and Kaede are selectable, and most references describe them as functionally similar in performance in the NES original. Wikipedia+1
- Shared resources
 In 2-player, continues are shared, and the game becomes a negotiation: who spends the grenade, who grabs the weapon upgrade, who takes the risky route. Wikipedia

- 10. Ports, Related Releases, and Spinoff Weirdness
- The Game Boy near-port that became something else
 A Game Boy version began as an adaptation of Shadow of the Ninja, but the project was reworked and released as Ninja Gaiden Shadow under Tecmo. Wikipedia+1
- Modern remake
 Shadow of the Ninja: Reborn released in 2024 as a full remake of the NES game. Wikipedia+2Steam Store+2
