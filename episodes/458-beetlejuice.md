# 458 - Beetlejuice

- A Rare-developed LJN license: you play the "ghost with the most" in a May 1991 NES game that mixes the 1988 film with the animated series. (Wikipedia)

## I. Setup & Premise

- The pitch: you are Beetlejuice, the "ghost with the most," and you scare the living Deetz family out of the Maitlands' house
  - Wikipedia's list of Beetlejuice games says it was developed by Rare and released for the NES in May 1991, published by LJN. It "blends elements of the film universe with elements of the animated series." (Wikipedia)
  - The game is loosely based on the 1988 film, but it looks more like the cartoon. (Wikipedia, Beetlejuice Wiki)
  - Early levels follow the film, with Beetlejuice moving through the Maitland house to find and scare away Otho. Later levels are original: a sewer, mythical creatures, the afterlife. (TASVideos, Beetlejuice Wiki)
  - Den of Geek notes the game uses the more villainous film version of the character. It ends with Beetlejuice promoting himself for future jobs. (Den of Geek)
- Region notes
  - The searches found only a US (NTSC) release. No PAL or Japanese release turned up, so do not claim one. (unverified)

## II. What Makes It Stand Out

- It is two games stitched together
  - Side-view platforming levels and top-down overhead levels. Side levels are timed jumping with the occasional attack. Overhead levels have a mild adventure element: find objects, give them to other people, open the route to the exit. (TASVideos)
- It is a licensed game with a strange economy
  - Vouchers are the currency, and you spend them on "scares," which are power-ups with a limited number of attacks. (Speed Demos Archive, TASVideos)
- It is hard, and reviewers say so
  - The TASVideos entry says it is "very hard to play normally." Retro reviewers complain you fall through what look like solid walls and die from jumping on enemies too often. (TASVideos, GoCollect)
  - The cutscenes and minigames are called the best part because they tie in film scenes and supporting characters. (GoCollect)
- The music
  - It is praised, even though it does not fit the film's tone. The score is not Danny Elfman's theme. (Wikipedia, GoCollect)
  - Discussion question: is a catchy but off-tone soundtrack a plus or a minus for a Beetlejuice game?

## III. Gameplay Structure

- Level count depends on who you ask
  - Speed Demos Archive describes six levels: Village and house, Storm Drains, two overhead levels, the Attic, and the Afterlife.
  - TASVideos splits it into eight stages: Town, Sewers, Basement, House, Attic, Graveyard, Attic Maze, Afterlife.
  - Wikipedia and the fan wiki list The Village, Storm Drains, the Maitlands' House (basement, kitchen, living rooms, attic, model graveyard), the Attic, and the Afterlife Waiting Room.
  - The sources are counting differently, so treat the number as unsettled.
- The finish
  - The final area is the Waiting Room to the Afterlife. You collect the numbers 1 to 6 (or five tickets, per Speed Demos Archive) and then exit. (TASVideos, Speed Demos Archive)
- Typical vouchers
  - Keys, books, and a fly in the village are worth 75 each. Beetles give 25 or more. A red beetle gives 10. (Speed Demos Archive)
  - You stomp the beetle for points and vouchers, and a beetle hole acts as the shop. (TASVideos, Speed Demos Archive)
- Potions
  - Overhead levels use thrown potions to defeat enemies like sweepers and footballers. The initial throw direction is random among four. (Speed Demos Archive)
- Difficulty and length
  - A competitive any% speedrun takes roughly 25 to 30 minutes by Speed Demos Archive. TAS runs are far faster, around 7 minutes. (Speed Demos Archive, TASVideos)

## IV. Unique Gameplay Elements

- The scare list (attack counts, Speed Demos Archive and TASVideos)
  - Medusa head: 1 attack.
  - Birdman: 2 attacks plus extra jump height. Cost about 150.
  - Two-headed man: 3 attacks.
  - Skeleton: 5 attacks and immunity to bees. Cost 250, and it is mandatory to proceed.
  - Umbrella head: 9 attacks. About 400.
  - Ogre: 12 attacks and one-hit kills cave frogs. About 500.
  - Ghost: 15 attacks. Snake: 19 attacks.
- Shop stock depends on position
  - Which set of items loads depends on the x-pixel where Beetlejuice enters the shop. Purchases take about 3.4 seconds. (Speed Demos Archive)
- Book for the Recently Deceased
  - A prototype had a Spook Scroll that held a password. The final game replaced it with the Book for the Recently Deceased, which gives shop access. (search summary of The Cutting Room Floor; the page itself returned an error, so treat as lightly verified)
- Door safeguard
  - Overhead doors unlock when you defeat the enemies in a room, or automatically after 14 cycles, which prevents softlocks. (Speed Demos Archive)
- Cheats
  - Game Genie codes exist for lives, invincibility, mid-air walking, and unlocking all doors. One code for 1 life, one for 6, one for 9. (Almar's Guides, Cheat Happens)
  - No in-game passwords were found for the final game.
- Discussion question: is a shop where the stock changes with where you stand clever design, or a hidden-info mess?

## V. Pop Culture: The Ghost with the Most, 1988-1991

- The film
  - Tim Burton directed it. It opened March 30, 1988, from Warner Bros. Budget about $15 million. (Wikipedia)
  - Box office: sources disagree. One Wikipedia summary says $75.1 million worldwide, another search result says $84.6 million. Say "roughly $75 to $85 million."
  - It won the Academy Award for Best Makeup and Saturn Awards for Best Horror Film, Best Makeup, and Best Supporting Actress (Sylvia Sidney). (Wikipedia)
  - Rotten Tomatoes 83 percent. Roger Ebert gave it two stars, which is a good fun fact. (Wikipedia)
- The cartoon
  - It ran from September 9, 1989 to October 26, 1991 on ABC, then on Fox Kids from September 9 to December 6, 1991. (Wikipedia)
  - 94 episodes, 109 segments. Tim Burton was executive producer along with David Geffen, made with Nelvana. (Wikipedia)
  - It won a Daytime Emmy for Outstanding Animated Program in 1990, tied with The New Adventures of Winnie the Pooh. (Wikipedia)
  - The tone changed: Beetlejuice became a prankster and best friend to Lydia, not the film's antagonist. (Wikipedia) That is why a game that follows the film's villain version while looking like the cartoon feels mixed.
  - Merchandise launched in 1990: trading cards, stickers, puzzles, video games. (Wikipedia)
- LJN and movie licenses
  - LJN was founded in 1967 by Jack Friedman, bought by MCA in 1985 and sold to Acclaim in 1990. It published NES games from 1987. (Wikipedia)
  - It was known for licensed movie games like A Nightmare on Elm Street and Friday the 13th. By 1990, video games were at least 75 percent of $70 million in sales. (Wikipedia)
  - Rare's Bayliss said LJN "gave us a lot of licensed projects," naming Roger Rabbit and Beetlejuice, before Elm Street. (Time Extension)
- Elsewhere in 1991
  - The Super Nintendo reached the US on August 23, 1991, three months after this game. (MarioWiki, SNES Central)
  - Discussion question: how weird was it to get an NES game in 1991 for a 1988 movie while the cartoon was at its peak?

## VI. Reception & Legacy

- Retro verdict is mixed to negative
  - HonestGamers gave it 1.5 out of 5 (reviewed June 17, 2019). (HonestGamers)
  - Den of Geek called it "excessively bizarre and borderline unplayable" in its roundup of horror-movie games. (Den of Geek, short quote)
  - Reviewers praise the music, minigames, and cutscenes, and criticize the graphics and the difficulty. (Wikipedia, GoCollect)
- Angry Video Game Nerd covered it in episode 121, a Halloween episode first aired October 14, 2014. (Wikipedia)
- Collecting
  - PriceCharting lists a loose cart around $35 and sealed copies around $193. (PriceCharting search summary)
- No 1991 magazine scores were found, so no contemporary reception data. Do not invent any.

## VII. Sequels and Spinoffs

- Beetlejuice: Horrific Hijinx from the Neitherworld! (Game Boy, 1992)
  - Rare again, published by Acclaim under the LJN brand, and based on the cartoon. You rescue Lydia from Astoroth, with minecarts and a Pogosnake. (Wikipedia)
  - One review called it "sluggish." (Destructoid)
- Adventures of Beetlejuice: Skeletons in the Closet (MS-DOS, late 1991), by Riedel Software Productions, published by Hi Tech Expressions. (Wikipedia)
- Later: Lego Dimensions fun pack (2017) and Betelgeuse in MultiVersus (2024). (Wikipedia)
- The film's sequel
  - Beetlejuice Beetlejuice arrived on September 6, 2024. It opened to about $110 million domestic, and its worldwide gross reached around $449 million. (Wikipedia, The Numbers)
- Rare's other work
  - Rare developed over 40 NES titles by Nesdev's count and over 60 by another source. Sources disagree.
  - Notable: Wizards & Warriors (1987), Marble Madness and Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1989), Snake Rattle 'n' Roll (1990), Battletoads (1991). Later GoldenEye 007, Banjo-Kazooie, Perfect Dark, and Microsoft bought Rare in 2003. (Nesdev, Wikipedia)

## VIII. Essential Games List Vote

- Case for Essential
  - It is one of the stranger licensed games on the NES, with real ideas: a voucher economy, scares as power-ups, two play styles.
  - The music is praised.
  - The minigames and cutscenes bring in the film's characters.
  - Rare's pedigree gives it historical interest, and it has a speedrun community.
  - It captures the Beetlejuice weirdness better than most movie games.
- Case against Essential
  - It is very hard, with collision and wall problems.
  - Reviewers score it poorly. HonestGamers gave 1.5 out of 5.
  - The mandatory skeleton scare and fetch quests make progress opaque.
  - It does not use the film's theme, and it mixes film and cartoon tones.
  - It is mostly a curiosity, not something every NES player must play.
- Discussion question: is a bold but frustrating licensed game essential, or only interesting?
