# 367 - Maniac Mansion

- Why is there a chainsaw in the kitchen?
- Overview
- Maniac Mansion is a graphic adventure game developed and published by Lucasfilm Games in 1987 for the Commodore 64 and Apple II.
- Created by Ron Gilbert and Gary Winnick, the game is notable for its unique gameplay, dark humor, and innovative SCUMM engine (Script Creation Utility for Maniac Mansion).
- Plot & Setting
- The story follows protagonist Dave Miller and his friends as they infiltrate a creepy mansion to rescue Dave's girlfriend, Sandy Pantz, from Dr. Fred Edison.
- The mansion is home to a bizarre cast, including the Edison family and a sentient green tentacle.
- The mansion’s backstory involves a meteor that crashes nearby, influencing Dr. Fred to conduct strange experiments.
- Gameplay Mechanics
- Non-linear puzzle-solving with multiple playable characters and endings.
- Players choose two companions to join Dave from a pool of six characters, each with unique abilities.
- Inventory-based puzzles and environmental interaction are central to progress.
- Notably, player choices impact outcomes, leading to a variety of endings.
- Technical Innovations
- The game introduced the SCUMM engine, which revolutionized adventure game development at Lucasfilm Games.
- The verb-noun interface became a staple for subsequent adventure titles.
- Critical Reception
- Praised for its humor, creativity, and depth.
- Credited as a foundational work in the adventure game genre.
- Censorship and Content Changes
- Profanity and inappropriate content removed or altered to fit Nintendo's policies.
  - Example: "Don't be a shit head" became "Don't be a tuna head."
  - Sexual and graphic content edited or removed (e.g., swimsuit calendar, classical sculpture).
  - Graffiti hints shortened instead of being removed entirely.
- Nintendo initially approved the game but later requested more edits.
- The infamous ability to microwave a hamster remained in the first batch of cartridges but was removed from future shipments.
- Television Adaptation of Maniac Mansion
- Development and Production:
  - Lucasfilm conceived a television adaptation of the game in 1990.
  - Rights were purchased by The Family Channel, and the series was co-produced with Atlantis Films.
  - Debuted in September 1990 on YTV (Canada) and The Family Channel (United States).
  - Eugene Levy led the writing staff; Joe Flaherty starred as Dr. Fred Edison.
- Premise:
  - Loosely based on the video game but diverged significantly during production.
  - Focused on the Edison family's everyday life rather than the game's plot.
- Critical Reception:
  - Received positive initial reviews from Variety, Entertainment Weekly, and the Los Angeles Times.
  - Time named it one of the year’s best new series.
  - Ken Tucker (Entertainment Weekly) questioned its placement on The Family Channel, citing Flaherty's subversive humor.
  - Richard Cobbett (PC Gamer) later criticized it for generic storylines and lack of relevance to the game.
- Series Longevity:
  - Aired for three seasons with a total of 66 episodes.
- Sequels and Spinoffs
- Famicom Version (Jaleco, June 23, 1988)
  - Characters redrawn in a cute art style.
  - Shrunken rooms without scrolling; flip-screen navigation for larger rooms.
  - Many background details removed.
  - No save feature; required a lengthy password (over 100 characters).
- Inspired a sequel, Day of the Tentacle (1993), and became a cult classic.
- George "The Fat Man" Sanger composed the background music for the NES version.
- A hint book was available for $7.95, offering puzzle solutions and a detailed mansion map.
- Final Release and Distribution
- Lucasfilm Games re-submitted the edited version, and Nintendo manufactured 250,000 cartridges.
