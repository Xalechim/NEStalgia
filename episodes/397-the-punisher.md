# 397 - The Punisher

- DON’T FORGET WHILE YOU’RE OUT HUNTING THEM, THEY’RE HUNTING YOU
- Introduction to Punisher
- Creation & debut: First appeared in The Amazing Spider-Man #129 (Feb 1974); created by writer Gerry Conway with artists Ross Andru and John Romita Sr. Stan Lee suggested the name “The Punisher” (Conway’s working name was “The Assassin”).
- Movies/TV at a glance: Three films—The Punisher (1989, Dolph Lundgren), The Punisher (2004, Thomas Jane), Punisher: War Zone (2008, Ray Stevenson)—plus Jon Bernthal’s definitive turn in Netflix’s Daredevil (2016) and The Punisher (2017–2019). Bernthal returns in Daredevil: Born Again (2025)
- Big comics runs / storylines:
- “Welcome Back, Frank” (2000, Garth Ennis/Steve Dillon) relaunched Frank under Marvel Knights and redefined the tone. Marvel
- Punisher MAX – “The Slavers” (2005–06) is a fan-favorite, brutally grounded arc about human trafficking. Marvel
- “Franken-Castle” (2009–10) turns Frank into a stitched-together monster after Daken dismembers him—yes, really. Wikipedia
- Cosmic Ghost Rider (2018): In a future timeline Frank becomes Ghost Rider, then a Herald of Galactus—the wildest Frank since…ever. Marvel
- Oddball & delightful:
- Archie Meets the Punisher (1994): A straight-faced crossover with Archie Comics that somehow works. Wikipedia
- Space: Punisher (2012): A pulp sci-fi miniseries blasting crime across the cosmos. Marvel
- Introduction to the Game
- What it is: An over‑the‑shoulder, on‑rails shooter for NES (1991) starring Marvel’s vigilante, Frank Castle. Developed by Beam Software and published by LJN.
- Why it matters: Odd duck in the NES library—no Zapper support, but a cursor‑aim shooter mashed with light brawler moments, environmental destruction, and Marvel fan service.

- Release, Credits, and Setup
- Release: 1991 (NES, NA). Game Boy sibling in the same year (The Punisher: The Ultimate Payback!). Similar cursor shooter game, but Spider-Man is helping the Punsiher gun people down?!
- Studio & License: Beam Software (Melbourne) for LJN; Marvel creative names in credits.
- Framing: Wanted‑poster hub that lets you target crime bosses leading up to the Kingpin.
- Controls & Core Loop (explain early)
- A = fire (rapid). B = lob explosive (grenade/rocket). D‑Pad steers the on‑screen crosshair; when not firing, Frank slides left/right at the bottom to dodge.
- Scrolling: Stages auto‑scroll across city streets, subways, docks, prisons, and sewers.
- Goal: Survive waves, shoot destructibles (windows, signs, crates, manholes) to reveal ammo, health, armor, rockets, and tips (Daily Bugle papers).
- Grade & rewards: End‑of‑stage kill % grants tiered bonuses (health → ammo → grenades → extra life → Super Gun for 100%).
- Discuss now
- Do the dodge‑while‑aiming controls click after a few minutes, or never quite gel?
- Arsenal & Pickups (quick, specific)
- Guns: Default SMG (Uzi‑like) → Assault Rifle (faster ROF) → Super Gun (earned via 100% clear).
- Explosives: Grenades (arc blast) and Rocket Launcher (aim anywhere; big splash).
- Survivability: Health kits, bulletproof vest (extends life bar), extra lives (skull), ammo clips; Daily Bugle newspapers scroll hints.
- Difficulty & Mastery
- Triage: Prioritize grenade/rocket targets (chainguns, launchers, clustered ninjas).
- Economy: Don’t hoard rockets past helicopters/tanks; vests extend risk budget—grab even at near‑full.
- 100% route: Going for kill‑perfect to earn the Super Gun changes how aggressive you play; skipping bonus rooms can preserve spawn counts.
- Presentation & Feel
- Visuals: Big sprites, lots on screen; Punisher “slides” along foreground; environmental debris everywhere.
- Audio: Sparse music; gun/explosion SFX dominate; occasional mournful sax riff from the busker.

- Stage‑by‑Stage Flavor & Talking Points (ask questions where they occur)
- Level 1 – HITMAN (Streets → Rooftops → Helicopter)
- Street learnings: Window snipers, alley ambushes, innocents on screen (a blues sax player—don’t shoot him or you lose health). Early Assault Rifle and grenades hidden in cars/trash.
- Boss set‑piece: Helicopter with chain‑guns; target hardpoints with rockets, then pelt cockpit between bursts.
- Discuss now
- Did the blues busker fake anyone out as a “powerup”? Do innocent NPCs belong in a shooter like this?
- Level 2 – JIGSAW (Docks → Prison yard → Prison interior)
- Pier play: Supports and crates hide ammo/health; scuba gunners pop from the water’s shadows.
- Prison mix: Flood of green‑berets, destructible consoles/TVs for supplies; optional bonus rooms behind “GO” prompts.
- Boss beat: Jigsaw swaps between gunfire and in‑your‑face melee; brief close‑quarters sequence where you time kicks/punches, then resume explosives.
- Discuss now
- Love or hate the sudden melee interludes—cool change‑up or control tax?
- Level 3 – COLONEL KLIEGG (Harbor loop → Tank fight)
- Harbor redux with harder ninjas and ammo management.
- Boss: Rolling tank duel—strafe, toss explosives during salvos, finish when Kliegg exposes the hatch.
- Discuss now
- Does the tank fight sell “Punisher vs. machine,” or just become a grenade dump?
- Level 4 – SIJO KANAKA (City → Subway bonus → Sewers)
- City/subway: Newspapers point secrets (ride the train bonus); ninjas telegraph with leaps—tag them as they land.
- Sewers: Scarce vests—remember which blue pipes/boxes hide health.
- Boss: Aerial sword arcs + shuriken; bait leaps, kick punish, then resume fire.
- Level 5 – ASSASSIN (Warehouse labyrinth → Bonus rooms)
- Warehouses: Carton pyramids stuffed with ammo/rockets; chain‑gun elites on stairwells; quick detours to “GO” bonus rooms.
- Boss: Cyborg with detachable arm cannons—pop parts off in phases; grenades trivialize patterns.
- Final Level – KINGPIN (Inner city gauntlet → Office finale)
- Attrition test: Heavier snipers, ninjas + berets overlap; tons of destructible pickups if you remember which signs/manholes/benches matter.
- Finale: There is some hand to hand combat with Kingpin
- Beyond the Game (context you can weave between segments)
- Comics moment: Late‑80s/early‑90s anti‑hero boom (Punisher maxed in popularity; Kingpin/Jigsaw as household villain names).
- Platform quirks: Released under Nintendo’s content rules—bloodless hit reactions, but unusually intense gunplay for NES.
