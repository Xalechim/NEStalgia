# 209 - Jordan vs. Bird: One on One

Rare made this for Milton Bradley in 1989: half-court basketball starring Michael Jordan and Larry Bird. It is advertised as three games in one, but we call it three minigames, the way Track and Field 2 was not 12 games in one. It has no style, and the licensed names are its only draw.

### One on one

- **Backyard rules.** You can toggle winner's out and pick first to 11, first to 15 or a full game. None of it changes how the game plays, only how long it takes. A brick wall sits behind the court, and the sprites look like teenagers in gym class, not the grown pros of Double Dribble.
- **Defense is a crapshoot.** A block needs near-perfect position and timing with no tell, and the steal button needs spamming. Chasing the rebound after a miss worked best.
- **The AI is simple.** Box the computer into a corner and it waits until the shot clock is nearly out, then throws a low-percentage shot you can rebound or block. As Jordan, driving to the net means you will hit your dunk, which is basically press X to win.

### Slam dunk contest

This is Michael against Michael. In Follow the Leader you pick a move, watch it, then repeat it, and the moves are just left, right or down the middle. The score comes down to when you release the button relative to the hoop. Dunks landed for some players, but another tried A, B, tapping and holding, and just ran in circles until the clock ran out.

We wanted flair, like the diving in Track and Field or the half pipe in Skate or Die.

### Three-point shootout

Larry Bird goes against another Larry Bird, in the same jersey. Carts of balls get wheeled out, then disappear and respawn, which recreates the problem they solve. Releasing at the top of your jump felt like it made more shots. None of the side modes has a reward, so they feel like warm-ups for one on one.

### Worth knowing

The original was a computer game, One-on-One: Dr. J versus Larry Bird, which never came to consoles. On the Jordan tangent, all 12 voting Nintendo Power staffers called Michael Jordan Chaos in the Windy City one of the worst games of all time.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean said the only selling point is the two licensed likenesses: with basketball player A and basketball player B, it would have zero upside. Joe joked that only the randomly spawning basketball cart kept it off, then agreed it didn't do anything better than Double Dribble. We also never felt we got a complete game: even one on one plays like a demo.
