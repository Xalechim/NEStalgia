# 260 - Kings of the Beach

Konami made this for Ultra Games in 1990: two-on-two beach volleyball with real pro players and a tournament that ends in Australia, though the screen is not upside down there. We never know what we're getting from an NES sports game, and this one surprised us. It is simple, and it works.

### What works

- **Few inputs, good feel.** You set, spike, serve and block. A, B or both together hit the ball, and the D-pad steers it afterward. We were shaky at first, then it felt fair, and a higher difficulty was a decent challenge.
- **The ball moves like a volleyball.** It is fast when first hit, slows at the top and speeds up again. Next to other sports games, it moves the way a volleyball should.
- **It looks good.** The sprites are clearly defined and fairly realistic, and the ball has texture.
- **Arguing with the ref.** Argue too much and you get a yellow card, then a red. We went back and forth on whether a game that knows the right answer should add bad calls. Playing an older brother, you would just want it to say who won.

### What doesn't

- **It locks you in place.** Get under the ball and the game holds you there. That probably helps with depth perception, but it blocks undercutting for a direct spike.
- **Not much to it.** There are three tutorials for three moves and little to the tournament. Over time it could get stale.
- **The computer.** Skip the aftertouch and it becomes a juggling game of four people volleying. Later it gets almost too good, which sent us into Mario Tennis on the N64 and Birdo.

### Worth knowing

- **Real players.** Sinjin Smith and Randy Stoklos have bios, and you can't pick who you play as except in versus. In 1990 you would need a library and a sports atlas to look them up. Two characters are made up, and Lester is from the Skate or Die series.
- **Four players.** The NES Four Score puts two on each side, not four against two computers. Joe hadn't tried it, but figured it is "definitely a multiplayer" game.
- **The greeters.** Winning a tournament seems to get you a bikini-clad greeter. By the second one, the one-piece is a two-piece.

### The bottom line

Play it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. It does almost exactly what it sets out to do, but there is not enough substance, and it is one iteration of the sport on the NES, the same reasoning we gave NES Golf. It is a fun one-on-one or party game, not a must-play unless you are looking for it.
