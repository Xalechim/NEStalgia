# 273 - Rollerball

HAL Laboratory made this for HAL America, a pinball game billed as having "a distinctly American touch." That touch is a Statue of Liberty and a Big Apple on the main menu, and we noted there are no American designers on it. It is pinball, and it works, but it isn't doing anything above water.

### What works

- **Things to do.** Each screen has missions: light up letters, hit bumpers until they disappear, open bonus modes. It is still just points, but it is more than most earlier pinball games gave us, and the last one had no theme at all.
- **A very long table.** Skyscraper is four screens, about twice the length of a standard table, like a stack of short pinball machines. Fall off one and you land on the next, and you only lose a life past the bottom one. With that safety net, a game can run longer than you'd care to play.

### What doesn't

- **The top is the dull part.** The little bonus area is the least interesting section, and you can sit there a long time. With no risk up there, we wanted multiball, locked bumpers or dead flippers.
- **The theme is wallpaper.** You go from ground level to the top to the sky with a blimp, but only in the background. The Statue of Liberty is pixel art, and we wanted to light that torch.
- **The menu.** The select screen offers Skyscraper or Match Play, plus two blank screens that look like locked modes. They unlock nothing. Skyscraper also sounds like a map, or the machine's name, not a mode.
- **The side exits.** Two drains on the side that you can't control. We hate them, and figure they are there to eat quarters. The game didn't invent them.

### Match play and the donkey

Match play is the two-player mode, on a much smaller screen. Each player has a set number of points, and the first to lose them all loses. You share one side of the screen, so it is a race to stay alive, not air hockey.

The Japanese version has an elephant and a giraffe. Here the giraffe became a donkey, which left us wanting a presidential debate pinball game.

### Worth knowing

- **The title.** Last episode we spitballed a roller derby game on skates. Instead the ball rolls, so we'd call it ball roller.
- **The score.** The player one marker sits next to your score, so you seem to start with 100 million.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean called it his favorite pinball game so far, though he said that is a low bar. Joe said it works fine but will blend into the other 700 NES games and every other pinball game. Nothing about it stands out.
