# 425 - Kabuki: Quantum Fighter

It's 2056, a virus has taken over the defense supercomputer that runs the nuclear weapons, and the fix is to upload Colonel Scott O'Connor into it as data. The system builds his avatar from a kabuki actor ancestor, so he arrives in white face paint with long red hair, and he fights with it. Mike, Sean and Joe played, and the gap between a deadly serious plot and a hero headbanging at robots is where most of the charm lives.

### Why it's fun

- **The hair.** It out-ranges your punches by a lot and feels crunchier. Crouching oddly drops you back to punching, which we decided exists so you appreciate the hair.
- **The hooks.** Tap A for a short swing, hold for a full arc, and let go to hang when you reach the next one. Mike said this is how he wanted the grapple in Bionic Commando to work. We kept pressing A again on landing and falling, then stopped.
- **Chips.** Hair is free, while weapons won from bosses cost chips that drop at random from enemies. The fusion gun's three-way spread speeds up boss fights, and the bolo ties that hunt on their own were a favorite. During a boss you can pause and press up or down to trade health for chips at two to one. That is on page 17 of the manual, and we hadn't read that far.
- **Bosses with patterns.** The first is your size and moves like a player. The third-stage green spider robot hangs from the ceiling, sends minions first, and has to be baited down. The penultimate boss is a mech you climb. The last fight is on the computer's guts, and the floor is squirming.

### Where we split

- **The levels.** Mike's complaint is dropping blind into spikes and enemies, then redoing a hook section, plus enemies spawning in unfair places. Joe and Sean enjoyed traversing it.
- **A story that fizzles.** The intro warns that going in could change who you are, and nothing comes of it. The ending is a virus from space and Scott saying "I'd like to return to my body now."
- **Not very kabuki.** The costume is all there is of the theater, with no percussion and no staging, and the stages look like biological dark fantasy, not circuitry.

### Beyond the game

Human Entertainment developed it and HAL Laboratory published it. The Famicom original was a tie-in to a 1990 film called Zipang, which from what we could find is about navy men and a comet-shaped bead, not a kabuki hero. We couldn't tell where the kabuki came from.

### The bottom line

Play it. On the Essential Games List, Joe voted yes: one of the best action platformers we've played, short enough for one night, and he loves the music. Sean and Mike voted no, so it didn't make the list. Sean called it competent but not something he expects to replay, and Mike said the level design holds back a great character and great bosses. A hidden gem, in Joe's words.
