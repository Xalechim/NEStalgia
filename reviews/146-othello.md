# 146 - Othello

HAL Laboratory made this for Acclaim Entertainment in 1988, and it is the board game on the NES, with computer opponents. All three hosts were there, and we opened by joking that someone should be kicked off because Othello is really a two-player game. Joe said it is pretty darn accurately just exactly Othello. The computer is the only real reason to own it, and we would rather play at a table.

### What works

- **A good computer opponent.** The AI seems well designed. The manual says the difficulty levels differ in how long the computer gets to think, so it looks further ahead on the higher ones. Level one probably never makes a stupid move, just a worse one than level four would.
- **A training center.** Sean's case for it: you can test strategies against the computer over and over with no stakes.

### What doesn't

- **It will not teach you.** The computer only makes good to amazing moves and there is no beginner mode. Mike had to leave the game to learn that corners matter, and we told him that sounds like asking for a tutorial. Ideas floated: an easier computer, or daily puzzles like Lichess.
- **The menu.** Start, start, start takes you to the end of the name entry, and then you still have to press A. We agreed that is goofy.
- **Look and sound.** The look is Spartan, with gray filling the space around the board and a small floating hand cursor with purple nails. The menu music sounds like notes spilling out of a cup, nothing like Mega Man or Castlevania.

### Worth knowing

- **Tips.** Joe's: hold the middle square, and don't mind giving your opponent more of your color early, since it makes the end easier to reverse. Another: keep only three or four pieces early, then grab edges, lines and diagonals all at once, though a really good player can take every piece you have.
- **The dots.** An online version showed little dots for every available move, and it made for better play. We agreed that is a modification of Othello, which is "now called Esposito."

### The bottom line

Skip it. We called it not essential. Joe said the AI is nice, but nothing about playing on the NES makes it a must-play, and then came a pun that it is "not a black and white issue." The box line "a minute to learn, a lifetime to master" was the wrong angle. It is more like a minute to learn and a minute to put down, because the best Othello is against real people as good as you, and that is hard to find in your living room in 1988.
