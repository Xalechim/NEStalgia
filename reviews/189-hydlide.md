# 189 - Hydlide

T&E Soft made this for FCI in 1989, an action RPG that began as a super popular Japanese computer game from 1984. By the time it reached the NES, we wondered if it was too late. It was: we called it a tech demo, a prototype with real ideas and no game around them.

### What works

- **The ideas were early.** The experience system and the different types of magic all work. If it is the first of its kind, that is an accomplishment, but it hasn't aged well.
- **Health regenerates.** Stand on the grass and your life fills back up. We liked the idea of regeneration in an NES game.
- **The ending is a great story.** You go to an island and a dragon flies back and forth. Burn a bush, find a tunnel, make the girl whole again, and the screen says congratulations. We joked that it is probably better than playing it.

### What doesn't

- **No feedback.** Hold A to attack and the word in the corner changes from defense to attack. There is no sword swing, and a dead enemy just vanishes. In a real-time game you cannot react to what you cannot see.
- **It is a lawn mowing simulator.** Enemies wander without purpose, and the vampire chases you worse than the Pac-Man ghosts do. Stronger enemies block each area, so you grind. A 45 minute playthrough we watched was about 38 minutes of grinding.
- **The health bar is tiny.** You keep stopping to rest, and we pictured a hero having a picnic in the grass.
- **Random death.** Run into a tree on the screen below the start and a swarm of bees kills you. A password reload can drop you back at low health, dying every six steps.
- **One song.** The loop is short, the same track plays even in the dungeons, and a second track shows up only in the last room, for about 12 seconds.

### Worth knowing

Saving gives you a password, but the save is erased when you turn off the console, and the manual warns you. The menu has six options: item, save, load, password, speed and game. Game is how you get out of the menu.

The sequels add a morality meter, then an in-game clock so you must sleep and eat, then a Sega Saturn version. Hydlide 2 never came to the NES, and we were relieved.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. It is a curio to study for its poor design decisions, and we can't recommend anyone play it. Hydlide had an idea, and later games worked out how to execute it.
