# 123 - Donkey Kong Classics

Nintendo made and published this in 1988: Donkey Kong and Donkey Kong Jr. on one cartridge. All three hosts were there, and Joe missed the original Donkey Kong episode. Nothing in the games changed, so we spent a long time on the menu screen, then on remakes and remasters.

### The menu

- **The palette.** Deep blue background, pinkish red title, and we liked it. Red on blue is hard to read, though, and the O's in "Donkey Kong" can look like A's.
- **How it works.** Up and down pick the mode, game A or B and one or two players. Select switches between Donkey Kong and Donkey Kong Jr. Left and right do the same.
- **The asterisk.** The cursor has eight points, which we blamed on the NES being an eight-bit system. We saw a windmill in it, then a spider, which did not hold up.

### The games

Both play exactly like the originals, with nothing added. Donkey Kong is still missing the Pie Factory level, while Jr. is complete. We had assumed ROM space was the reason, and fitting Jr. on the cart made us doubt that.

Joe said that without nostalgia for the arcade original, he would not be interested after playing it on the NES. It is fine and historically important, but he has always thought it was more popular than good. We also kept hitting an NES quirk: miscount the ladder climbs and you hover above the ladder while a barrel arrives.

### Why does this exist?

- **Bundling.** Two popular games on one cart, like Super Mario/Duck Hunt, and Donkey Kong was huge in the arcades. There was no new Donkey Kong game until Donkey Kong 94, so it looks like the last gasp of the mascot.
- **No incentive.** No Donkey Kong 3, no Donkey Kong Jr. Math, so it is an incomplete collection, and if you own the games already there is no reason to buy it.
- **The return of Donkey Kong.** An early Nintendo Power teased a game with that name. The argument is that this was it, and Nintendo of America never got the memo.

### The detour

A long run on remakes, remasters and re-releases, from Crash Bandicoot to Chrono Trigger. Donkey Kong Classics is a straight re-release, and a strange one, since it landed on the same system instead of a system later.

### The bottom line

Skip it. It did not go onto the Essential Games List. Joe's reasoning was that neither game was essential alone, so putting them together does not make them essential, and Sean's was a "perfunctory no vote." We called it a squandered chance: a combined game with extra features could have made the list. If Nintendo makes Donkey Kong Classics 2, we're buying.
