# 241 - Cybernoid: The Fighting Machine

Studio 12 Productions made this NES port for Acclaim in 1989. It gets filed as a shmup, but we couldn't call it one. You steer a ship through tight screens, shooting enemies, dodging hazards and picking the right weapon for each obstacle. We liked the idea more than the game, and the title is very 80s.

### What works

- **It switches jobs fast.** Each puzzle is locked to one screen, so you get a second to assess it. It swings between shooting, solving and timing without feeling jarring.
- **The weapons are puzzle pieces.** You have bombs, shields, bouncers, seekers and a power-up called Genocide, which blows up every enemy ship. The green blocks in the first puzzle need bouncers.
- **It builds a little.** The red elevator things kill you on touch. Later come two in a row moving opposite ways, then ones with obstacles to shoot while you stay between them.
- **The start screen.** Your ship and its blueprints, with a classified border. You can't pick a loadout, but it is the coolest part.

### What doesn't

- **Short and brutal.** There are three levels, and it can be beaten in under seven minutes. We still died a lot, and late screens are about finding the one or two spots that won't kill you. Even easy, one of three difficulty modes, is not easy.
- **Softlocks.** Everything is limited, and you can't know what you'll need. Waste a bouncer and the only way to get it back may be to die, or to start over if you're on your last life.
- **A story from nowhere.** It lives in the manual. At the end a woman says "well done, Cybernoid Pilot. You have defeated the Baycores," and it feels unearned.
- **Random progression.** We wanted it to teach one thing, then add to it.

### Worth knowing

- **The sound.** You get a constant boom boom boom until you pause and press B, which plays a music track. We preferred the music.
- **One guy.** One programmer made the original computer version, for the ZX Spectrum. Acclaim hired Studio 12 to port it. A sequel, Cybernoid 2, The Revenge, has the same gameplay in new environments.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Joe called it a basic, crude version of a neat idea, almost a proof of concept, and Sean said it was strange enough that he didn't know how it got on the NES. It reminded us of Air Fortress, which had a much clearer objective in each stage. This one is a little too tough and too short, so go play Air Fortress instead. It's a better game.
