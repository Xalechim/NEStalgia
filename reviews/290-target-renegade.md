# 290 - Target Renegade

Software Creations made this for Taito in 1990, a sequel to Renegade, our first beat-em-up on the system. We praised that one for just feeling like an arcade brawler, which no longer flies after Double Dragon and River City Ransom. Mike and Sean played this one without Joe. We agree it is a step down from recent beat-em-ups, though Sean liked it anyway.

### What works

- **The bosses.** Weak points are spelled out as tips on the status bar. The first boss is huge and throws tires, and one kick takes a quarter of your health, so you punch his gut and run, over and over.
- **The enemies.** Bret Hart lookalikes, skateboarders, construction workers who skip leg day, and girls in tube tops who look ready to whip their hair at you before they punch. One guy just stands there with his hands in his pockets, and we took him for a pervert. You still have to beat him up to move on.
- **The attitude.** The status bar tells you where to go with a lot of "yo, kid." The music is intense, mostly guitar solos, and the opening is hot.

### What doesn't

- **It looks like 1987.** The sprites look outlined and filled in with the paint bucket, with barely any shading. The backgrounds are nicer.
- **More of the same.** Motorcycle riders must be kicked off their bikes, and replacements keep coming. Thrown fire extinguishers and garbage cans only matter in the scene where you find them, and outside the bosses the enemies are sponges.
- **A strange leaderboard.** The first thing you see is a high score table of ten slots all named Target Renegade. With no saving, it says "today's" scores, as if friends would leave the console on for hours.

### Worth knowing

Your brother is kidnapped in this version, and the rescue happens off screen as a line of text. The last stage has a better joke: the men's and women's room doors are huge and Mr. Big's is tiny, revealing he is the smallest character in the game.

The manual calls each batch of enemies a "scene," and its notes are funnier than the game: the karate clan "can make coleslaw out of anyone," and nothing explains why.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Mike was sure within ten minutes: even if it improves on Renegade, it felt like 25 percent of a game we had already played. Sean enjoyed the goofy enemies, the over-the-top music and a HUD with an attitude, but agreed it is slick in some ways and underdeveloped in others.
