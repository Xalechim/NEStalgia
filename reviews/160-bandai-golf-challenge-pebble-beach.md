# 160 - Bandai Golf: Challenge Pebble Beach

TOSE made this for Bandai in 1989, a golf game named after the Pebble Beach course instead of a golfer. Sam joined Mike, Sean and Joe for this one. We kept comparing it to NES Golf and Lee Trevino's Fighting Golf, and it lost to both.

### How it plays

- **Golf from a menu.** You play mostly from satellite-style imagery, and your golfer only shows up when you swing, like the rotoscoped "up next" character on cable TV. He is out of proportion to the course, so shot length is hard to judge.
- **Rigid.** Where your ball lands limits your clubs: no one wood after the tee shot, and sometimes no three wood off the fairway. Aiming moves in 45 degree steps or a bit less, and the putting D-pad is so sensitive you tap it and hope.
- **A tiny swing window.** Miss the sweet spot a little and the ball goes nowhere, where NES Golf gave far more leeway. The meter also moves at a random speed that has nothing to do with real golf, so timing shouldn't be the hard part.

### What works

- **The information.** The elevation cross-section is cool and useful. We finally get the distance to the hole, a long complaint about the other golf games. There is also a handicap, which we don't think the others had, and a detailed scorecard.

### What doesn't

- **The music.** The title screen has groovy, Kirby's Dream Land-inspired music. On the course it is the same track over and over.
- **The wind.** It fluctuates, and the readout seems to disappear once you pick a club, so we couldn't tell if it keeps changing. You can just wait for a great wind in your favor.
- **No personality.** All that data adds up to golf as boring as it seems on TV. We wanted a silly Lunar Pool-style mini golf game, and the putting system is nearly all one would need.

### Worth knowing

We argued over the colon in the title, and "Pebble Beach challenge would have made more sense." Pebble Beach is a bucket-list course.

In the manual, page four lists "start" in bold, then the title screen, then an asterisk for title music, like notes for making the game. All of it is in a subtle Masters green.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List; it was four no votes. Mike said Lee Trevino's Fighting Golf beats it in every way. Joe said that without NES Golf and Lee Trevino this would be fine, but since we have both, skip it. Sean likened it to a Gran Turismo B-Spec mode, telling a guy what to do instead of playing golf.
