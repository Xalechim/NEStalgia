# 412 - Nintendo World Cup

The real 1990 World Cup averaged 2.2 goals a game and was famously defensive. Nintendo World Cup is five-on-five soccer with no fouls, no offsides and players who get knocked out and lie on the field. It is a Kunio-kun game from Technos Japan, the people behind the dodgeball game and River City Ransom, with the Famicom's high school story stripped out and 13 national teams swapped in. Sam and Joe were out, so this was just Mike and Sean.

### The fun parts

- **Violence as strategy.** A knocked-out player stays down until the half ends, and nobody substitutes. That turns a match into a power play: six on five, six on two if you are ruthless enough. With no referees, nobody can dive. Scores climb well past anything real soccer produces, and every game we played was a blowout.
- **Super shots.** Press A and B together while shooting and each team has its own: backflips, headers, a spinning disc for the USA. They animate like the special throws in Dodgeball. You get five per half.
- **Pre-match choices.** Offensive or defensive, how aggressively teammates tackle, and which six players start. It is a gentle way of handing strategy to the computer. Roster stats are hidden even though the manual says players differ, so you learn by playing.
- **Turf.** In versus mode you can pick grass, sand, dirt or ice. Play a sand match, then go straight into tournament mode without resetting, and the sand carries over, which was almost certainly not intended.
- **Four-player soccer.** The box and manual push the NES Four Score and the wireless NES Satellite. We read it as Nintendo of America localizing the game to sell Four Scores while America was back in the World Cup for the first time since 1950.

### The less fun parts

- **One player only.** You control one guy, with no switch button. We worked out why (A and B are taken) and that it is a choice, since four people can play at once.
- **Cheese beats finesse.** Twirling in front of the goalie nearly guarantees a goal, and a direct shot gets saved. Sean's biggest complaint is the feel: passes get intercepted, a one-timer sends the ball backward, and you end up playing the janky version to win.
- **A hollow shell.** We would have liked the high school tournament and its story.

### Beyond the game

Versions came to the Game Boy, PC Engine and Mega Drive, and a Kunio hockey game never left Japan. Nobody ever added rules or referees, and no North American sequel followed.

### The bottom line

Play it. Neither of us voted it Essential: Mike can see people remembering it fondly, but soccer is not what he wants from an 8-bit game, and Sean called it fun and not essential at all. It is better with friends, where the jank becomes part of the fun.
