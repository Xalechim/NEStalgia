# 054 - Athena

Athena is SNK's second NES game after Ikari Warriors, ported by Micronics from the arcade original. We talked about it as our first adventure where the hero is a woman from start to finish, which should have been a bigger moment than it was. The game itself is a side-scroller that spends most of its energy fighting you, and we had more to say about Athena's afterlife than about playing her.

### What works

- **A look of its own.** The backgrounds, trees included, have a slightly painted feel, and the style stays consistent across all eight worlds. It doesn't read as generic NES art.
- **The armor idea.** You build a set from helmets, chest armor, boots and shields, found by digging in the dirt or taking drops from fallen enemies. A helmet lets you smash blocks for pickups. We called the "RPG-like elements" label generous, but the upgrades are a fair hook.
- **The mermaid and wings.** In the sea and sky worlds Athena transforms, and it gets you through a tight squeeze or two. That's about all it does, and you lose it quickly.

### What doesn't

- **Hits that never register.** There are no invincibility frames, so a crowd of those horse-headed enemies drains you every frame you touch one. There is also almost no sound or visual feedback for hitting or being hit, and a game over just cuts to black. We briefly wondered if the controller had come unplugged.
- **Soft locks.** In the very first level, a rapid-fire archer's arrows can seal off the only way back, and we had to take a death to escape. Worse, you need the bow to beat the final boss, Dante, and nothing tells you to pick it up. Miss it and you start over.
- **Enemies everywhere.** They spawn endlessly and seem placed with no thought, and swapping from a good weapon to a dropped club by accident is a nuisance. We didn't blame the game for that last one, since Mario had only two power-ups to juggle.
- **The jump.** Keep jumping and you leap high. Stop for a second and you're back to a low hop that is nearly useless.
- **No story, no transitions.** The only scene is Athena jumping into a void, with no door shown, and worlds just cut to black.

### Beyond the game

The designer's 1996 interview is mostly pride: he drew Athena himself, got fan mail written to the character, and was proudest of a fire sword effect that ate his memory budget. We joked that it sounds like a filmmaker boasting about one special effect in a movie with no sound. Her descendant Athena Asamiya became a fixture of the King of Fighters series, and the original turns up as a secret boss in SNK vs. Capcom: SVC Chaos, in Neo Geo Battle Coliseum, and in a 2006 Japanese mobile sequel.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List, and the arcade version is just as bad, so don't hunt down a cabinet. We had more to say about the history than the game, which tells you enough. Kid Icarus did the Greek-myth hero thing first and better.
