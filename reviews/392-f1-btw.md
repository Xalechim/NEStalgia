# 392 - Formula One: Built to Win

Formula One: Built to Win is an NES exclusive that plays like an early cousin of Gran Turismo: buy cars, upgrade parts, pass tests, and climb from a Mini Cooper to the Formula One circuit. Mike and Sean were on this one. We were stunned it exists, even as the racing itself left us cooler. Our verdict is Play it.

### What works

- **A career on a cartridge.** You start in New York, Detroit and Miami, earn cash, buy parts, then move on to Yellowstone, Las Vegas, Denver and eventually F1. It's a sandbox, not a level ladder: you pick your town and race, and each city sells different parts. It battery-saves, so you can do New York today and Detroit tomorrow.
- **Upgrades you can feel.** Engines make you faster, tires help handling, and heavier gear has trade-offs. Even small improvements read clearly, which can't be said for most games with several vehicles.
- **Parts you watch go on.** In an RPG you buy the sword and it just appears. Here the old engine comes off, the new one goes on, and you sell old gear at a different shop. Every new car also swaps your dashboard, from a lame Mini Cooper to something futuristic.
- **Cars that feel different.** In free mode the Mini Cooper, the economy sports car, the Ferrari F40 and the F1 car all handle differently, and each has its own engine sound.
- **The F1 stretch.** Those races use real 1990 tracks, including Brazil, Mexico City and Phoenix for the USA round. The car is fast and twitchy, the sense of speed is convincing, and nitro costs finally start to matter.
- **Vegas.** Fly there and bet your winnings on slots. It's less of a cheat than it sounds, since nothing sells to you until you've cleared the lower-rank races.

### What doesn't

- **The racing itself.** It's arcade driving, and opponents act more like moving obstacles. Results depend on parts and money: either you're underequipped and can't win, or overequipped and wave goodbye to everyone. Mike called it a B plus racer.
- **Traffic.** Blue cars are your opponents and they block you. Green cars are civilians who stay in their lane. We never understood why sanctioned, entry-fee races share the road with commuters, and rear-ending anything spins you out.
- **Always last at the start.** Every pre-F1 race is a rolling start from the back.
- **Nitro housekeeping.** Refilling after races is a box you forget to check.
- **Music or effects, not both.**
- **No reward at the end.** Win the title and that's it. Free mode offers time attack on any car and track, but with no computer opponents.

### The bottom line

Play it. Neither Mike nor Sean voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean called it worth playing if you like racing games. The scaffolding around the racing is something we hadn't seen on the system, but the racing underneath is merely decent, and we wish the F1 strategy had shown up sooner. Maybe the developers had a better one in them, and we'd happily see it.
