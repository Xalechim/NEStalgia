# 460 - Flight of the Intruder

Imagineering developed it, Mindscape and Activision published it, and it landed in 1991 as our special Christmas episode, which led to a lot of Santa Claus jokes. It is a trimmed port of a PC flight sim, with a 32-page NES manual standing in for the 192-page computer one. It is not based on the movie. It borrows the name of the 1986 novel and the Vietnam setting, and none of the plot or characters.

### The three-part mission

- **Dogfight.** A circular 3D space, with a horizon line, a radar and a cockpit view to keep you oriented. Enemy planes start tiny and grow as they close, and they are where you expect when you turn around. It was the easiest mode to win, and we compared it to the all-range mode in Star Fox 64. The catch is that turning is hard to feel, and we wished for a landmark or a moon on the horizon.
- **Bombing.** The camera sits behind the plane and you fire at ships and things that might be hangars or houses. Once the radar-guided missiles run out, it is hard to tell where the rest will land, and DEFCON counts down from five with every miss. The plane sprite and the twists and turns look great, and dodging return fire is the best part.
- **Landing.** All you get is a small readout of numbers in the top left corner. The carrier sits at 70 feet, so you come down to the 80 to 100 range and hold it there, then slow down. The B button lowers the landing gear, and you need the arrestor hook too. A good pilot catches the third of four cables. We called it a drawn-out Flappy Bird, and we wished it were in first person so we could see the carrier.

### The sim details

Night missions arrive later and are a palette swap, like the Game Boy Color button trick. The cockpit looks great, and you can watch the gauges work in real time. There is no music during the dogfights. The computer version makes you navigate to the mission by compass, and we were glad the NES cut that.

### Where it runs out

The three segments repeat for every mission. The manual gives each mission a date and a target, such as a radar station near the Tanho bridge, but they play the same. Missions get longer, the enemies do not get smarter, and the palette changes. By the end it felt like padding.

### Odds and ends

The Phantom Air Mission is the European title for this same game. Imagineering also made Stealth ATF on the NES. The PC original is still sold on GOG, and the NES cartridge runs about 15 dollars loose on eBay.

### The bottom line

Play it, but only if you like flight sims. We called it a competent flight sim for the NES, and also boring once the first three missions are over. Joe called it a jack-of-some-trades and said no, Sean said it is not essential, and nobody made the case for the Essential Games List. If the genre is your thing, the PC original is probably the better version.
