# 229 - Romance of the Three Kingdoms

Koei made and published this top-down strategy game in 1989. You run provinces, armies and generals to become supreme ruler of the falling Han Dynasty. Mike and Sean played without Joe. It is Nobunaga's Ambition in China, same team and same systems, so we only covered what changed. It is impressive on the hardware and cumbersome to play.

### What's better than Nobunaga's Ambition

- **Fighting has layers.** Adjacent troops can attack together, so small armies can surround one big enemy unit. Tiles can be set on fire, by you or the computer, and wind decides whether you start a chain reaction or burn yourself. That keeps combat from being a number cruncher with nice pixels.
- **It is a war game.** Generals are the point: recruit the best from other states, kill the weak leaders first. Diplomacy mostly means giving a general a horse or some rice to keep him happy.
- **Advisors.** Strong generals can become advisors who approve or disapprove of your move. The one we got mostly asked if we had something better to do.

### What doesn't

- **Menu diving.** To attack province 30 you open the map, wait for it to draw, hunt for the number, check who owns it in another menu, declare war, then pick generals, men and rice. Big plans get lost in small steps.
- **Nothing explains it.** The manual would need to be a textbook. The game asks how much rice to bring to war with no baseline, and we said half. What got us through was an hour-long YouTube beginner's lesson.
- **Save, re-roll, repeat.** You have to save mid-turn, because a general who doesn't appreciate your rice wastes the turn. Stats can be re-rolled instantly, which undercuts choosing a character. The video starts with 15 minutes of restarting to land the right general, so every campaign looks alike.
- **It takes forever.** The guy in the video reached his second land battle in an hour, so we figure 20 plus hours to finish. It is a computer game trapped on a console with two buttons and a D-pad: "It really doesn't want to be here."

### Worth knowing

We will be playing Romance of the Three Kingdoms 2 on the NES, and we hope it is more than a graphics upgrade and teaches the systems in the game.

### The bottom line

Skip it. It did not go onto the Essential Games List. Sean's was the only vote on the recording, and it was no: he can't get the modern blueprint of the genre out of his head, and he knows more about Japanese history, so Nobunaga's Ambition appealed to him more. He called neither worthy.
