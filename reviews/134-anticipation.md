# 134 - Anticipation

Rare made this and Nintendo released it in 1988. The box calls it Nintendo's first video board game. It is really Pictionary with the computer doing the drawing and a thin board game on top. We were expecting Monopoly and got a mini game with a board attached.

### The picture game

- **The computer draws.** Dots appear, the computer connects them, and the first player to recognize the picture buzzes in. We missed drawing ourselves, and without it the game feels like half a game. We'll have to wait for Color a Dinosaur to draw anything on the NES.
- **The pictures are decent.** The finished drawings are pretty competent line art, and most were guessable a quarter or halfway through. The "industrial beehive" was not.
- **The computer gives itself away.** To fake a wrong guess, it fills in junk characters, like V-O-L, a star and a percent sign for volcano. We thought it should just pick one wrong letter and be told it's wrong.

### Difficulty

On easy, a game can be finished alone while the computer players never touch the board. Higher difficulties remove the dots and category titles, and the computer can guess a blowtorch from three or four tiny lines, then spend five or six seconds drawing it. We found no difficulty curve, and Mike and Joe both stuck to medium.

### The board game

- **How it works.** Sean walked us through it: pick a token (shoes, trumpet, ice cream or teddy bear), go around a circle of colored spaces, and answer a picture to collect each of four colors. Collect all four to go up a level.
- **The dice are your speed.** Answer fast and you roll a six, slow and you roll a one. That clashes with the four colors: you may want a three for the last one, but the computer may beat you to the answer.
- **Feature squares.** The manual promises "something crazy will happen. Stay cool and press the A button right on time." It may be the most exciting thing in the game, which isn't very.

### What it could have been

We wanted more board game: a trivia round, a catch-the-ball style mini game, Mario Party style tiles, cards that make others guess after one line. Even a category like movies turned out to be just pictures.

### The bottom line

Skip it. We called it not essential. Joe first voted yes as a joke, lumping it in with Spy vs. Spy and Super Mario Bros. 3, then walked it back. Sean's summary: Pictionary minus the drawing, minus yelling at friends who keep saying you're wrong, minus the drunken antics, plus "a multicolored circle with ice cream on it." It isn't horrible, but it is boring, and more fun on paper with a friend.
