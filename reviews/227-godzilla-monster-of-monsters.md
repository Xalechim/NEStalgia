# 227 - Godzilla: Monster of Monsters

Compile made this for Toho in 1989. Godzilla and Mothra take on a legion of space monsters sent by Planet X, in a hex-tile board game with short side-scrolling levels for getting around and a battle screen for the monster fights. We found it tedious, and Godzilla deserved better.

### The board game

- **Moving barely matters.** We kept looking for a reason to position pieces: flanking, strongholds, a reward for the harder route. The answer was no. You can stay put and let the monsters come to you, which saves the health the walk would cost.
- **Chained terrain.** Move several tiles and the levels play back to back, wilderness into volcano into jungle. In theory you steer toward terrain you handle well. In practice it is a coat of paint.
- **Repetition.** Monsters repeat, and you have seen most of the terrain by the second or third board.
- **Bare fights.** Battles play out in a blank black space with no backdrop. Mothra treats them like a shmup boss, and Godzilla trades blows and hopes he has enough power for atomic breath.

### Godzilla and Mothra

- **Godzilla plods.** He moves slowly, which gives him weight, and you do feel like the boss: hard to take down, generous health drops, but hard to dodge.
- **The knockback.** Every hit shoves you back, and since the side-scroller locks off where you came from, enemies pin you in the corner. The squid-looking boss did it without even doing damage.
- **Mothra flies, then resets.** A hit sends her to the bottom left corner, and an invisible ceiling leaves you half the screen while the enemies get all of it.

### Worth knowing

- **Passwords.** "Destroy all monsters" gives you a board with all the final monsters, "sound" is a sound test, and "start to end" plays the opening and ending without the game. Three-letter planet codes like URA drop you into Uranus at level one.
- **The setting.** The early levels are rocks with tanks, fighter jets and satellite dishes, not cities, as if Earth's defenders forgot you're on their side.
- **The creepypasta.** Sean found a story on the Wikipedia page about a haunted copy found in 2006 that turns out to be sentient, with a monster named Red and a girlfriend trapped in the game. It is too funny to be scary, and it made Sean's Essential Reading list.

### The bottom line

Skip it. We called it not essential. Joe called it "not that bad," but with too many flaws: the knockback, the lag, a crash, and hits you can't avoid. The Godzilla name made it sting more, and unnamed monsters might have gotten a pass. Sean expects to forget it quickly apart from the story.
