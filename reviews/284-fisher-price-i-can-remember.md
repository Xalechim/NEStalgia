# 284 - Fisher-Price: I Can Remember

Beam Software made this for GameTek in 1990, a Fisher-Price match game for little kids. Pick a numbered card, then another, and matching pictures become part of a bigger picture. You can play alone, against the computer, or with a friend. It is very simple, which is fine for a kid's game, and we had so little to say that we milked the forgetting jokes all episode.

### What works

- **A fine kid's idea.** Card matching helps develop that skill, and a younger brother or sister could feel like they're playing a video game while the older one plays.
- **The scoring slides.** The more cards on the screen, the more a match is worth.
- **The leaderboard.** In a three-player tournament, one player entered a new name every round and held the top three spots.

### What doesn't

- **The pictures.** We couldn't tell if a plane had an action figure's head glued on, or settle on a camcorder versus a flute. The big picture underneath is sometimes harder to make out than the small icons.
- **No card flip.** Super Mario Bros. 3 had a card flip animation for a minigame that barely shows up. Here the match game is the whole game.
- **The sound.** It is so abrasive we can't believe children were the target. Get something wrong and you will remember for a very long time.
- **Too much wrapper.** A giant copyright screen comes first, and even "I Can Remember" is trademarked. Then comes six pages of instructions, and you have to spell your name. We pictured a little brother getting this game and thinking he'd failed.
- **Odd menus.** The only way to change levels is to pick "new players, new level, new game." The computer is named Electro, a Spider-Man villain, which is not a friendly name.
- **Nothing builds.** The three levels only change the number of cards, and the payoff is a new image. We learned more from the Sesame Street games.

### Worth knowing

Our redesign would age the pictures up with the levels, from a rattle to a video game controller to a lighter, with Pac-Man style cutscenes in between. Fisher-Price is headquartered in East Aurora, New York, outside Buffalo, and Mattel bought it later. With Barbie out, maybe an I Can Remember movie is next.

### The bottom line

Skip it. We called it not essential. In a way it is a lot like Super Mario Bros. 3, minus most of what a video game is. You play it once or twice, and you probably have a deck of cards in your junk drawer already.
