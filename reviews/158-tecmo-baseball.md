# 158 - Tecmo Baseball

Tecmo made and published this in 1989, another NES baseball game from a different company rather than a sequel. It feels like a hybrid of RBI Baseball, NES Baseball and Bases Loaded. Sam joined Mike, Sean and Joe. The pitching and baserunning are good, and the batting and fielding gave us trouble.

### What works

- **Pitching.** Up, down or nothing on the D-pad sets the speed, then you pick one of eight directions, then steer a curve while the ball is in the air. It was probably the best-executed pitching of the baseball games so far.
- **Baserunning.** Probably the most fluid running system so far, and the most enjoyable. You can send a runner or call him back. Fielders get a little more leeway on timing, so double plays that felt impossible in earlier games were doable.

### What doesn't

- **Batting.** The hardest we've had to bat in an NES baseball game so far. With the camera behind the pitcher, the ball shrinks away from you, which makes it harder to time. The strike zone is "up to your nose," the umpire is a liar, and there are no difficulty settings.
- **Impossible pitches.** After about 50 rewound swings at one pitch without connecting, we decided a few pitches can't be hit.
- **Fielding.** You steer all your fielders at once, so chasing a ball with the wrong one sends everyone the same way. It finally answered why Nintendo didn't trust us to control the fielders in NES Baseball. A high-pitched whistle helped, mostly in the infield.
- **The pop fly.** Every runner takes off on his own, and if the ball is caught they are all out, because there's no getting back. Backyard Baseball has the runners go slowly and then do the right thing once it's caught.

### Worth knowing

- **Between innings.** The scoreboard sometimes just says "fight." We expected a benches-clearing brawl. Other times it says "big hit" or "Tecmo is number one."

### The bottom line

Skip it. We didn't vote it onto the Essential Games List. Sam said an NES baseball game has to be "way more arcade-y and less realistic" to be fun, and this one "simulates baseball well enough" but isn't on the list for him. Sean said it ends up "not being good enough at the only thing it can do." You're "kind of doomed" in a genre with nothing on the list if you're not the best, and RBI Baseball is still "far and away the best one on the system." This one has "slight levels of paranormal activity," the ump especially. Keep your copy, just don't put it on a Ouija board.
