# 112 - World Class Track Meet

Human Entertainment made this and Nintendo published it in 1988, but it is Stadium Events with a new logo. Mike and Sean recorded this one on their own. We accidentally played the same game twice, so how it plays barely came up, and most of the episode went to everything around it.

### Same game, new logo

- **Just a logo swap.** Apart from a repainted title screen, the mode selection is laid out the same way, with the same text. Nintendo bought Bandai's Family Fun Fitness Pad and its games, then released the Power Pad.
- **Buying it twice.** Stadium Events allegedly sold 23,000 copies, though some people think as few as 1,000. Either way, someone could have bought the same game twice. World Class Track Meet sold millions, and around 1990 or 91 it was likely your pack-in, on a cartridge with Super Mario Bros. and Duck Hunt.
- **The gas pad.** If the tagline had been "cooking with gas," they could have called it the gas pad.

### The collector sidebar

Why does a repackaged game sell for so much more? Our take: collecting has little to do with love of video games and more to do with an obsession with completion.

A Nintendo PlayStation was at $228,000 at a Heritage auction with three days left, over $300,000 with fees, and the guess was half a million. We also wondered what restoration means when a cartridge's chip degrades, the way comic books get a restored label.

### Video games in 1988

- **Accessories.** We bet anyone who bought a ROB still has him, and Nintendo never canceled him. The NES Max looks like a mistake and the curves look wrong, but it has a joystick and a turbo option for 18 bucks.
- **Missing arcade ports.** Pac-Man, Street Fighter 1 and R-Type were the ones we named.
- **The competition.** Only six Master System games were worth listing, and its box art looks like graph paper. The TurboGrafx-16 used an 8-bit CPU.
- **Nintendo being precious.** Zelda 2, Castlevania 2, Final Fantasy and Dragon Warrior were already out in Japan. We wondered about localization or quality control, and noted that almost all the garbage games have been third party.

### Worth knowing

This was episode 112, or 111 unique games if Stadium Events and this count as one. We called it basically a clip show.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean's reasoning: "It is the same game. So it will get the same score." The three-in-one cartridge with Super Mario Bros. and Duck Hunt is probably fewer than 200 episodes away, and we will review it once more.
