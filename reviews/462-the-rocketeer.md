# 462 - The Rocketeer

Realtime Associates and Ironwind Software built it, and Bandai published it in 1991, the same year as the Disney movie. Sean watched the film to prepare. The pitch is a side scrolling run and gun platformer with a jet pack, but not all the time, and that "not all the time" is the whole problem.

### The jet pack problem

- **A run and gun with a jet pack on the side.** We figured it is easier to copy a game there are hundreds of than to design one around flying. It is like the Spider-Man games before web-slinging: you are cosplaying as the Rocketeer.
- **The fuel meter is needed, and it still skips the game.** With fuel you hover over enemies and nothing matters. Super Mario Bros. 3 handles flight better, and here most levels are inside and around buildings.
- **You walk and punch.** The jet pack is the reason to play, and the game keeps taking it away.
- **One shared ammo pool.** Every weapon draws from it, fists cost nothing, and ammo is plentiful. Changing weapons with Select mid-fight is clunky.

### Fighting and difficulty

Enemies spawn right into you, and the aiming is awkward: jump and you go too high to shoot on the way down, stand and you cannot reach them. Joe found it hard and reached the second level before watching a playthrough. Sean called it kind of easy. Take a step, duck, take a step, duck worked until impatience set in. The little boss battles seemed uninspired.

### Places and cutscenes

- **The levels look good.** There is a meat freezer, a club with a stage and chandeliers, and a pretty forest. None of it changes how the game plays, but it gives a sense of progress.
- **The last two levels get bigger.** They are almost a maze, with more space to fly.
- **The cutscenes keep it short.** Nice pixel art, no long panels.

### Odds and ends

- A password, 1 6 9 9 6 3 4 9 1, unlocks a debug mode: skip the stage, or hold A to refill health and ammo.
- Realtime also made the NES Maniac Mansion port and Dick Tracy, which looked stylish but was pretty awful. George Sanger did the music, credited as the Fat Man.
- Enemies die in a purple smoke cloud, which we found funny.
- The movie cost about $40 million and opened to $9.6 million in fourth place. Pizza Hut sold a kids pack with a cup, a personal pan pizza and a paper glider.
- There is no NES sequel, though there are MS-DOS and Super NES games.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List: three votes of not essential. Sean liked it more after watching the movie, and we praised how it keeps the shape of the film without rebuilding each scene as a level. Sean was not sure it is even a Play it, and Joe found it fine only barely. It looks good, but you spend most of it walking and punching.
