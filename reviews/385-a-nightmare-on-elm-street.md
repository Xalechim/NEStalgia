# 385 - A Nightmare on Elm Street

Joe was missing, so Mike and Sean took this one on their own and blamed the dream demons. Rare developed it and LJN published it, a pairing that left us unsure what we were about to get. It is loosely based on the third movie, Dream Warriors, and you collect Freddy Krueger's scattered bones while a sleep meter ticks down. Up to four players can join with the Four Score.

### What works

- **A good idea on paper.** Like Castlevania II and Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde, it has a waking world and a dream world. Fall asleep and you can become an acrobat, a shadow warrior (a ninja) or a necromancer, each with a projectile or special move. The javelin acrobat was a favorite.
- **"Freddy's coming."** Linger in the dream world and the title card appears, followed by a full-size Freddy. That has real tension, and we could see it scaring a kid.
- **It's competent.** Everything works and the controls are tight. We just didn't enjoy it much.

### What doesn't

- **The two worlds fight each other.** The dream world is more fun and more powerful, yet you're meant to want to stay awake. Doors are locked while you sleep and the power-up icons only appear in the waking world, which at least gives you a reason to be up. Standing still makes the meter run faster, so doing nothing is a legitimate shortcut.
- **Odd enemies.** The game opens with a snake already crawling toward you, nearly as big as your teenager. Giant spiders and Frankensteins in the waking world have nothing to do with Elm Street.
- **Freddy is a letdown.** The stage bosses are chain-chomp versions of him made of beads, with a head or claws. The full-size fight has him waving a claw around. He shapeshifts in the movies, and the game uses none of that.
- **Not scary.** Nightmares make you weak, and you can't even throw a punch. Here you get a projectile and a better jump, not much more. Falling asleep mid-jump makes you feel like a narcoleptic.
- **Four players is a mess.** The HUD shows four slots no matter what, and the Angry Video Game Nerd footage Mike found looked like a "clusterfuck": stuck screens, stuck players, and everybody falling asleep when one person does.

### Beyond the game

An early concept had you playing as Freddy, which we both wanted. We also pitched a modern version where you start weak and unlock dream powers through a skill tree.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Mike said no because the game leaves you with a list of wants it never meets. Sean voted no because it feels like a franchise's coat of paint on a serviceable action platformer, with mechanics that seem tacked on. With just Mike and Sean on the episode, neither put it on the Essential Games List.
