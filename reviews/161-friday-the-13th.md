# 161 - Friday the 13th

Atlus made this for LJN in 1989: six camp counselors at Crystal Lake defend 15 kids while hunting Jason Voorhees. Sam joined Mike, Sean and Joe, and we recorded on Friday the 12th to be safe. It is frustrating, but its reputation as one of the worst NES games is overstated.

### What works

- **Jason is everywhere.** All the counselors and Jason are live on the map, so you can run into him anywhere, like Mr. X in Resident Evil 2. The imagery isn't scary, but you are so vulnerable that it gets stressful. Joe jumped when he walked into the first house and Jason was already there.
- **The Jason fight.** One hit takes eight bars of your health while his barely moves, so you throw rocks (the cabin floors are gravel) and dodge. It felt like Punch-Out, until he runs away "for plot reasons."
- **Different every time.** Counselors, the day (Jason is much faster on day two) and weapons all change things, and you can rush Jason on day one with rocks.

### What doesn't

- **Zombies and wolves.** They spawn constantly, and next to Jason it felt like two different games.
- **The cabins.** Walking and turning in the pseudo 3D rooms is awkward, notes are visible from only one angle, and the next room loads a second or two late. We split on the fix: the small rooms build tension, but side-scrolling or top-down would feel better.
- **Counselor management.** Sean read the whole manual and still didn't grasp it, so he powered up one counselor, fought Jason until that counselor was nearly dead, then handed the knife to the next.

### Worth knowing

- **The purple jumpsuit.** Jason never wore one in the movies. An unconfirmed theory is that Atlus took it from a Japanese ad for the third film. The 2017 game later added a purple jumpsuit skin, which we took as an apology.
- **The reputation.** Nintendo Power's 100th issue special put it sixth worst game of any system, and James Rolfe later said he quite likes it.

### The bottom line

Play it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List, so the drought goes on: Bionic Commando and Blaster Master were our last two, 20-plus episodes ago. Sam called it "not as bad as people say, but not essential." Sean wanted the first survival horror game on the NES and got a "camp counselor manager simulator." Joe called it ahead of its time. Mike called it a play it because it deserves a fair shake, though "nine out of 10 gamers will get frustrated."
