# 126 - MagMax

Nihon Bussan made this shmup for FCI in 1988, and all three hosts played it. You are a ship that turns into a walking robot, built from pieces you pick up. The box art promises a three-headed robotic dragon and a lightsaber. We liked the idea more than the game.

### What works

- **Building the robot.** You collect a head, legs and a cannon for the stomach. Watching your ship grow into a mech beats getting a new cannon that shoots lightning.
- **Parts are hit points.** Fully built, you can take four full shots before you die. Lose the head and the robot carries on as if it is no big deal.
- **Above and below ground.** You walk above ground and fly below it, and only the angle of the background lines changes. Above, your shots bunch into a beam aimed at the ground. Below, they spread out like a traditional horizontal shmup, and every body part is a hit box. We still preferred the music down there: relaxing, almost meditative, where up top it is a little too happy.

### What doesn't

- **The legs.** They are the silliest part. They kick at the air and don't match your speed, while the arcade version shows them planted with a shadow.
- **Over too fast.** The full robot comes quickly and is "pretty pedestrian" once you have it, which got called out because he has legs. Most power-ups only add another normal gun. We wanted mix-and-match heads and legs, Mega Man style.
- **Two buttons, one job.** A and B both shoot with no difference.
- **One boss, on repeat.** Computer Babylon, the dragon we compared to Mecha King Ghidorah, is the only boss. Dodge his bullets for about 20 seconds and the screen scrolls on without a fight. You can't really win, it is an endless loop, and the only goal is a high score.

### Worth knowing

- **The story.** Per the manual, aliens put humans under the control of Computer Babylon, and it began killing them.
- **The name.** Asked what MagMax stands for, we landed on fixing the potholes, since there are lots of them.
- **The maker.** Konami made all three of our Essential shmups: Life Force, Gradius and Stinger. This is our first game from Nihon Bussan, and there are no sequels.

### The bottom line

Skip it. We called it not essential. Joe liked building the robot but said the game got a little too quick, and that putting it on the Essential Games List would devalue the games already there. Sean called it a 25-cent game. The box art, on the other hand, is worth hanging on the wall.
