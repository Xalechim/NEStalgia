# 154 - Zelda II: The Adventure of Link

Zelda II reached North America in December 1988, two years after Japan's Famicom Disk System release. Miyamoto only produced it; Tadashi Sugiyama directed, and Tezuka and Kondo weren't involved. It began as a side-scrolling sword-and-shield idea that got a Zelda label, and Miyamoto later called it a failure. Mike, Sean and Joe were here, all new to it, and we talked for almost two hours.

### What we liked

- **Duels.** The shield is the best of the combat. Duck for low shots, stand for high, and creep toward a dagger thrower while his throws clink off you.
- **A world that surprises.** Past Death Mountain you land on the first game's map, with the graveyard and cave right where you left them. The raft takes you to a new continent.
- **The overworld fights.** Enemies show on the map and you can run, and the tile you stood on shapes the arena. Joe wasn't sold on random encounters and came around.
- **Magic and missable upgrades.** The shield spell lasts a full screen and costs little, and the up and down strikes can be missed, which made finding them feel like progress.
- **The bosses.** A knight whose armor comes off, a horseback rider, a dragon. Shadow Link was the coolest thing we'd seen on the system: your size, black on black, and reactive instead of looping. There is a known exploit of hanging out on the left, and we're fine with that.

### What we didn't

- **A dagger for a sword.** You have to hug enemies, hits seem to clink at random off shields, and we ended up jumping to strike over them.
- **Clues that aren't.** Secrets sit on single tiles, and the NPCs are "literally the strategy guide," when they aren't too cryptic. One really is named Error, another Bagu.
- **Palaces that run in a line.** Down and to the right gets you through even the Grand Palace, which is padded with filler rooms. No map, no compass, no choice, and side-scrolling is why.
- **Experience.** We split. Joe loved watching a five-hit enemy become a two-hit one, Sean was indifferent, and Mike felt Link should just grow as he goes.
- **Where magic comes from.** Old men in basements, not palaces.

### The bottom line

Play it. It did not make the Essential Games List. Joe voted yes, with the caveat that it's essential only if you play with a guide. Sean voted no. That left Mike, who called it the best, worst game we've played, and voted no too. With three voting, two yeses were needed. Mike's reasoning: it's better than Rygar, but it stumbles into being fun, and the payoff doesn't match the time it asks. We were also heading into the Best of 1988 episode, where the list could change.
