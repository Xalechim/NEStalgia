# 457 - World Champ

Pixel developed it and Visco, Romstar and Taito published it in 1991. It is a boxing game with a career mode, and we spent the first stretch of the episode on a pretend box company (ChampBoxes.ai) because we were hoping it would be about boxes. We agreed it is no Punch-Out alternative, and it never gives you much reason to keep getting better.

### Build a boxer

- **Light RPG elements.** You start as a nobody and raise speed, stamina, defense and punch through training or through the fights on your card. Training uses the same controls as fighting, so it felt like more fights, not minigames. We trained too hard before the first match, because the two menus are not clear until you hit select and toggle between them.
- **A ranking ladder with no names.** Opponents are just ranking one through six, which felt too much like a computer. You start at six, but you can challenge ranking one at any time, and losing only drops your placement. Your stats stay.
- **Passwords.** You name your fighter and get a password, and it carries into two-player. We argued over whether that is worth anything when everyone fights with the same sprite, and compared it to taking a level 99 Charizard to a friend's house.

### In the ring

- **It looks like boxing.** The camera never changes, yet the boxers circle each other on their own, the crowd fills out as you rise, a Playboy bunny holds up the round number, and the scores show between rounds. We liked that more than the older Ranking from Data East, and thought it got boxing right where Ring King did not.
- **The punching is a button masher.** Hit boxes are hard to read, and we had stretches that looked like a slap fight, with both boxers punching past each other.
- **Blocking is a guess.** One button blocks and nothing telegraphs the next punch, so there is no rock, paper, scissors. Dumping all your points into straights worked until a ranking one opponent beat that boxer.
- **Most fights end in a TKO**, which here means three knockdowns in one round.

### Odds and ends

We could not find a manual or a Wikipedia article, and a different game shows up when you search the title. A loose copy goes for about $45. The Japanese version is Great Boxing Rush Up, with real boxer names and Iron Mike as the final fight, and Europe got World Champ Super Boxing Great Fight. Here the names were swapped for Action Jim and Jumbo Joe, and there is no licensed star, unlike Mike Tyson's Punch-Out. Heavyweight Championship Boxing on the Game Boy had the build-your-boxer idea more than a year earlier.

### The bottom line

Worth a play if you like boxing games, but it is not an Essential. Joe and Sean both passed on putting it on the Essential Games List. The grinding is a nice change at first, then wears off, and Sean put it best: a solid foundation, but they forgot to build the house. Play it with a friend, because the computer opponent just gives you a battle of the stats.
