# 106 - Bases Loaded

TOSE made this for Jaleco in 1988. It is our fourth baseball game on the NES and the first to look like a TV broadcast instead of a cast of "meatball men." Sean's take on baseball as weekly Battle of Gettysburg reenactments opened the show. We loved how it looks. How it plays split us.

### What works

- **It looks like the broadcast.** The camera sits in center field behind the pitcher, even when you bat. Players look like people, umpires get names, home runs get a short animation, and a voiced announcer calls strikes and balls.
- **The catcher's mitt.** It moves to show where you're pitching, which helps you read the ball. It floats off a catcher who never moves, a funny touch.
- **Zone hitting.** You pick one of nine zones to swing at instead of chasing the ball around the batter's box. Pitches come in slower, so it is easier to read than RBI Baseball.
- **Pitching.** Four pitches on the D-pad, then a zone. A change-up that starts low and climbs got a few strikeouts in a row, until one was hit out.

### What doesn't

- **It's slow.** A game takes a long time, let alone a season, and 25 minutes might get you half of one.
- **Sluggish fielders.** You can't see fly balls, so let the outfielders auto-run until you can. A ball that gets past you feels like a guaranteed triple.
- **The umpires.** Close plays seem to go to the fielding team, so "the tie always goes to the computer." We found that a weird choice.

### The perspective split

The new view has a cost: you can't even see the bases loaded, only squares on a little map. We split on whether the new view is an improvement.

The case for it is that it is a better game than the earlier baseball ones, 10 out of 10 for NES baseball so far, with pacing that needs tweaks. The case against is that the old view behind the catcher's mitt, meatball players and all, was more fun to hit and pitch in, and the TV look could have been saved for between innings.

### Worth knowing

- **The season.** Passwords save your progress and the other teams' records. We argued over 162 games versus a nine-game round robin, and the manual we checked turned out to be RBI Baseball's. "Baseball's haunted."
- **Sequels.** Three more NES Bases Loaded games await. Asked if we're looking forward to them, the answer was "nostalgia, not basestalgia."

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. The presentation is a real step forward, but it plays slowly, and RBI Baseball did not make the list either, so neither does this.
