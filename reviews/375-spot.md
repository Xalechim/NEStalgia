# 375 - Spot: The Video Game

Spot: The Video Game drops 7-Up's red-circle mascot into a board game. Mike, Joe and Sean were on this one, and we spent a good chunk of the hour on soda, including whether anybody has seen a bottle of 7-Up lately. We expected a platformer, since Cool Spot is the Spot we remember, and booted up an Othello variant instead.

### What works

- **The rules have a twist.** It is Othello-style capture on a 7x7 board that you can carve into any shape, for two to four players. You can step one square and clone your piece, like a cell dividing, or jump two squares and leave a hole behind. Jumping into the enemy to grab a bunch of pieces also leaves a gap for them to take a whole three-by-three block of yours.
- **Spot's moves.** Any piece you move turns into Spot, who loiters, breakdances, cartwheels, does an Egypt walk left and right, and moonwalks down and left. Each direction and each jump has its own animation. We agreed it is the best thing in the game.
- **The computers play to win.** They are vicious, and protecting your interior pieces turns out to be real strategy. Pipe Dream got the "five seconds to learn" comparison.
- **Odd presentation.** The setup screen shows an NES inside a TV, and you can change the channel to a two-Spot band or swap the cartridge. At the end the view pulls back to a stadium doing the wave, where everyone in the crowd is a Spot.

### What doesn't

- **Wrong game for a mascot.** We kept asking why Spot isn't doing those tricks in a game we control. Sean's line was that it is mascot no-nos 101.
- **A board game with a skin.** A real board game could do most of this. It would just be tedious with four colors of discs, and that was the only argument we found for a video version.
- **Plain menus.** The logo has two black dots that look like googly eyes, and no sunglasses.
- **We called them lazy, then took it back.** The NES version was made in six weeks by the team behind the original, a computer game called Infection from Wise Owl Software, and it had to be redone in assembly. Sean apologized to the developers.

### Beyond the game

7-Up wanted a fast route onto the NES, saw a game full of circles, and put Spot on it. For a limited time it cost $24.99 with four 7-Up bottles. Spot's later career includes Cool Spot, Spot Goes to Hollywood and a Game Boy adventure.

### The bottom line

Skip it. All three of us said no to the Essential Games List. Joe called it a fine little take on Othello but a disappointment for a mascot this cool. If you want the board game, there are better versions, and if you want Spot, he has better games.
