# 266 - Rock 'n' Ball

KID made this for NTVIC in 1990: video game pinball with a pick-a-character screen and several extra modes. The back of the box says pinball is famous in video game history, which we found debatable. We liked the twists on pinball and found the actual pinball dull.

### Regular pinball

- **A generic board.** The single-player table has a TV, a shoe, a jacket and a burger, and it is unclear whether it means the 1950s or rock and roll.
- **Unlimited tilt.** Real machines lock the flippers and catch you cheating. This one encourages it. A ball wedged between two bumpers at the lower right and racked up score for about four minutes. The screen stops at 999,999, but the high score entry showed a far bigger number.
- **Barely any objectives.** Knock down temporary bumpers, hit the thing they unlock, and a door opens to a bonus stage. The one bonus mode is very easy.

### The characters

You pick Tom, Billy, John, Paul, Lisa or Diana. Each is a name and a sprite with a victory dance. We wanted Casino Kid style rivals with personalities. Tom has pink hair, one of the surest signs of rock and roll.

### The other modes

- **Nine ball.** A pachinko style gambling board. Lock numbers over the holes with A, bet, then land the ball in them for double or triple. With ten balls on a large board it can take forever, and the credits are useless beyond your next bet.
- **Battle Pinball.** Flippers on both sides, against the computer or a friend, in three flavors: Thunder, Bomber and Attack. Thunder is the simplest, with blockers and ramps. Bomber has a panel with a bomb, lightning that stops a flipper and a skull that removes them. Bumping the table becomes a way to ruin the other player's shot. We settled on pinball crossed with Pong.
- **Sports pinball.** Hockey and soccer, where you move a goalkeeper and your flipper spins all the way around. Left spins it clockwise, right counterclockwise, and getting it wrong means own goals. It strays furthest from pinball, and it was a favorite.

### Worth knowing

Japan got Family Pinball, with a Pac-Man themed board. Mike took the missing Pac-Man personally, and we agreed Pac-Man should have been the ball.

Sean's biggest problem is the name: only the one single-player board is remotely rock, so it should have been called Battle Pinball.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. The regular pinball it sells is lackluster, and the battle and sports modes want a friend. Joe called it a curio for pinball enthusiasts. We agreed it dropped the ball.
