# 144 - Blades of Steel

Konami made and published this in 1988, our second hockey game after Ice Hockey. Mike, Sean, Joe and Sam were all on the show. We had a good time and found the computer too easy.

### What works

- **The fights.** Three bumps and a fight starts. You can hit the chest or the head, or block, and it is some of the best side-scrolling fighting we've seen on the NES so far, better than Urban Champion. The loser is penalized and the winner stays on, with no stoppage while the refs haul him to the cage. A fight in front of the goalie earns a "weird soccer-style penalty shot."
- **Players that respond.** It doesn't feel like ice, but we agreed it isn't supposed to. Players are responsive, they glow so you know who you control, and a pass goes to a teammate instead of floating into the ether.
- **Presentation.** A Zamboni covers the whole ice on the menu screen, goal celebrations gather around, and between periods you might get a round of Gradius or an ad for Jackal or Contra.

### What doesn't

- **The computer.** It was easy at all three levels, even pro, and it falls for the same trick around the net. With a 9-0 lead, there was nothing left to do but pass the puck around until the clock ran out.
- **Long, high-scoring games.** The clock starts at 20 minutes and runs fast, so periods end up being what we imagine are five minutes. We agreed we wanted a shorter option, like two-minute periods.
- **Shooting and the goalie.** Your aim is a scrolling cursor that picks a spot when you time it, and you steer the goalie at the same time as another player, which is mind-bendy. Your goalie can't freeze the puck.
- **One player.** There is no season or career mode, just a tournament for the "quote-unquote cup." It is better against a friend.

### Worth knowing

The teams are fictional, from four Canadian and four American cities, and Buffalo isn't one, which costs points. There is no offside, but there is icing. The announcer calls every pass but not shots.

### The bottom line

Play it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List, and we had already said a sports game never would. Sam called it a good, solid game, but "This ain't it." Joe joked that Konami didn't pay us, then said there are too many better hockey games. Mike said gamers don't need our recommendation for hockey when so many versions exist.

Sean, a hockey fan, put it at the top of his own list of NES sports games and said: "It's fun and it's weird and it's mostly because it's weird."
