# 100 - Major League Baseball

Atlus made this for LJN in 1988, and it is the first licensed sports game we have played. It has 26 teams in their real uniform colors, and the back of the box sells you on being the manager. We were impressed by how much it includes and let down by how little of it matters.

### What works

- **Manager stuff.** You build your lineup, the stats are close to the previous year's real numbers, and you can pinch hit, bring in a closer, or sub position players, even though that last one changes nothing.
- **Pitching.** It was called the best part, since you control the ball through the whole pitch. Choose up, down or neither before the throw, then hold left or right to steer it inside or outside.
- **Small touches.** Hold B instead of A to tag a runner. Umpires teleport to the scene of an out, then change how many hands they hold up depending on the call.

### What doesn't

- **Batting is too easy.** Scores get out of control for both teams, and one game had about 50 hits. You can swing again after a miss, because a strike only counts once the pitch reaches the catcher. The stats barely matter: a .270 hitter and a .320 hitter felt the same.
- **Fielding is fidgety.** On an outfield pop fly you can't see your fielder until he shows up "40 miles west" of where he should be. Infield throws crawl while the computer's outfielders throw on fire.
- **The computer runs scared.** Its runners stay put at first when you throw to second, and our attempts to steal and pick off never worked.
- **Controls and menus.** The directional button is the base the runner is on, not where he is going. One wrong button in team selection dumps you to the main menu with an angry beep.
- **Sound.** The stadium is blinking lights with one guy whistling over and over, and the manual brags about a crowd roar that is "literally just static."

### Worth knowing

The box score has a spot for errors and the manual says they happen, but we never saw one. There is also an MLB challenge: exhaust all of the computer's pitchers and it gets stuck in a loop of error sounds until you shut the game off.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Asked if we would have been mad about it in 1988, Sean said he would have been impressed by the stats and the pinch-hitting minutiae, and called it a step in the right direction. Joe said it was good enough for 1988. We commend the effort, but it didn't follow through enough.
