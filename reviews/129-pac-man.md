# 129 - Pac-Man

Namco made this and Tengen released it in 1988. It is the arcade icon we all know, and we had a hard time finding anything to criticize. We enjoyed it, and nobody put it on the Essential Games List anyway.

### What works

- **Cleaning the board.** Watching the dots disappear is the appeal, and the progress is right there on screen. We compared it to mowing the lawn and looking back at what you did.
- **Simple, and elegant.** We often pick on simple games, but here all you can do is eat dots and we loved it. That is simplicity versus elegance.
- **The ghosts.** Blinky, Pinky, Inky and Clyde don't just form a conga line behind you. As we understand it, one chases, one roams and one dodges the corners you don't, which keeps the game from being way too easy.
- **Strategy.** Joe saved the big pellets as a defense when something was chasing him. Waiting against a wall for the ghosts to close in before eating a pellet drew a joke that camping is illegal in video games, then a ruling that it is legitimate.

### What doesn't

- **Turning.** The one NES-specific complaint came from Joe: turns felt unresponsive, or so precise that he missed them, and it took a sharp tap at the exact moment. Not everyone had the same experience.
- **Same maze, just faster.** The game doesn't get more complicated, everything just gets faster. Joe wanted new maze layouts, and we pointed him to Pac-Man Championship Edition DX, which does exactly that.
- **Nitpicks only.** Mike went looking for faults, like dots on every square inch and more maze exits, and admitted none of them were valid.

### Worth knowing

- **Tengen.** It is an Atari spinoff label, created to get around Nintendo's yearly limit on games per developer, and it reverse engineered the lockout chip. Nintendo told stores like Toys R Us that selling Tengen games meant no official Nintendo games.
- **Where it came from.** Every version is based on Namco's official Famicom Pac-Man from 1984, which took four years to get here. Namco released it again in 1993, so we get to do this talk again around 2028.
- **The spinoffs.** Pac-Man Plus added a can of Coca-Cola, Baby Pac-Man added a pinball table, and Professor Pac-Man is a quiz game. None sold nearly as well, and the first extra ideas weren't the right ones.

### The bottom line

Play it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Joe called it a Play it: simple, satisfying fun that doesn't make him itch to go back. Mike said Pac-Man is an essential arcade game, but "there's nothing that makes the NES version special," and you can play it anywhere. Sean echoed him.
