# 105 - R.B.I. Baseball

Atari Games made this for Tengen in 1988, built on Namco's Famicom game Pro Yaku Family Stadium. It is our third baseball game, after NES Baseball (episode two) and Major League Baseball (episode 100). Joe called it the best one we've played, by a lot, though not the best a baseball game can be on the NES.

### What works

- **Quick and controllable.** It looks like NES Baseball but is faster in pace and more fluid, and innings fly by.
- **The license trade.** It has the Players Association but not Major League Baseball, so real players sit on made-up teams. Darryl Strawberry is on the New York team, but there are no Mets. Losing team names beats losing player names.
- **Small touches.** The ball bounces off the grass, fielders might dive for it, and a throw to an empty first base sails into the distance. Double plays and pickoffs are possible. A newspaper with the box score ends the game.
- **Smarter, not superhuman.** Computer runners are still too cautious, but they will probably take the next base if they can make it.
- **Depth if you want it.** Some pitchers throw a knuckleball that is hard to read, and stamina and substitutions are in there but easy to miss. You can play casually or dig in.

### What doesn't

- **Every hit is a hit and run.** Everybody runs on contact, and you have to command runners back, which gets rough with more than one man on.
- **Fly balls are a guess.** No cursor shows where your outfielders are, and the audio cue for pop-ups doesn't work as well as it should.
- **Chaotic fielding.** Grounders are too fast to catch and throws are too slow, though it is far less noticeable than in Major League Baseball.
- **Flat presentation.** Home runs get a few fireworks, and the fans are tiny dots that shake like flies. The music is the worst part of the game: one tone, insanely repetitive.

### Worth knowing

Sean opened by explaining baseball: 18 people designate each other batsmen and pitchmen, and the pitchmen throw balls from a tall hill.

This is the licensed gray cart. Tengen also put out an unlicensed black cart, and we are saving that fight with Nintendo for our first unlicensed game.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. We called it a middle-of-the-run, perfectly functioning NES game. If you have played it for years it stays fun, which we don't think Major League Baseball and NES Baseball have going for them. Baseball games keep getting better, so an Essential NES one is hard, though not impossible. Asked how close this came, we gave arbitrary numbers from 25 to 40 percent.
