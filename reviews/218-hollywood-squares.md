# 218 - Hollywood Squares

Rare made this for GameTek in 1989: a tic-tac-toe game show where each square holds a celebrity with an answer. Instead of a traditional episode, Mike hosted a round on the mic, playing all nine celebrities with deliberately weak zingers, while Sean and Joe were the contestants. Joe won. The format works, but there isn't much game.

### What works

- **No typing.** It only asks true or false, which suits the NES better than Jeopardy, where one typo costs you. No computer player smashes the keyboard into gibberish when it misses, either.
- **The tic-tac-toe twist.** Get it wrong and the other contestant takes your box, so a solved game gets some risk. That twist was the hook, more than the trivia.
- **A deep question pool.** The GameFAQs list kept scrolling, and our guess was about 2,000 questions. Nobody hit a repeat.
- **The contestants.** Two girls and two boys who differ by shirt color and grimace like they would rather be elsewhere. It matches the vibe.

### What doesn't

- **No celebrities.** Hollywood Squares is about the celebrities, and these are random people with generic names. It is Trivial Pursuit with a tic-tac-toe board. We pitched parody stars, or Rare's own characters, like the slalom butt guy.
- **A coin flip.** It is always a 50/50 guess, which felt a little unfair in our favor.
- **Shaky trivia.** Some answers come from magazine articles, so truth is not set in stone. Whether it is ever a good idea to put vodka on your eyeglasses doesn't feel like trivia. Another asked if being diddled is good or bad (bad, since the dictionary says swindled).
- **Fake prizes.** The car is a key matched to a car that differs only in color, and the Alps trip is a congratulations screen. We suggested turning prizes into points, such as a Hawaii trip worth 25,000.

### Worth knowing

The only sequel is on the Nintendo Wii, with real celebrities like Kathy Griffin and Jeffrey Tambor. You can still buy it new for $12.99.

We never settled the Secret Square. After YouTube clips and a call to someone's mom, we pieced it together: it is revealed to the home audience, pays bonus money for a right answer, and the money carries over if you miss. We still don't know if the celebrities lie or just get it wrong.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Joe won the right to decide the Essential Games List vote alone, and he said it is not essential, with a nod for the number of questions. Sean agreed. Joe called it more viable than Jeopardy, though Sean, as the loser, still thought Jeopardy was the better game.
