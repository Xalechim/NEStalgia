# 292 - Vegas Dream

HAL Laboratory made this and HAL America published it in 1990. You are a tourist who walks into a casino with $700 and tries to reach $10 million, playing blackjack, roulette, two slot machines and keno. Mike and Sean played it; Joe was not on this one. We expected a Casino Kid sequel or a Vegas RPG and got a fever dream: plain casino games with odd little stories wedged in.

### What works

- **The encounters.** Between bets, people approach with a choice: a woman who wants to go to a show, a stranger who needs $200. Then the news reports the result, always about you. It is very weird and we liked it.
- **The scammer.** A man in sunglasses praises your luck and buys you a drink. You either get a ring to auction off, or you fall down the stairs and the ring is gone. We decided he pushed you and took the ring back.
- **The endings.** Lose everything and you board a plane home, arguably the better ending, since you keep your soul. Reach $10 million and you lounge by your pool, demand "the best beverage," threaten to fire the help, and the game congratulates you for becoming a jerk.

### What doesn't

- **Every encounter is a coin flip.** Yes means a win or a scam. Marriage is just saying yes to everything, nothing unlocks, and ours was a $200 scam.
- **Easy money outside the casino.** High-low on the keno board doubles your bet per right guess. Half a million became about $8 million on one screen.
- **Missing basics.** Blackjack hides your total until the hand ends, slots drop you back to max bet, and roulette has no re-bet option for the chips you keep on the same squares. The wheel animation is great once, then too long.
- **Keno.** Pick up to 15 numbers out of 80, it draws 20, and you just watch. The manual prints payoff odds, but the game doesn't show them while you play.

### Worth knowing

Be careful early, when $200 is a lot. Once you are rich, say yes to everything except the show, which takes half your money. HAL's SNES follow-up, Vegas Stakes, lets you walk around the casino and swaps keno for craps.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. The test was whether it beats Casino Kid, which also missed the list. Mike says the games are no real improvement and misses the tell system. Sean thinks it is better, but not by much, since it captures the guilt of a casino. As the closing PSA said, the house always wins and keno has terrible odds.
