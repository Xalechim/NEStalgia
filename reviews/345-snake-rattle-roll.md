# 345 - Snake Rattle 'n' Roll

Rare's isometric snake platformer snuck up on us. Mike, Sean and Joe expected a title that "just doesn't really stand out" on a list, and found a polished, charming game with an awkward camera. We also spent the opening minutes debating whether Rattle and Roll have last names, which tells you how much lore the manual provides.

### Why it won us over

- **Eat, grow, weigh in.** You start as a snake head, swallow nibbly pibblies until you are long enough, and hit the scale to open the exit. Ten stages and a Bigfoot boss keep changing the loop so it never goes stale.
- **A gentle start.** Early stages let you learn the jumping without much punishment, and a hit costs you part of your tail, like losing rings in Sonic. There is no health bar.
- **The shadow.** A crisp circle under you eases the height problem every isometric game has. It never saved a first jump, but it taught us what we could land on afterward.
- **The attitude.** Toilet seats (we first took them for clams), stomping feet, bodybuilders, and a shark whose music ramps up the second you hit water. Mike had a Banjo-Kazooie flashback.
- **Two players.** Joe played co-op and loved it: you combine your weight on the scale, and you shout at your partner to go eat more before you can climb.
- **Controls that click.** Pressing down sends you down and to the right, which takes a while to rewire. We all ended up tilting the controller. After that it felt smooth, close to Marble Madness, and ahead of its time.

### What gets in the way

- **Depth perception.** Some stretches look squishy, and you can't tell whether you missed a jump or it was impossible.
- **Hidden fall deaths.** A set drop distance kills you, and it is shorter than it looks.
- **The fake power-up.** It reverses your controls. We mostly set the controller down until it wore off, and it takes a minute afterward to trust that things are normal.

### Beyond the game

The final stage goes gray, so we assumed it was the moon, where a Bigfoot with a flag tries to claim it. A UFO then takes you to a "Snakes in Space" title card. That sequel never happened, and the Game Boy follow-up was Sneaky Snakes. We kept coming back to wasted branding: cute characters and nothing built around them.

### The bottom line

Essential. Mike was conflicted, said his heart said no, then talked himself into a yes: a fun, short-lived, harmless catalog entry that still stands out. Sean voted yes because it is unique and polished, and he had less to complain about than usual. Joe said it was a close call but he could not vote against something he loved. We all voted it onto the Essential Games List. Give the controls real time before you judge them.
