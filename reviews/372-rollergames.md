# 372 - RollerGames

Most of this episode is about the TV show. RollerGames was a 1989 televised, theatrical take on roller derby, with a figure-eight track, an alligator pit for tie-breaking sudden death, and teams like the T-Birds, the Violators, the Maniacs, Bad Attitude, the Rockers and Hot Flash. It lasted one season and ended in bankruptcy. Mike and Sean were the only two hosts this week, and we watched the first two-hour episode and loved it far more than the game.

### The show we couldn't stop talking about

- **Peak 80s.** Feathered hair, a heartless entertainment lawyer for a manager, a biker who worships a head in a box, and an alligator handler named Animal who seemed angry on the gators' behalf. Cheerleaders joined the fights, and the athletes looked less athletic than the cheerleaders.
- **A conflict of interest.** Commissioner Bill Griffith Sr. runs the league while Bill Griffith Jr. manages the Los Angeles T-Birds.
- **Hard to follow.** It cuts from action shot to action shot, so you never see enough to learn the rules. Points for passing skaters are real, and the wall of death is worth more.

### What the game does

- **It isn't the sport.** It's a side-scrolling action game starring the show's teams, with the managers as bosses, and the plot has an alien helping the Eastern Empire teams kidnap Commissioner Griffith. Odd levels are beat-em-up stretches where the screen locks and enemies fly in. Even levels are platforming around barrels, flamethrowers and helicopter bombs.
- **The skating feel is the good part.** Momentum makes it feel like you're on skates, and it matters most when platforming. Mostly it fades in the fights, which only go left or right.
- **Your pick of skater is cosmetic.** You choose a team and a character from each, and the difference turned out to be attack animations and nothing else. The Hot Flash skater only felt faster.
- **Short and not overbearing.** The knee-strike move is a highlight. Bosses just soak up damage, and the worst has a boss jumping at you while a trident pokes up through the grate below.

### Beyond the game

There is an arcade game that is much closer to the sport, in an effect-heavy four-on-four mode with a spinning background to sell speed and fighting-game one-on-one scraps. We watched a long-play and we'd like to find a cabinet. The 1999 TNN show Roller Jam used inline skates and a banked oval track, and played itself as a real sport.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Both of us voted it off the Essential Games List. The game is a baseline functional video game that gives skates and almost nothing else, and the sport is barely there. We joked that even Double Dragon with Billy and Jimmy on skates would be better, since the real problem is that they added skates, did little with them, and didn't make a good beat-em-up. Our score: 416 out of 1,000, in the form of a match in which the show beats the game 38 to 13.
