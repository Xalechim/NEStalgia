# 444 - Ski or Die

Ski or Die is Skate or Die with the sport swapped, and we spent a good part of the episode asking why. Mike, Sean and Joe were on this one. Rodney Dangerfield is back running the hub, you ski around a menu to pick an event, and then you do very little skiing. Five events, some good, some baffling, and an "or die" series that ended here.

### The five events

- **Snowboard half pipe.** The best of the bunch. It felt like snowboarding, the scrolling toward you is readable and smooth (it reminded us of SSX Tricky), and turning spins your rider around so the inputs make sense. The sprites that start as punk penguins and turn out to be rabbits with chainsaws are a trap.
- **Snowball blast.** You are a snowball turret behind an ice wall, flanked from four sides, with a radar showing how many kids stand in each direction. It packs a lot of information onto the screen and the kids really do move from side to side. It also happens too fast: turn away and three kids fill the gap. We wanted buckshot, a rocket launcher, and a wall that crumbles like the shields in Space Invaders.
- **Downhill blitz.** The only real skiing, with a nice touch in the tracks you leave in the snow. Dead ends everywhere, and nobody tells you that you can jump for what feels like three minutes of hang time. The default controls feel reversed until you find the "goofy" option.
- **Acro aerials.** We never scored above a 3.5 and kept doing the same split trick while the judges threw their helmets. Of everything here it felt most like the winter-games code you have to crack.
- **Inner tube thrash.** Lazy-river pace, pickups from pocket knives to dinner forks, and the finish-line winner takes 1,000 points while passing someone is worth 100. It reads like one layout repeated, something that could have been built in a day.

### Worth knowing

The manual calls the penguins "punk penguins," which sent us off wondering what makes a penguin punk. The chainsaw toad and bunnies are worth "a face transplant" if you are not careful. There was also a fair amount of Tootsie Pop and snowflake philosophy, none of it useful.

### The bottom line

Skip it. In the Essential Games List segment we all voted no. Joe called it up there with his favorite sports-compilation games, which tells you how low that bar sits, but even the half pipe and the snowball fight left him wanting a whole game built around just one of them. Sean thought it a fine, serviceable game to own, and nobody could shake how strange it was to go from skating to skiing and keep the Rodney Dangerfield characters.
