# 427 - Peter Pan and the Pirates

Sean was out, so this was Mike and Joe. The full title is Fox's Peter Pan and the Pirates: The Revenge of Captain Hook, and it is based on the 1990 Fox cartoon, with no relation to the Disney movie. Our first impression was that it felt like an NES bootleg, and by the end we couldn't argue with that.

### What's wrong

- **No weight, no feedback.** Flying and jumping share a pose, with no animation on either. Peter's knife is smaller than his hand, you can't tell when a stab connects, and touching an enemy drains health continuously for as long as you stand there.
- **A pause that doesn't pause.** No pause sound, and the music keeps going.
- **Flight.** You press jump and up together, there is no real diagonal, and landing takes a while. We kept flying by accident while jumping.
- **Repeats.** You can't leave a stage until every pirate is dead, which keeps you from flying over everything. When we first noticed a stage repeating we assumed the enemies had moved. They hadn't: same layouts, same placements, new palette. Maybe three or four stages are original.
- **The interface.** Joe and his friend Mark called the health icons telephones, and they turned out to be treasure chests. The brown-haired head that looks like Peter is the pirates remaining. The manual says the flight bags are food. The music is one thin beep going up and down.

### What works, a little

- **Easy, in your favor.** Collect enough chests and you can brute-force it, which beats games where the jank is also brutal. There are no continues or passwords, but you barely need them. A cage trap that falls on you is mostly funny and sometimes sends you to a bonus round of health pickups.
- **Cannonballs.** They sit in the level and you can fire them only while touching them. We came around to liking that more than a limited supply.
- **The ending.** You can't just stab Hook. You knock him off the ship down the plank, and the crocodile gets him. The pirate ship stage has the best pixel art in the game.

### Beyond the game

Equilibrium, an American studio with nothing else to its name, developed it, and THQ published it as its first release. Tim Curry voiced Hook on the show. Disney sued Fox over it and lost, then bought Fox Kids Worldwide in 2001, and there is no legal way to watch the show now.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Joe was plain: it kept him curious, it was more fun with a friend to laugh at it with, and he never wants to play it again, so not even Play it. Mike thought it feels like something that could run on an Atari 2600 with stick figures. It did not go on the Essential Games List. If anything gets a reissue, we'd pick the show.
