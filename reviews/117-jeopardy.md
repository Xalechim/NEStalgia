# 117 - Jeopardy!

Rare made this for GameTek in 1988, and it is the first Jeopardy! video game. It does what the show does and we found little to fault, but it is still just Jeopardy!. For the episode we played a real game: Mike hosted, Sean and Joe were the contestants, and Joe won. There was no prize.

### What works

- **It feels like the show.** The categories read like real Jeopardy! ones, not general kid stuff, and the answers are often a play on the category name. It is not as hard as TV Jeopardy!, but a $500 clue is harder than a $100 one.
- **Real wagering.** The Daily Double hides behind one clue in the first round and two in Double Jeopardy, and you wager before you see the question.
- **A friend makes it work.** Against another person it is just trivia, and it works surprisingly well. The game tells the other player not to look at the screen while someone types, which is funny because there is no other way to do it.

### What doesn't

- **No time limit.** Unlike the show, you have to answer every clue in both rounds. That was too many questions for one game, and against the computer there was a point of thinking "come on, please tell me they're going to cut me off at some point."
- **Picky typing.** Leave the E out of Blarney and you are marked wrong. Joe's Agent 99 answer was rejected even though the manual says numbers, Roman numerals or spelled-out numbers work.

### Worth knowing

- **Odd computer misses.** When the computers are wrong they type gibberish with some of the right letters, like hangman. It is clever, but it can tip you off, which real Jeopardy! would not.
- **Three players.** Players one and three share controller one and player two needs a second, so it is technically the first three-player NES game, and one controller still locks you out.
- **Rare's other work.** They made nine game show games for the NES, Double Dare included.
- **Our Final Jeopardy.** The category was game designers. Sean wagered his entire $1,200 and answered Hideo Kojima, though Metal Gear was not in the clue. Joe wagered $1 and answered Konami. Both were wrong.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean called it the best Jeopardy! that can exist on the NES, but even a perfect Jeopardy! game would never be essential. Joe expected it to be atrociously boring and found it works with a friend. Sean's pick for a memorable game show game is Who Wants to Beat Up a Millionaire on the Dreamcast.
