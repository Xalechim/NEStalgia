# 455 - Nobunaga's Ambition II

Koei made and published this one, and Joe was out trying to conquer Japan, so it was just Mike and Sean. It is the third Nobunaga on the Famicom but the second to reach North America, which is why it gets a "2" here. You pick a daimyo and try to conquer all 38 fiefs, and conquest is only a third of the job: taxes, rice harvests, floods, peasant riots and mutinous soldiers fill the rest.

### What the sequel adds

- **400 real samurai.** Each has a portrait and stats: age, political influence, war ability, charisma, ambition, loyalty, troop count and more. Ages follow real birth years, and one who allegedly died at 47 dies at 47, battle or not.
- **Two scenarios.** In the warlord rivalry one there are 28 daimyo, nobody holds more than four fiefs, and the map is wide open. In the Nobunaga's Ambition scenario Oda already owns almost everything.
- **A new coat of paint.** The art is brighter, there are some animations, and combat zoomed out into tiles instead of big card-like squares. There is also a day-night cycle that changes how much of the map you see. It feels more update than sequel.

### Where it fights you

- **Siloed screens.** Your stats are on one page, the rest of the world is on another that takes a minute to load, and you almost need a notepad to cross-reference them.
- **Dead buttons.** Start and Select do nothing in a game with this many menus. Once you pick any generals for a battle, the back-out button becomes the move-forward-and-fight button.
- **Menus over the map.** A unit's menu covers half the battlefield and can hide the enemy you are trying to reach.
- **An off-by-one bug.** The samurai view lists Kozuke Province 2 as Province 1, while the daimyo view has it right.
- **Battles that run 30 minutes or more.** Units get few moves, and reaching a fort drops you onto another map to cross.

### Tricks from the guides

After a battle you can behead, recruit or release a captured samurai. Release just sends him to another army, and a samurai with bad stats can be handed one soldier and sent to charge so he dies instead of being captured. Selling a big pile of food drops the market price, so you can buy it back cheaper and repeat for infinite money.

### Odds and ends

The Switch got it on Hamster's Console Archives for $7.99 with save states and remappable buttons. Pokemon Conquest on the DS is Pokemon plus Nobunaga's Ambition in Japan. A speedrun by Aaron2U2 runs two hours and nine minutes, and a normal playthrough takes at least six hours.

### The bottom line

Play it, but only for people who already like this kind of game on this kind of hardware. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List, since the computer versions came first and are better, and the novelty of seeing something this complex on the NES has worn off. It is better than the first game in every way except menu legibility, so the first Nobunaga's Ambition drops from Play it to Skip it, and this is the clearest of the Koei games we have played.
