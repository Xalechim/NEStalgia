# 113 - Adventure Island

Hudson Soft made and published this 1988 platformer, which Mike called a Super Mario Bros. knockoff in many ways: Caveman Mario crosses eight worlds of four stages each, with a boss at the end of each world. The controls get close to Mario. Then it repeats itself until the fun wears off.

### What works

- **It handles like Mario.** Let go of the D-pad and you slide a little, which feels natural and helps you time jumps. A standing jump is low, a running jump is high, and pressing both buttons cheats. It is heavier than Mario, with a high arc and a fast fall, so landing between two enemies is trickier.
- **Colorful and relaxed.** The graphics are silly, the palette is closer to Super Mario Bros. 3 than most NES games we play, and the music is fantastic.
- **The pause sound.** It feels final, like you may never start the game again. We joked that pausing should power off the system.

### What doesn't

- **The meter.** It acts as your life, drains slowly, and tripping over a rock takes a chunk. Only fruit refills it, and the fruit pops into view late. Everything else kills you in one hit, so the meter felt like an extra layer on a laid-back game.
- **Same boss, new head.** Every fight is jump up, hit the head. Nothing before the bosses flashes when you hit it, so it is easy to throw hammers at the body for a long time. Bowser got more complicated each world. This guy never gains new powers.
- **The same level, again.** Later worlds recycle exact layouts with a few more enemies. There is no sense of progress, and only the checkpoint number tells you where you are.
- **The skateboard.** It gives you speed and an extra hit, but you can't stop moving. The game needed new enemies or a double jump, and we said this makes you worse at things.

### Worth knowing

- **Wonder Boy.** We said it is pretty much a direct port of the Sega arcade game. Hudson Soft had the rights to use the game but not the name.
- **16-shot.** The hero is based on a Hudson Soft employee who could hit the A button 16 times in a second.
- **The ending.** The box says you rescue Princess Leilani, but the ending congratulates you on saving your lovely Tina. We joked that we got the wrong island.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Joe thought early on that it would be essential, but the repetition took over. Mike's point: cut it to four worlds and it still would not add on to itself, because the first four stages could be the entire game.
