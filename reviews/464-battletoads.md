# 464 - Battletoads

Rare made it, Tradewest and Masaya published it, and it came out in 1991 looking more polished than any first entry we have covered. It also arrives with a reputation as one of the hardest games on the NES, and it still managed to surprise us.

### What is great

- **Combos that end big.** You just mash the attack button, and each chain finishes with something bigger: a boot for a foot, a huge fist, a bell swinging on the rappel stage. No Street Fighter 2 style inputs, and we called them the best combos we have had so far.
- **It looks like it came from a design bible.** The spaceship and the characters all seem built to be reused. The tall walker enemies lean away when you hit them, and we had not seen the NES bend things like that.
- **Bosses with ideas.** The first one puts you in its crosshairs, then leaves ammo you throw back, and the screen cracks. Later bosses are fun concepts, but easier than the levels before them.
- **The final tower.** A rotating tower with a frog jumping around it is new to the NES, and it looks smooth.

### The cliff

- **Stage three is a wall.** Level one teaches you to pick things up, level two is easy rappelling, then the jet bike arrives at "trance like club music speeds." It is a first boxing class followed by a professional boxer.
- **Depth is hard to read.** The hit boxes are not tight, and we slid off platforms we were standing in the middle of.
- **Ice level.** Slippery platforms, a snowman throwing snowballs, and one hit sends you back down. You must kill him and throw his snowballs at a wall.
- **Out of order.** The snake level is the one we liked, a bit like Super Mario World, and it should have been stage three.
- **Cheap moments.** When the cog breaks, about a dozen white balls spray across the screen and any touch counts as a hit.
- **A secret warp.** Hitting one of the walls in the bike section skips you ahead to the surfboard stage.

### Two players

You play at the same time, and friendly fire cannot be turned off. When one of you dies, you both restart the stage, but the dead toad comes back with full health and the other keeps what they had, so you are never both at full health again.

### Odds and ends

The working title was Amphibians with a Z, and it began as a hand-drawn, Disney style game before it got more violent. Mark Betteridge designed some levels and got frustrated because he could not beat them.

### The bottom line

Not essential. Joe voted it onto the Essential Games List, because the first three levels are a blast and the design is intricate, like a Kaizo run. Mike and Sean voted no: it is adversarial, and with only three continues a mistake in level six sends you to the beginning. With a few more seconds to react it would have been a no-brainer.
