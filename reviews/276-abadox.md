# 276 - Abadox

Natsume made this for Milton Bradley in 1990, and it goes hard in more ways than one: a tiny armored pilot flies through the insides of a planet-sized alien. It was our first Natsume game. The art is gnarly and the difficulty is punishing.

### What works

- **Body horror, fully committed.** Flying eyeballs replace the usual enemy ships, and walls of fleshy membrane turn into grabbing hands. The first boss is a skeleton with its brain still attached and a third eye. We couldn't agree whether it was a cow, a wolf or a horse-dog.
- **Vertical stages.** Some levels run vertically through intestine-like passages, including one where the path splits in two.
- **Tight spaces.** The room shrinks as you go, and it nudged us to hang back and manage what is ahead.
- **Power-ups.** You can hold every type, including the orbiting "shield balls," and a new pickup doesn't bump an old one. Base fire can be overwritten, though.
- **Continues.** Unlimited, and they restart you on the stage you died on.

### What doesn't

- **One hit costs everything.** You lose every power-up and restart from a checkpoint. Fully powered it isn't that hard, but getting there is, and then you are too nervous to stay there.
- **Foreground tricks.** Some obstacles can be shot away and others are in front of you, and a few used the same tiles as the walls.
- **Cheesed bosses.** Joe beat nearly every boss he reached by finding a spot where no bullets landed and shooting from it. A couple did move. He doesn't feel he really beat them.
- **The escape.** After the last boss you squeeze through walls on a screen faster than anything before. We split on whether that feels misplaced or a fitting payoff for being eaten at the start.

### Worth knowing

In the year 5,012, a parasite called Parasitis eats the planet Abadox, destroys the galactic military and swallows a hospital ship carrying Princess Maria. You are the last fighter, sent in to rescue her. We asked why a princess was needed.

We also imagined an Abadox Twister, with a mat shaped like a cube you go inside.

### The bottom line

Play it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Mike called it one of the best shmups on the NES but held it back. Joe found it good and polished, a play if you like shmups but not a must play. Sean said it stands above most shmups we've played and might suit an Essential shmups list, but called it incredibly frustrating. We kept coming back to what happens when you die and how far back you're pushed.
