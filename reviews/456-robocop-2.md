# 456 - RoboCop 2

Painting by Numbers developed it, Data East and Ocean published it, and Mike, Sean and Joe were all there. The big news is that RoboCop can finally jump, which we asked for in the first game. It turned out to be a monkey's paw wish, because the game gets slippery in every direction.

### Moving like he is covered in sludge

- **Slide on every surface.** Starting is as slow as stopping, so you crawl for a moment, then ramp up. We said it felt like the sliders were cranked up to 11, with short precision platforms and no runway to build speed.
- **No going back.** High and low routes lock each other out, and you cannot return for what you missed. Combined with the slide, a platform may not leave enough room for a running start to the thing you wanted.
- **It does not get better.** You just get used to it. Nintendo Power's April 1991 issue said RoboCop is initially difficult to control, and we agree.

### The quota

You hunt the drug nuke, arrest criminals and rescue hostages, and you need 60% of the nuke and 60% of the arrests to move on. That number is never shown until the stage ends, and you cannot tell how many things the level holds. Some stages open with a warning about a secret nuke cache, which counts toward the percentage and is easy to miss. Arrestable enemies flash on screen and blink a tiny indicator at the top, but you arrest them by walking into them in a game that trains you to shoot everything.

### The bonus stages

Miss the quota and you land in a Hogan's Alley style shooting gallery, played with a D-pad and just as slippery. Treat the gun like a machine gun and fire as you sweep across the targets. It is the one place the slipperiness works. There is also an optional slide puzzle that restores RoboCop's memories, which we liked as extra content. Game over is RoboCop being taken away for calibration.

### The robot and the moons

RoboCop 2 himself is a hulking final boss with great sprite work, and the odds feel stacked against you. Before him comes a last level with a moon every 30 steps or so, too many moons for us.

### Odds and ends

The cartridge is built to hang if a Game Genie is attached. In the movie, RoboCop walks into an arcade full of Data East cabinets: Bad Dudes, Sly Spy, Heavy Barrel, Midnight Resistance, The Real Ghostbusters and Kung Fu Master. The arcade version is a brawler that looks great and keeps RoboCop from being slippery, and we wish this had been that port.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. The quota hides its own win conditions, the movement is bad, and the platforming is complicated for that control. It is playable and entertaining for a little while, but more frustrating than fun, and RoboCop 1 is the better game. We are still getting RoboCop 3.
