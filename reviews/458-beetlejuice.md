# 458 - Beetlejuice

Rare developed it and LJN published it in 1991, and Mike, Sean and Joe were all there. We have all seen the movie, and we spent a while on the animated series and the sequel. You play as Beetlejuice, alternating side-scrolling levels with top-down ones, and the game is loosely based on the 1988 film with more of the cartoon's zaniness. Our first note was that it is jank, and it stayed jank.

### The scare economy

- **Points are currency.** Enemies give you points, which the game calls help tickets, and the shrunken head runs a shop where you spend them on scares. A beetle is worth 25 points and a scare costs about 150 to 500.
- **Nothing is permanent.** Scares wear off, so you stock up before you pass a shop. The Skeleton is the most expensive and mandatory for the first boss, and it lasts two or three fireballs. Eight of them is a lot of grinding.
- **Beetle stomping.** Harmless beetles spawn outside the shops so you cannot get soft locked, and you have to stomp them with a stomp button. Beetlejuice looks like a frustrated regular guy missing a bug. A Let's Play we watched spent about 12 minutes on it.
- **Bosses are invincible.** You have to knock them off the stage to the right, and the game never says so. Hit one from the wrong side and you get no feedback at all. We only got past the first one after a Let's Play.

### The platforming

The jump is as high and light as a feather, then you fall like a rock, and it is hard to course correct in the air. The level geometry is the real enemy. Torches that look like background will kill you, while other things that look solid let you fall through, and the edges of the screen are deadly too. The one nice touch is the death screen, where his bones explode apart. We also hit flickering and a sprite that vanishes and pops back.

### The house

The top-down levels are more exploratory, and the people in the house send you on fetch quests while blocking the way you need. Beetlejuice will not leave a room until the enemies are cleared, which feels right for him. But the house is an endless labyrinth, and we wanted a mundane, real place with monsters in it. The final level, the bureaucratic afterlife, is the part that feels most like the movie. The ending is anticlimactic.

### Missing the movie

The lines are flat exposition on a black screen, like a fire extinguisher that "ought to put out a few fires." When he walks down the screen his face is just a capital M. The music is bright and cheery, and no Danny Elfman.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. The scares system is cool on paper, and we liked that points are a currency, but it all ties together as a huge mess, and it is hard for bad reasons. The Game Boy game, Horrific Hijinks, is also from Rare, based on the cartoon, and Destructoid called it sluggish.
