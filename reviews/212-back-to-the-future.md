# 212 - Back to the Future

We like the movie, and Joe especially loved it as a kid. The LJN game mostly isn't the movie. It's a skateboard obstacle course broken up by a few bonus scenes, and those scenes are the only parts that feel like Back to the Future.

### What the game does

- **Street stages.** Marty skates down an auto-scrolling street, dodging people, birds, sewer gaps and the guy who chases him, while collecting clocks so the family photo doesn't fade. We compared it to Paperboy without the newspapers. The clocks sit right in your path, so there's no real pressure, and buying time isn't from the movie anyway.
- **Skateboard and bowling balls.** The board speeds up the scroll and doubles as an extra hit. The bowling balls make no sense for Marty, and we joked that everything should just be a clock, or that he should carry the Zapper after the Wild Gunman scene in Part 2.
- **Diner.** You fling milkshakes at Biff's gang with no meter and no stated goal. It let us through somewhere around 37 bullies, and nothing told us that. When one reaches you, he slides Marty across the counter and through the door, the best animation in the game.
- **Classroom.** You block Lorraine's zigzagging hearts, which reads like a Simpsons parody of a video game.
- **Dance.** You catch notes with the guitar. Once we noticed sharp is high, bass is low and the note is middle, it got easy.
- **The DeLorean.** The last stage has you dodge lightning at a top speed of 88, and the bolts have big hitboxes. It ends on a text box that says congratulations.

### What lets it down

- **One level, many times.** The street is cut into six pieces with a map screen between each, as if you'd finished something. No new enemies show up; the sky just gets darker.
- **Where's Doc?** He isn't in the game. He could have handed out tips or upgrades between levels.
- **Two songs.** The Power of Love is sped up so much we joked it was dodging a copyright claim, and the other is Johnny B. Goode.

### Beyond the game

Three earlier Back to the Future games exist, including a Japanese text adventure and an MSX version where Marty is the size of a Goomba. We also touched on the Telltale game and the Universal ride on GameCube, and Joe spent the post-show on how the movies break their own time travel rules.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean said he could feel himself fading away like the photo, and Joe said he'd have been let down even as a kid. We figure we did everyone a favor: the episode is better than the game, so if you want to play it, listen to us instead.
