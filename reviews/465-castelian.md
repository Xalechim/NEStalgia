# 465 - Castelian

Bits Studios made the NES version, which was published by Triffix, Hiro and Storm Sales Curve in 1991. It is a rework of a 1987 home computer game called Nebulous, and you play Julius, a little Anurian hybrid who climbs cylindrical towers and then bombs them. We did not expect a spinning tower to feel so unremarkable, but we had just seen one in Battletoads.

### Julius and his towers

- **The trick.** The tower turns as you walk around it, with platforms, doors and elevators to get you higher. It is neat on the NES, but it mostly hides what is coming next, and it looked better in Battletoads.
- **The bomb.** Julius plants it at the top, and the tower crumbles as he heads back down. You cannot really see the bomb, so it just looks like the screen scrolling.
- **The enemies.** Hexalons, eyeballs, spheres and dissolving pathways, which we summed up as being attacked by math. A molecule enemy chases you if you stay put too long, and you cannot outrun it.

### The controls

- **One button, two jobs.** A fires and also jumps, but only while you walk forward. B does nothing. Our theory is that being able to jump and shoot together would have trivialized the game.
- **Passenger mode.** On an elevator you cannot jump or shoot, and sometimes it pushes you into an enemy.
- **Invisible rules.** Disappearing platforms look like the others, and the slide on the first stage has only a sound, no visual cue.

### The clock

Enemy hits just drop you to a lower platform, which is fair. The real enemy is time: we got half a step from the final door and ran out. Level three requires you to be knocked down by the molecule, which we had to look up.

### Odds and ends

The level names kept us busy: Tower of Eyes, Realm of Robots, Trap of Tricks, Slippery Slide, Broken Path, and Swimmer's Delight. Tower 7 has no doors. Only four enemies can be on screen at once. In Japan it became Kyoro-chan Land, with the Choco Ball parrot as the lead, passwords and a pause menu. Nebulous won best original game at the Golden Joystick Awards for 1987, and it was also called Tower Toppler on the Atari 7800.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody made a case for the Essential Games List. The puzzles never ask for deep thought, you just find the path as you go, and the platforming feels like filler. Julius has his defenders, but the game is forgettable. It earned its award as a point of history, and we are glad to have seen it once.
