# 463 - Times of Lore

Toho put the NES version out in 1991, three years after the computer original. It is an original fantasy world called Albareth with no license behind it. It plays less like an RPG and more like a giant fetch quest and a talking simulator, with a candle for a health bar.

### Living in the world

- **A world that goes on without you.** There is a day and night cycle, and NPCs keep routines. One early quest guy was asleep, so we killed enemies for several real-time minutes waiting for morning.
- **Quests that just happen.** You catch a spy by waiting at the gate at night, and the spy looks like every other guard. Others include a camp of orcs and holy water for a zombie king.
- **It is unforgiving.** Fail or kill the wrong person and you can keep playing but cannot win, so save often or use a guide.

### The controls

- **One button talks, the same button stabs.** Open the menu with B first, then press A to talk. Press A first and you stab them and they turn on you. We killed friendly people by accident, and one experiment was killing the whole town.
- **Turning around takes forever.** The walk is slow and shuffly, and the uneven scroll made us feel sick.
- **Dialogue that looks at you first.** Every line opens with a person looking at you and saying something, then says something very short. The opening quotation marks are missing, which became a running joke.

### The candle and other oddities

The health candle sits in the bottom right and melts slowly as you take damage or go hungry. It looks cool but covers text and incoming enemies. Dead enemies, even bugs, become a human skeleton and then a gravestone, so our headcanon is that your character is crazy. Trees are built from corner sprites that rarely line up into circles. Say no to the vice king and you land in jail, then drop dead in a cutscene. You pick a knight, Valkyrie or barbarian at the start. It seems to be just the sprite, and we all picked Valkyrie.

### Combat

It is trivial. You get little for fighting beyond food or gold, you can run past nearly everything once you find shoes, and most bosses are beaten by solving them with an item. The giant shakes the screen, and he guards a ring of invisibility. You end it by killing the abbot for the medallion, then get a pile of text.

### Odds and ends

- The NES version has townspeople that take more hits to kill and fewer lethal monsters. The PC map is about 16 times bigger, with the same places and more woods.
- Bad Blood runs on the same code, a post-apocalyptic game with a CRT around it and a bottle instead of the candle.
- Ultima 6 borrows the icon interface. There is no sequel.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. We respect the scope and ambition, and it had to exist for other games to build on, but it is almost irrelevant today and better games do the same thing. It is really for NES archaeologists, who have already played it.
