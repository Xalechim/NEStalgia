# 225 - Casino Kid

SOFEL made and published this in 1989. A kid beats one player at a time at poker and blackjack to become king of the casino. Up close he is Gary Oak, and his personality is bobbing his head. It looks like an RPG and plays like poker with a nice wrapper, and we all wished the wrapper did more.

### What works

- **The poker.** It is five card draw, and every opponent has a name, a face and a personality. Faces change with the cards and the hands they win or lose, from shocked eyes to anime eyes. The first opponent, an old man, basically tells you he has a bad hand. Later tells get murkier, and some opponents have none. It isn't deep, but it felt more real than we expected.
- **The hub.** You walk the casino and talk to people for hints, and richer players won't sit down until you have the money. Moving up gives a real sense of growth, and it beats a plain menu.

### What doesn't

- **A glorified menu.** The look is Dragon Warrior and Final Fantasy, but only people can be talked to, and each is just a button for the next match. We wanted new games to unlock. You can see roulette tables you can't play.
- **Finding your next opponent.** The first blackjack player is tucked in the bottom right, past five or six other blackjack tables. Hints say where someone is, like Ted being northeast of a table, but never who you are looking for, so you talk to everyone.
- **Blackjack.** The dealer is just the casino, so the personalities don't matter. We argued over cutting it for more poker types like Jack's Wild or Texas Hold'em, or keeping it for variety.

### Worth knowing

- **The ending.** Beat the last opponent and you are warped to a separate mansion with evil music and lightning. The King wears a hood, with a spade that looks like an arrow pointing to a K. It is poker only, with bets from $10,000 to $100,000.
- **A kid in a casino.** It is odd that a game with bunny-costumed women was aimed at kids. We wanted the grand prize to be an NES.
- **The Famicom version.** Based on a manga, it has airfare to casinos like New York City, the real Golden Nugget in Las Vegas, and the slots and roulette cut from ours. We had just spent 30 minutes on the stripped-down version.

### The bottom line

Play it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean called it video poker with some talking heads, though he would like to try the Famicom version. Joe called it not essential but really cool.
