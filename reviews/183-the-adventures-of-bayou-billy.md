# 183 - The Adventures of Bayou Billy

Konami made this in 1989: a New Orleans adventure that is one-third beat-em-up, one-third light gun game and one-third driving game. We liked the ambition and the extras, but the beat-em-up stages, which are most of the game, dragged it down. Joe liked it the most.

### What works

- **Variety.** Each switch in play style came right when we might have gotten bored.
- **The light gun stages.** The controller worked well in place of a Zapper. Enemies hide in bushes and trees, so you shoot a head poking out like a shooting gallery. They felt easy, more like a rest, with less going for them than Operation Wolf.
- **Driving.** We called them Mach Rider-esque driving levels with a timer, not races. Losing all your lives sends you back to the start of the stage, which one host called a good balance while another said the length hurt the fun.
- **Practice mode.** Shorter stages let you work on the Zapper or the driving, and finishing them awards a power-up for the real game.
- **The extras.** There is a sound mode that lets you play any music track or sound effect, and the manual is colorfully written and funny.

### What doesn't

- **The beat-em-up stages.** It mirrors Double Dragon, but you can only jump by doing the jump kick. Enemies take forever to kill, one estimate being 16 hits, and inputs seem to lag a little. Sean played the first level five or six times and then cheated to get past it.
- **Cheese as strategy.** We ran in circles at the right distance and stood off from the crocodiles to hit them. A gun that kills in one hit and a whip help, but it takes several hits before a boss's health bar moves.
- **Losing everything.** Dying costs you your weapon and bulletproof vest, even in stages where they do nothing.
- **A forced hit.** In the light gun boss fight before Godfather Gordon, you cannot avoid damage, and arriving without enough health means you die.

### Worth knowing

The Famicom version is called Mad City, and the NES version is a harder take on it in every way. The Japanese game has four endings. One refuses to show you an ending if you used practice-mode power-ups.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List.

Joe enjoyed it a lot and considered voting Essential, but the beat-em-up stages stopped him. He still called it a "play it". Sean gave the beat-em-up a D-minus. Mike was let down: it is flawed enough that he could not recommend it to anybody, and more types of gameplay does not mean you should add them.
