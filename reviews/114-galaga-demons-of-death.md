# 114 - Galaga: Demons of Death

Namco made this and Bandai published it in 1988. It is the 1981 arcade game, already out on the Famicom in 1985, with a new subtitle and nothing else changed. We called it iconic and a good emulation, but there was not enough to it for the Essential Games List.

### What works

- **Capture and rescue.** Boss Galagas use tractor beams to take your ship, which costs a life. Free it and you fly two ships side by side. In some ways it is the very first shmup power-up, and you only get it by failing first.
- **The challenge stages.** You can't die, and the enemies loop through in a line, so wiping out a full row before it escapes is satisfying. They felt like a reprieve, and we wished they were a separate target-practice mode.
- **Little touches.** Medals on the side of the screen change as you advance, with a king's crown at stage 10. After game over you get your hit ratio. We saw numbers like 56, 45 and 36 percent, and it knocks you down a notch.

### What doesn't

- **The loop splits us.** As best we remember there are four enemy types, each with its own set pattern. Mike liked the basicness: learn how the bee-like ones swoop behind you, then replay early stages for a better score, like Balloon Fight. Not everyone agreed: a shmup with even the power-ups stripped away was pretty boring.
- **Hard to a fault.** Sean found the later levels difficult to a fault, maybe a holdover from its quarter-eating days.
- **Stingy shooting.** Only one bullet can be on screen at a time, so spamming is punished, and the turbo controller gets about two quick shots before a wait.
- **The box.** The Mayday transmission is on the front and back and ends with "it's only a video game." We argued over whether that reassures you or admits there is nothing special here.

### Worth knowing

- **Is it a shmup?** Mike's rubric says no: you can't navigate a level, there are no obstacles, no bosses at the end of stages and almost no power-ups. He kept saying no through Gaplus and Galaga 88. The counterargument was that it is a proto-shmup.
- **Already ported.** Sega Galaga on the SG-1000 in 1983 was the first home conversion, and we heard it has everything the NES version has.
- **Pause.** Everything freezes except the scrolling star field.

### The bottom line

Skip it. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List. Sean called it iconic but said it belongs on an arcade classics list: "it is not the soul of the NES." Joe said it does nothing particularly wrong, but there is not enough to it. We said it needed a Mode B, either a shmup spin or that challenge mode, and the jokes that followed included Grand Theft Auto San Andreas as a Mode B.
