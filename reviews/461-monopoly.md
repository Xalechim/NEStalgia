# 461 - Monopoly

Sculptured Software made it, Parker Brothers and Tomy published it in 1991, and we spent far longer on the board game than on the cartridge. Before you play a single turn, the Monopoly guy shows up without his hat, and when he gets you out of jail he does a jazzy slide that Joe described as a moonwalk. It is Monopoly with the chores removed, and it is the best board game adaptation we have played.

### What it fixes

- **The computer is the banker.** Nobody grabs a stray $500 bill, and nobody tracks deeds. The manual suggests a practice game with humans, then eight ruthless computer opponents. Real or computer, up to eight can play.
- **A time limit.** You can set a game from 15 minutes to four hours. It ends on net worth: assets plus cash, minus mortgages. We were impressed, though we thought turns or spaces would be harder to game than a clock.
- **A game editor.** Starting cash is editable down to the dollar, and so are properties, tokens and the number of buildings. Scenarios hand out property cards, two per player, so nobody opens with Park Place and Boardwalk.
- **Drop in, drop out.** Switch any player to a computer mid game. Ending early shows who is ahead on assets, which is how most real games end anyway.
- **Fast computers.** They blast through the menus, so a game stays short, even if you cannot always read what they did.

### The trading

We did not expect haggling on NES hardware. In one trade a computer offered a deal where we gave up New York Avenue for nothing, and when we flipped it around it named a price. Another time it asked for $700 and then for more property on top. Our experiences differed, though: one player offered the same thing twice and got the same price both times. It sometimes refuses outright, even Park Place when you own Boardwalk.

### What bugged us

- **Hands.** A disembodied left hand rolls the dice. Right-handers felt left out.
- **Who owns what.** You cycle through a menu of cards sorted by color to find out, though it is no worse than the real board.
- **The opponents.** You pick them from portraits, and the manual says Arthur is an orphanage-raised millionaire who drives hard bargains. We could not see any difference between personalities.
- **Free parking** still pays nothing, and there is no house rules section.

### Around the board

The Landlord's Game came out in 1904 as a lesson against monopolies. The Monopoly board is Atlantic City, with Marvin Gardens misspelled. The Super NES, Genesis and Game Boy got this same game, and the Super NES one has nicer graphics and nothing else.

### The bottom line

Play it, but it is not essential. Nobody voted it onto the Essential Games List, and not because it is bad: it is still just Monopoly, and the essential version of that is the board game. It is a great pick if you want Monopoly without a banker and have nothing released in the last 35 years.
